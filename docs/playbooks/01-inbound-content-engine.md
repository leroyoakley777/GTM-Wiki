# Inbound Content Engine

An inbound content engine is a repeatable system that turns customer problems into search-visible assets that attract, educate, and convert your ICP. It is not a blog. It is not a content calendar. It is a machine with inputs (customer intelligence), a production line (hub-and-spoke architecture), and an output loop (distribution + measurement + reinvestment).

This playbook covers building the engine from zero to growth-stage scale. For the channel strategy that feeds it, see [Channels: Inbound](../channels/02-inbound.md). For the agentic layer, see [Agentic: Agentic Outbound](../agentic/02-agentic-outbound.md) - the outbound pattern applies to inbound research and compose.

---

## One Number That Matters Most

**8-16 blog posts per month** is the optimal publishing cadence for growth-stage SaaS building topic authority. Companies publishing 16+ posts generate 4.5x more leads than those publishing 0-4 [1]. Below 8 posts, you are not compounding. Above 16, diminishing returns set in unless you have a team.

---

## When This Playbook Applies

Use this playbook when:

- You have a defined ICP and positioning (see [ICP Definition](../foundations/02-icp-definition.md) and [Positioning and Messaging](../foundations/03-positioning-and-messaging.md))
- You need a compounding acquisition channel that does not require perpetual ad spend
- You are willing to invest 6-12 months before the engine pays back (content compounds; paid does not)
- You can commit to a minimum of 6 posts per month consistently

Do not start here if you have not validated ICP and messaging. Content without positioning is noise.

## Architecture: The Hub-and-Spoke Model

The engine runs on one architecture: **one anchor asset (hub) per quarter, 8-12 spoke assets that link to it.**

### Hub (Anchor Asset)

- One definitive resource per quarter: "Ultimate Guide to X," "State of Y Report," "Complete Playbook for Z"
- Targets a high-volume head keyword (e.g., "b2b content marketing")
- 5,000-10,000 words, original research or proprietary data where possible
- Serves as the central node for internal linking
- Designed to earn backlinks and citations

### Spokes (Cluster Content)

- 8-12 articles per quarter that answer specific questions within the hub topic
- Target long-tail keywords with clear search intent
- 1,500-2,500 words each
- Each spoke links back to the hub; the hub links to every spoke
- Distribution: SEO, email, social, sales enablement

### Why This Works for Small Teams

A team of one cannot sustain a content treadmill. The hub model concentrates research effort: one deep dive fuels a quarter of output. The hub becomes the SEO powerhouse; the spokes capture qualified traffic at every funnel stage.

## Phase 1: Foundation (Weeks 1-2)

### Step 1: Extract Customer Intelligence

Do not guess at topics. Mine them.

**Sources, in priority order:**

1. **Sales demo recordings** - What questions repeat? What objections stall deals?
2. **Support tickets** - What confuses users? What workarounds exist?
3. **Onboarding calls** - What "aha moments" correlate with retention?
4. **G2/Capterra 3-star reviews of competitors** - Nuanced pain points, not rants or praise
5. **Reddit/Slack communities** - Search "How do I..." and "Struggling with..." in your niche subreddits
6. **Keyword research** - Validate demand, but only after you have the problems

**Output:** A ranked list of 20-30 customer problems with the exact language buyers use. This is your topic well. Every piece of content draws from it.

### Step 2: Define Your Content Pillars

Group problems into 3-4 pillars that map to your product's core use cases. Example for a lead routing tool:

| Pillar | Head Keyword | Hub Topic (Quarterly) |
|--------|--------------|----------------------|
| Lead Management | "lead routing" | Ultimate Guide to Lead Routing |
| Sales Operations | "sales process" | Sales Process Design Playbook |
| RevOps | "revenue operations" | State of RevOps Benchmark Report |

Each pillar gets one hub per year (quarterly rotation across pillars).

### Step 3: Build the First Hub Brief

Before writing, produce a brief that includes:

- Target head keyword + 10-15 long-tail spokes
- Outline with H2/H3 structure
- Data sources for original claims (or plan to generate them)
- Competitive gaps: what the top 3 results miss
- Internal linking map: which existing pages link to/from this hub
- Distribution checklist: email, social, sales enablement, partner share

## Phase 2: Production System (Weeks 3-4)

### Weekly Cadence

| Day | Activity | Owner (Team of One) |
|-----|----------|---------------------|
| Monday | Research + outline 2 spokes | You |
| Tuesday | Write spoke 1 (2,000 words) | You |
| Wednesday | Write spoke 2 | You |
| Thursday | Edit, optimize, publish both | You |
| Friday | Distribute (email, LinkedIn, Slack communities) + update hub | You |

**Two spokes per week = 8 posts/month.** This is the minimum viable cadence.

### Writing Standards

- **Lead with the problem, not the product.** Buyers are problem-aware before solution-aware.
- **One idea per section.** A section that makes two claims makes neither.
- **Proof beats claims.** A specific customer result, a data point, or a recognizable use case > superlatives.
- **Product mentions: TOFU 1-2x per 1,500 words as example; MOFU 3-5x as recommended solution; BOFU product-centric with educational framing.** Never pure product demo disguised as education.
- **Internal links: 3-5 per spoke back to hub + 2-3 to other relevant spokes/pages.**

### Quality Gate (Every Piece)

Before publish, verify:

- [ ] Target keyword in title, H1, first 100 words, one H2
- [ ] 3+ internal links (hub + related spokes/pages)
- [ ] 2+ external citations to authoritative sources
- [ ] Meta description under 160 chars with keyword
- [ ] Featured image + alt text
- [ ] Author byline with credentials
- [ ] `last_updated` frontmatter set to today

## Phase 3: Distribution Loop (Ongoing)

Content without distribution is a diary. The engine includes a mandatory distribution pass for every published piece.

### Primary Channels

| Channel | Action | Timing |
|---------|--------|--------|
| **SEO** | Submit to GSC; monitor indexing | Day 0 |
| **Email** | Add to next newsletter; nurture sequence if BOFU | Day 1 |
| **LinkedIn** | Founder/CEO post with native commentary + link | Day 1 |
| **Slack Communities** | Share in 3-5 relevant communities with context | Day 1-2 |
| **Sales Enablement** | Add to relevant battle cards / leave-behinds | Day 3 |
| **Repurposing** | Convert hub to 5-10 LinkedIn posts, 1 webinar, 1 PDF lead magnet | Week 2 |

### Repurposing Multiplier

One hub asset yields:

- 10-15 LinkedIn posts (one per section + data points)
- 1 webinar/slides deck
- 1 downloadable PDF (gated for lead capture)
- 5-8 email newsletter segments
- Sales one-pagers for top 3 spokes

This turns 1 quarter of production into 6+ weeks of distribution fuel.

## Phase 4: Measurement and Reinvestment

### Metrics That Matter

| Metric | Target (Growth Stage) | Cadence |
|--------|----------------------|---------|
| Organic traffic to hub | 2,000+/mo by month 6 | Monthly |
| Hub keyword rankings (top 10) | 5+ keywords | Monthly |
| Spoke conversion rate (visitor to lead) | 2-5% | Monthly |
| Leads attributed to content | 25-40% of inbound | Monthly |
| Backlinks to hub | 20+ referring domains by month 6 | Monthly |
| Content-assisted revenue | Track via CRM attribution | Quarterly |

### Reinvestment Rule

Every quarter, audit the hub performance:

- **Hub hit targets:** Double down. Build spokes for the next pillar. Promote hub harder.
- **Hub missed targets:** Diagnose. Was it keyword difficulty? Content quality? Distribution? Fix one variable, relaunch.
- **Spoke outliers:** Identify the 2-3 spokes driving 80% of conversions. Build more like them. Kill the bottom 20%.

## Scaling the Engine: Team of One to Growth Stage

| Stage | ARR | Posts/Month | Team | Budget (% ARR) | Outsource % |
|-------|-----|-------------|------|----------------|-------------|
| Early | $1-5M | 4-6 | 1 (founder/marketer) | 15-25% | 0-20% |
| Growth | $5-50M | 8-16 | 3-7 | 10-20% | 60-70% |
| Scale | $50M+ | 16-30+ | 10+ | 5-10% | 50-60% |

**Growth-stage benchmarks (Jam 7, 2026) [2]:**

- 8-16 posts/month optimal for topic authority
- Marketing budget 10-20% of ARR
- Team of 3-7; median $5M ARR companies have 3 in marketing
- 71% of SaaS over $5M ARR outsource at least one content function
- Content-market fit matters more than volume

### Hiring Sequence

1. **Content Operations Manager** - Runs the calendar, manages freelancers, owns quality gate
2. **SEO Specialist** - Keyword research, technical SEO, hub strategy
3. **Senior Writer/Editor** - Owns hub production, voice consistency
4. **Distribution Manager** - Email, social, repurposing, sales enablement

Do not hire writers first. Hire the operator who builds the system.

## Common Mistakes

**Mistake 1: The Content Treadmill.** Publishing 3 shallow posts/week because "consistency." Result: 90% creation, 10% distribution, zero authority. Fix: Hub model. One anchor, deep distribution.

**Mistake 2: Skipping Customer Intelligence.** Writing from keyword tools instead of demo recordings. Result: Content ranks but converts poorly. Fix: Mine sales/support/onboarding before every hub.

**Mistake 3: No Internal Linking.** Spokes published as orphans. Result: No topic authority, hub doesn't rank. Fix: Mandatory 3-5 internal links per spoke. Audit quarterly.

**Mistake 4: Gating Everything.** Putting the best content behind forms. Result: SEO value lost, trust not built. Fix: Gate only the PDF/downloadable version. Keep the HTML hub open.

**Mistake 5: Measuring Vanity.** Tracking pageviews instead of content-assisted pipeline. Result: Cannot justify budget. Fix: CRM attribution (first touch, last touch, multi-touch) on every lead.

**Mistake 6: Product-Led Content Confusion.** Writing pure product demos as "educational content." Result: Buyers bounce. Fix: Follow the TOFU/MOFU/BOFU product mention ratios above.

**Mistake 7: Inconsistent Cadence.** 12 posts one month, 2 the next. Result: Google crawls irregularly; team burns out. Fix: Two spokes/week, every week. No exceptions.

## Worked Example: Lead Routing SaaS (Quarter 1)

**Pillar:** Lead Management
**Hub:** "Ultimate Guide to Lead Routing" (target: "lead routing software," 2,400 MSV)
**Spokes (12):**

1. "Round Robin vs. Territory: Which Lead Routing Model Fits Your Team?"
2. "How to Route Leads by Company Size in Salesforce"
3. "Lead Response Time Benchmarks: What the Data Says"
4. "5 Lead Routing Mistakes That Cost You Deals"
5. "How to Build a Lead Scoring Model That Actually Works"
6. "Lead Routing for ABM: Account-Based Assignment Logic"
7. "Integrating Lead Routing with Your Marketing Automation"
8. "From Manual to Automated: A Migration Checklist"
9. "Lead Routing KPIs Every RevOps Leader Should Track"
10. "Case Study: How [Customer] Cut Response Time 80%"
11. "Lead Routing for Hybrid Teams: Remote + In-Office"
12. "The ROI of Lead Routing: A Calculator Template"

**Production:** 2 spokes/week x 6 weeks = 12 spokes. Hub written in weeks 1-2, published week 3. Spokes publish weeks 3-8, each linking to hub.

**Distribution:** Hub submitted to GSC, emailed to list, posted by CEO on LinkedIn, shared in RevGenius + Pavilion + Exit Five, added to sales leave-behinds. Each spoke gets same treatment on publish day.

**Measurement:** Month 3 review - hub at 1,200 organic visits/mo, 3 keywords top 10, 45 backlinks. Spoke #3 and #10 drive 60% of conversions. Next quarter: double down on benchmarks + case studies.

## Tools Stack (Minimal Viable)

| Function | Tool | Why |
|----------|------|-----|
| Keyword Research | Ahrefs / Semrush | Volume, difficulty, SERP analysis |
| Content Planning | Notion / Airtable | Hub/spoke mapping, calendar, briefs |
| Writing | Google Docs / Notion | Collaboration, version history |
| CMS | Webflow / Ghost / HubSpot | Publishing, SEO controls |
| Analytics | GA4 + GSC + HubSpot/CRM | Traffic, rankings, attribution |
| Distribution | Buffer / Typefully / HubSpot | Social scheduling, email |
| Repurposing | Descript / Canva | Video clips, graphics |

Start with Ahrefs + Notion + Google Docs + Webflow + GA4/GSC. Add the rest as the engine proves out.
