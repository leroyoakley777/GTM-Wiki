# Coaching and One-on-Ones

Coaching is the weekly act of changing one rep behavior with evidence from live work. A one-on-one is where that act happens. Inspection reads the deals. Coaching reads the rep.

Most one-on-ones become a status report. The rep narrates the week, the manager nods, and both leave with the habits they brought in. The meeting moved information. It did not move skill.

This page is the operator view. It pairs with [Inspection and deal review](./inspection-and-deal-review) for the deal loop, [Culture](/docs/culture) for the habits behind it, and [Enablement](/docs/enablement) for the artifacts coaching reinforces.

## What coaching and one-on-ones govern

Coaching decides four things a dashboard recap cannot:

- **Which skill moves this week.** One skill per rep, picked from live work, not a list of five. The most common sales management mistake is coaching too many skills at once <sup><a href="#sources">[262]</a></sup>.
- **Whether the rep improves or plateaus.** Transformative leaders develop reps, the reps start winning, and the winning builds the pride that keeps a team together <sup><a href="#sources">[336]</a></sup>.
- **Who is worth the time.** Hire for what you cannot teach, then coach the rest. Coachability and curiosity predict success better than polish <sup><a href="#sources">[262]</a></sup>.
- **Whether the roster holds.** A rep lost over a comp dispute costs the full ramp investment twice, once on the exit and once on the backfill <sup><a href="#sources">[337]</a></sup>.

The stakes sit in the numbers. Quota attainment across B2B sat at 27-30% in 2024 <sup><a href="#sources">[15]</a></sup>. Roughly 43-47% of sellers hit quota in the RepVue index <sup><a href="#sources">[92]</a></sup>. The top 14% of sellers generate 80% of revenue <sup><a href="#sources">[339]</a></sup>. Coaching is how a manager lifts the middle without flattening the top.

## Weekly coaching cadence

Coaching is a calendar item, not a mood. Same slot, every week.

| Cadence | What happens | Evidence to bring | Owner |
|---------|--------------|-------------------|-------|
| Weekly | A 30-minute one-on-one per rep | One call recording or one email thread | Front-line manager |
| Weekly | One skill picked and practiced | The rep's own words for the fix | Front-line manager |
| Weekly | A tape review on live work | The recording, not a summary | Front-line manager |
| Monthly | Ramp check against the 90-day plan | Held meetings and accepted opportunities | Manager and rep |
| Quarterly | Career and comp conversation | Written expectations, not vibes | Manager and rep |

One rule holds the meeting together: no skill change without a recording or a thread on the screen. A manager who coaches from memory coaches from taste.

## One skill at a time

A rep can hold one change. Give them five and they keep none.

Pick the skill from the evidence. A missed discovery question on ten calls is a discovery habit, not ten mistakes. A weak first line on twenty emails is a research habit, not twenty bad drafts. Name the skill in the rep's language, show the tape, and rehearse the replacement once before the meeting ends.

Ramp is the other half of the same loop. A ramped outbound rep generates 10-20 quality leads a month <sup><a href="#sources">[251]</a></sup>, and a structured ramp reaches that band faster than a rep left to figure it out alone <sup><a href="#sources">[262]</a></sup>. Coaching compresses the ramp when the manager names the skill early.

## How coaching changes by stage

- **Seed.** The founder coaches the first hires on calls the founder still runs. The standard is one founder habit, said out loud, not a competency model.
- **First sales team.** One manager coaches every rep weekly and reads ten work samples. The skill list lives in a written checklist <sup><a href="#sources">[262]</a></sup>.
- **Growth.** Coaching splits from inspection. The manager owns the skill, enablement owns the artifacts, and RevOps owns the stage definitions <sup><a href="#sources">[299]</a></sup>.
- **Scale.** Sample the work instead of reading every call. A front-line manager coaches a team, a second-line manager coaches the coaches, and the standard travels through the managers.

## Failure modes and fixes

- **Status-report one-on-ones.** The rep narrates the week. Fix: open one recording and coach the skill you find.
- **Five skills in one sitting.** The rep remembers none. Fix: pick one skill and practice it before the meeting ends <sup><a href="#sources">[262]</a></sup>.
- **Coaching from memory.** The manager judges from taste. Fix: bring the tape or the thread every time.
- **Coaching the top rep only.** The middle stays flat. Fix: sample every rep, not the two you like to talk to.
- **Coaching without a standard.** Every manager coaches a different motion. Fix: write the standard once and coach against it.
- **A one-on-one that skips the person.** The rep hears about pipeline and never about the career. Fix: give the last ten minutes to growth and comp.
- **Comp as an afterthought.** A rep who loses on pay leaves, and the ramp is paid again <sup><a href="#sources">[337]</a></sup>. Fix: settle the plan before the quarter, not during the exit.

## Worked example: a one-on-one that changes a habit

A 9-rep team sits at 41% of quota with two weeks left in the quarter <sup><a href="#sources">[92]</a></sup>. One rep, Dana, has booked 11 meetings and held 4. The manager opens the last three discovery calls.

The pattern shows in the first ten minutes of each call: Dana presents before asking. The manager names one skill, "ask the pain question before the pitch," and plays the exact moment where the buyer was ready to talk. They rehearse the opening once. Next week, the manager checks the same moment in two new calls.

One skill, one tape, one rehearsal, one check. That is the whole loop. A manager who repeats it weekly changes a habit in a month. A manager who runs a status meeting changes nothing.

## Coaching checklist

```text
WEEKLY COACHING CHECKLIST
[ ] One recording or thread on screen for every rep
[ ] One skill named, in the rep's own words
[ ] The skill rehearsed once inside the meeting
[ ] The fix tied to a buyer moment, not a manager preference
[ ] Last ten minutes on growth and comp
[ ] Next week's check booked against the same skill
[ ] Ramp plan reviewed for every rep inside 90 days
```

## Run it this week

One. Pick one rep. Open one call. Find the single moment where the outcome turned, good or bad.

Two. Name the skill behind that moment and say it back to the rep. Ask them to state the replacement in their own words.

Three. Rehearse it once. Book the same check for next week.

## Agentic layer

An agent can pull call recordings, transcribe them, and flag the moment a skill failed against the written standard. It can draft a one-line coaching note per call and track whether the same skill appears again next week. A human still runs the one-on-one and owns the change. Set the guardrail first: the agent reads and flags, and never sends feedback to the rep. See [Agentic outbound](/docs/agentic/agentic-outbound) for the desk-level SOP.
