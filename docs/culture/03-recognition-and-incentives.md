# Recognition and Incentives

A revenue team repeats the behavior it rewards. Recognition is the public, daily signal about what good looks like. Incentives are the written, quarterly version of the same signal. When the two disagree, the desk follows the money.

Most teams file recognition under morale and comp under HR. Both are culture instruments. Compensation is the operating system for go-to-market because it translates company strategy into rep behavior <sup><a href="#sources">[337]</a></sup>. Recognition is the weekly signal that tells the desk which behavior the plan meant.

This page is the operator view. It pairs with [Coaching and one-on-ones](./coaching-and-one-on-ones) for the behavior you reinforce, [Inspection and deal review](./inspection-and-deal-review) for the evidence behind it, and [Culture](/docs/culture) for the habits it holds.

## What recognition and incentives govern

Recognition and incentives decide four things a thank-you note cannot:

- **Which behavior repeats.** A desk paid on meetings booked books meetings, held or not. A desk held to new customer meetings and paid on accepted opportunities books better ones <sup><a href="#sources">[279]</a></sup>.
- **Whether the plan survives contact with the desk.** About 83% of SDRs miss quota in a given month <sup><a href="#sources">[242]</a></sup>. A plan that only pays the top decile reads as a lottery to everyone below it.
- **Who stays.** Standard SDR pay runs 60-70% base and 30-40% commission, and commission-heavy plans drive burnout and poor lead quality <sup><a href="#sources">[244]</a></sup>.
- **What the desk believes about the work.** Transformative leaders develop reps, the reps start winning, and the winning builds the pride that keeps a team together <sup><a href="#sources">[336]</a></sup>.

The stakes sit in the distribution. The top 14% of sellers generate 80% of revenue <sup><a href="#sources">[339]</a></sup>, and quota attainment across B2B sat at 27-30% in 2024 <sup><a href="#sources">[15]</a></sup>. A plan that widens the gap between top and middle spends the culture to buy a number.

## Cash and praise are different instruments

Cash buys behavior. Praise names it. Teams break when they use one for the other.

| Instrument | What it changes | What it cannot do | Use it when |
|------------|-----------------|-------------------|-------------|
| Commission and bonus | What a rep chooses to do next quarter | Fix a wrong quota or a bad territory | The behavior is measurable and the plan is fair |
| Spot award | What a rep does again this week | Substitute for base pay | One person did one specific thing well |
| Public recognition | What the desk believes the standard is | Replace coaching | You can name the exact behavior and its buyer moment |
| Promotion | What the team sees as a career path | Buy loyalty in a broken plan | The scope of the job changed |

A manager who awards send volume trains the desk to spray. A manager who reads the exact email that earned a reply sets the standard. The first changes the mood. The second changes the behavior.

## Design the plan around one behavior

Start from the behavior, not the budget. Pick the single behavior that moves the number this quarter, then pay for it and name it in public.

- If the motion needs more qualified conversations, hold reps to new customer meetings and tie accelerators to a net-new-logo gate <sup><a href="#sources">[279]</a></sup>.
- If the motion needs pipeline quality, pay on accepted opportunities, not on meetings booked.
- If the motion needs retention, settle the plan before the quarter, because a rep lost over comp costs the full ramp investment twice <sup><a href="#sources">[337]</a></sup>.

Set quotas slightly low rather than high. An aggressive quota drives out the A-players who could hit it and keeps the reps who cannot <sup><a href="#sources">[279]</a></sup>. A plan that pays the top decile and starves everyone else spends the middle to buy a leaderboard.

## How recognition and incentives change by stage

- **Seed.** The founder pays on outcomes the founder can see. There is no plan document, and that is fine, as long as the founder names the behavior out loud every week.
- **First sales team.** The first written plan sets the culture. Keep base at 60-70% of pay and commission at 30-40% so the desk is not paid to spray <sup><a href="#sources">[244]</a></sup>.
- **Growth.** Split the plan by seat. SDRs earn on qualified meetings, AEs earn on closed revenue, and the handoff between them gets its own written definition <sup><a href="#sources">[299]</a></sup>.
- **Scale.** Specialize the plan and the recognition. RevOps owns the plan mechanics, enablement owns the artifacts, and the manager owns the weekly habit of naming the behavior <sup><a href="#sources">[299]</a></sup>.

## Failure modes and fixes

- **Activity as reward.** A desk recognized for 150 emails a week at a 2.8% reply rate is a busy desk, not a good one <sup><a href="#sources">[341]</a></sup>. Fix: recognize the reply, not the send.
- **A lottery plan.** Only the top decile can win, so the middle disengages. Fix: pay a fair base and set quotas slightly low <sup><a href="#sources">[279]</a></sup>.
- **Recognition without a standard.** The award goes to whoever the manager likes. Fix: name the exact behavior and the buyer moment it served.
- **Comp as an afterthought.** A rep who loses on pay leaves, and the ramp is paid again <sup><a href="#sources">[337]</a></sup>. Fix: settle the plan before the quarter, not during the exit.
- **Praise as a substitute for coaching.** A shout-out does not change a habit. Fix: pair the recognition with one skill and a tape <sup><a href="#sources">[262]</a></sup>.
- **The plan nobody understands.** Reps guess what pays. Fix: write the plan in one page a rep can repeat from memory.

## Worked example: a plan that pays for the right meeting

A 9-rep team sits at 44% of quota, inside the RepVue band <sup><a href="#sources">[92]</a></sup>. The desk books 140 meetings a quarter and holds 60 of them. The plan pays on meetings booked, so the desk optimizes for the calendar and the AEs lose a week a month to no-shows.

The manager changes one line: pay on meetings held and accepted by an AE, then name the best held meeting every Friday. The desk books fewer meetings and holds more of them. One line, one Friday ritual, one behavior.

## Recognition and incentive checklist

```text
QUARTERLY PLAN AND WEEKLY RECOGNITION CHECKLIST
[ ] One behavior named as the plan's target
[ ] Base and commission split written and shared
[ ] Quota set slightly low, not aggressive
[ ] Accelerator tied to a net-new-logo gate
[ ] Every rep can repeat what pays from memory
[ ] One specific behavior recognized in public each week
[ ] Recognition names the buyer moment, not the person's charm
[ ] Plan reviewed before the quarter, never during an exit
```

## Run it this week

One. Write the one behavior the plan pays for. If you cannot write it in a sentence, the desk cannot repeat it.

Two. Pick the best example of that behavior from this week and name it in public, with the buyer moment.

Three. Check the plan against the behavior. If the two disagree, the plan wins, so fix the plan.

## Agentic layer

An agent can track which recognized behavior repeats and which one dies after a week. It can pull the plan, the quota, and the attainment by rep, then flag a plan where the top decile wins every period and the middle never does. A human still sets the plan and owns the room. Set the guardrail first: the agent reports the pattern and never changes a number a rep is paid on. See [Agentic outbound](/docs/agentic/agentic-outbound) for the desk-level SOP.
