# GTM Funnel

The funnel is the map of every path a buyer takes from first touch to paying
customer to loyal referrer. It is not a marketing diagram. It is the operating
model for the whole revenue machine. If you cannot name the funnel, you cannot
measure it. If you cannot measure it, you cannot improve it.

This page defines the funnel, explains why it is the backbone of a growth
engine, gives you the framework and the benchmarks, and walks you through how
to run it at high tempo without the common failures. Read it with [GTM Metrics](../foundations/gtm-metrics) and [Revenue Operations](../foundations/revenue-operations).

## Definition

A funnel is a sequence of stages. Each stage filters a set of people down to a
smaller set that is qualified to move to the next stage. The funnel shows you
where value is created and where it leaks.

The canonical growth funnel spans five stages: acquisition, activation,
retention, revenue, and referral. [1] The order matters less than the logic:
you bring people in, get them to the core value, keep them, monetize them, and
turn them into the channel that brings the next people in.

Two terms sit at the heart of any funnel discussion:

- **Acquisition.** How people find you. This is the top of the funnel. Channels
  include outbound, inbound, paid, partnerships, referrals, and product-led
  motion.
- **Activation.** How people reach your product's core value for the first
  time. This is the aha moment. [1]

The funnel is not a sales artifact. It is the shared language that lets
product, engineering, data, and marketing work from one set of numbers. A
single funnel means a single source of truth.

## Why it matters

A funnel turns vague ambition into a set of measurable bottlenecks. Most
companies have a funnel problem they cannot see. The funnel makes the problem
visible, which is the precondition for fixing it.

The funnel is the backbone of a growth engine for four reasons:

1. **It focuses effort on the highest-impact point.** Growth teams are organized
   around funnel areas, not departments. [1] Each team owns one stage:
   acquisition, activation, or retention. A team with a single stage can go
   deep instead of spreading thin.
2. **It makes compounding visible.** Small wins at a bottleneck compound. A
   five-percent conversion improvement every month nets roughly an eighty
   percent improvement over a year. [1] That is the difference between a flat
   business and a compounding one.
3. **It exposes the aha moment.** Once you identify what drives activation,
   you aim roughly thirty percent of time and effort at getting more users to
   reach that moment faster. [1] That is not a nice-to-have. It is a deliberate
   allocation of the team.
4. **It forces the revenue machine to work as one.** The funnel spans
   acquisition through revenue and referral. It cannot be run by a lone
   department, which is why the discipline that holds it together matters.
   [2]

## Framework

### Five stages

Use the AARRR model as your default funnel.

| Stage | Question it answers | Primary signal |
|-------|--------------------|----------------|
| Acquisition | How do people find us? | Qualified traffic, leads |
| Activation | When do they hit the aha moment? | First value event |
| Retention | Do they come back? | Day-7 return, 3-month return |
| Revenue | Do they pay, expand, and stay? | ARR, NRR, expansion |
| Referral | Do they bring others? | Referral rate, viral factor |

### Growth hacking cycle

The funnel is not a static diagram. It is run as a cycle. The growth hacking
cycle is: analyze data, gather insight, generate ideas, prioritize experiments,
run tests, review results, decide. [1]

Two behaviors make the cycle effective:

- **Double down on winners.** When an experiment moves a lever, run more
  experiments in that direction instead of drifting to the next idea.
- **Take moonshots.** Occasionally test ideas that push past the local maximum.
  Most fail. The few that win change the curve. [1]

### Virality equation

Referral is the fifth stage because it is measurable. Virality is the product
of three variables:

Viral factor = Payload by Conversion rate by Frequency. [1]

- **Payload.** How many people each user brings to the door.
- **Conversion rate.** How many of those people activate.
- **Frequency.** How often each user shares.

A loop that compounds at any value above one grows on its own. A loop below one
still helps, but it is a channel, not an engine.

### Growth team

People operate a funnel. The team that runs it must be cross-functional:
product, engineering, data, and marketing together. [1] The team reports to
the executive sponsor and owns one funnel area. [1]

A lone-ranger growth hacker cannot do this alone. [1] Sean Ellis coined the
term growth hacker in 2010 while VP of growth at Dropbox. Growth hacking is a
systematic method: a cross-functional team running data-driven experiments. It
is not a single silver-bullet hack. [1]

### Revenue operations as the holding system

The funnel is a model. The machine that runs it is revenue operations.
Revenue Operations is two systems in one: the management system, the EQ, that
aligns the people in the revenue teams, and the operating system, the IQ, that
combines technology, processes, and data assets to generate more sustainable
and scalable growth. [2]

The management system rests on six pillars: commercial leadership, commercial
architecture, commercial insights, commercial asset management, commercial
enablement, and commercial operations. [2] These are the levers that keep the
funnel honest across the whole revenue team.

Only the CEO or chief operating officer can truly unify sales, marketing, and
service into one revenue team, because that is the only manager to whom all of
these functions report. [2] If you are not a CEO or COO, your job is to hand
this problem up.

## Funnel variants

The five-stage model is the default. Real funnels differ by segment and by
stage of the company, and you should run the variant that matches where you
sell and how far along you are. [1]

- **By segment.** Self-serve and SMB funnels are short: acquisition flows
  straight into activation and revenue with little human touch. Enterprise
  funnels are long: acquisition produces qualified opportunities, activation
  happens during a sales cycle, and revenue depends on procurement and legal.
  The five stages stay, but the emphasis shifts down the funnel as deal size
  climbs.
- **By maturity.** A seed-stage company spends its effort on activation and
  retention, because it has not proven product-market fit yet. A series-stage
  company spends on acquisition and referral, because it has a middle that
  holds and needs volume. An enterprise company spends on revenue and
  retention, because it already has demand and needs expansion.
- **By motion.** Product-led funnels lean on the aha moment and freemium
  conversion. Sales-led funnels lean on qualification and the close. Partnership
  funnels insert a referral stage earlier, because partners bring pre-qualified
  demand.

The point of the variant is not to abandon the model. It is to tell you where
the leaks will be before you look. [1]

## How to execute

### 1. Prove product-market fit before scaling

Do not scale the top of the funnel until the middle holds. Test product-market
fit by surveying users with one question: "How would you feel if you could no
longer use this product?" [1]

- **40% or more answer very disappointed.** The product has must-have status.
  Scale marketing.
- **25 to 40% answer very disappointed.** Tweak the product or the messaging.
- **Below 25%.** Not ready for high-tempo growth. Fix the product first. [1]

### 2. Find the aha moment

Identify the event where a user first experiences core value. Measure which
users who hit that event retain better than those who do not. Then spend a
large share of effort on moving users to that moment faster. [1] This is the
single biggest lever in activation.

### 3. Instrument the funnel with real conversion data

You cannot run the cycle without numbers. Benchmarks give you a starting point,
not a target. Use them to check whether a stage is obviously broken.

- **Visitor to lead.** 2.6% average, 4.5% top quartile. [3] SMB runs 1.4%. [4]
- **Lead to MQL.** 31% average, 39% for B2B SaaS. [5]
- **MQL to SQL.** 13% across 5,500 sales professionals in 27 countries. [6]
- **SQL to opportunity.** 47% average. [7]
- **Opportunity to closed-won.** 21% average, down 4 to 6 points from 2021. [7]
- **Activation.** Average B2B SaaS activation is 37.5%, median 37%. [8]

A stage that runs far below its benchmark is a candidate for a focused growth
team. A stage that runs above its benchmark is a place to double down.

### 4. Set a high-tempo experiment cadence

Leading growth teams run 20 to 30 experiments a week. [1] Most experiments fail
to produce the hoped-for result. That is expected. Volume matters because
finding wins is a numbers game. [1]

Start slow. Set a minimum tests-per-week cadence you can actually keep, then
speed up as the team learns. A cadence you cannot sustain is worse than no
cadence.

### 5. Review and decide

Every week, review results against the funnel. Decide what to double down on,
what to kill, and what to run next. Write the decision down. The decision is
the output of the cycle. [1]

## Common mistakes

1. **Scaling the top before the middle holds.** You buy more traffic, but the
   funnel leaks at activation. You pay for empty growth. Test product-market
   fit first. [1]
2. **Chasing the silver-bullet hack.** There is no one growth hack. The win
   comes from the systematic cycle run by a cross-functional team. [1]
3. **Running too few experiments.** Wins are rare. If you run two experiments a
   week when the leading teams run twenty, you will not find the winners. [1]
4. **Owning a department instead of a funnel stage.** Growth fails when it is a
   side project. It succeeds when a dedicated team owns one stage and reports
   to an executive. [1]
5. **Ignoring retention.** Most teams obsess over acquisition and forget that
   the middle and bottom of the funnel decide whether revenue compounds. The
   funnel is only as strong as its weakest stage.
6. **Measuring without deciding.** Data is not the output of the cycle. The
   decision is. If you review numbers and change nothing, you are not running a
   growth cycle. [1]
7. **Running the funnel as a single team.** A lone-ranger growth hacker cannot
   do the work. The team must be cross-functional and report up. [1]
8. **Forgetting that the funnel is run by a system.** The funnel leaks when the
   people, technology, and data behind it are not aligned. That alignment is
   the job of revenue operations. [2]
