# GTM Channels Bullseye

This page explains the Bullseye framework for choosing a
go-to-market channel. It sits at the start of the GTM foundations
section, before the pages that cover individual channels in depth.
It answers one question: which channel deserves your limited time
and money.

## Definition

Bullseye is a testing framework for picking a customer acquisition
channel. You brainstorm ideas across all 19 traction channels, rank
them into three rings, test the most promising few, then double
down on the single channel that wins [1][5]. The 19 channels are
viral, PR, unconventional PR, SEM, social and display ads, offline
ads, SEO, content marketing, email, engineering as marketing,
targeting blogs, business development, sales, affiliates, existing
platforms, trade shows, offline events, speaking, and
community [4].

## Why it matters

Most startups fail because they cannot gain enough customers before
they run out of money, not because they cannot build a product.
Almost every failed startup has a product. What failed startups
lack is enough customers [1]. Getting distribution wrong is fatal.
The fix treats traction as a first-class job rather than an
afterthought [2].

Spend half of early time on product development and half on
traction development, starting now. This is the 50/50 rule. It
builds a customer base and gathers market feedback in parallel with
the product, instead of ramping marketing only after the product
ships [2].

Traction is the measurable evidence of customer demand: downloads,
subscribers, and sales transactions. It is the single best way to
improve a startup's odds of success, and it attracts investors,
talent, partners, and publicity [3]. Without it, nothing else
moves.

## Framework

The Bullseye framework runs four steps [5]:

1. Brainstorm at least one idea for every one of the 19 channels.
2. Rank the ideas into three rings: inner circle (most promising),
   potential, and long shots.
3. Pick three from the inner ring and run cheap tests on each.
4. Focus and double down on the single channel that wins.

The framework exists because you cannot predict which channel will
work. Only testing tells you [4].

Two supporting ideas sharpen the choice.

First, most startups find roughly 70 percent of their traction
from a single channel. Doubling down on that core channel beats
continuing to explore other channels [6].

Second, you cannot use the framework in a vacuum. Define what
traction means for your business, set a quantifiable traction goal,
list the milestones essential to reach it, and focus limited
resources strictly on that path. This is the Critical Path
framework [7].

## How to execute

Run the process in a tight loop, not as a one-off exercise.

- List all 19 channels in a table. Do not skip any, even the ones
  that seem irrelevant to your industry [4][5].
- Generate at least one concrete idea per channel. A vague idea is
  not testable [5].
- Rank every idea into the three rings based on cost, reach, and
  fit. Stay honest; optimism belongs in the inner ring only when
  evidence supports it [5].
- Test three inner-ring channels cheaply. Cheap means low budget
  and fast, measured against your Critical Path goal [5][7].
- Read the results. When a test does not hit its number, drop it
  and test the next candidate.
- When one channel wins, put most of your resources there. Do not
  keep exploring [6].

Below is a one-page cheat sheet you can paste into a working doc.

```
BULLSEYE CHANNEL SCORECARD
Channel                Idea                    Ring          Test budget   Verdict
---------------------- ----------------------- ------------- ------------- --------
1.  Viral              Referral loop           Inner        $200 / 2 wks  TEST
2.  PR                 Launch story            Potential    $100 / 1 wk   HOLD
3.  Unconventional PR  Stunt at conf           Long shot    n/a           SKIP
4.  SEM                Head-term ads           Inner        $500 / 2 wks  TEST
5.  Social/display ads Retargeting set         Potential    n/a           HOLD
6.  Offline ads        Billboard near venue    Long shot    n/a           SKIP
7.  SEO                Cornerstone posts       Inner        $0  / 4 wks   TEST
8.  Content marketing  Newsletter series       Potential    n/a           HOLD
9.  Email              Cold outreach           Inner        $0  / 2 wks   TEST
... (complete all 19)
Next action: pick the three TEST rows with the best fit, run them,
then double down on the single winner and drive 70 percent of traction there.
```

## Bullseye in the AI era

The framework still works. The channel list needs updating. Two
shifts change which rings deserve your tests.

First, AI search takes a share from classic inbound. Organic web
traffic falls 15 to 25 percent as zero-click search answers queries
directly [10]. Separate trackers find 73 percent of B2B websites
lost traffic, with an average 34 percent year-over-year decline
[11]. SEO and content marketing still belong in the brainstorm, but
rank them on captured demand rather than published volume: test
whether prospects arrive, not whether pages rank.

Second, new channels join the 19. AI answer engines, agent
marketplaces, and embedded distribution inside AI assistants behave
as channels with their own cost, reach, and fit. Brainstorm at
least one idea for each, rank them in the same three rings, and
test them with the same cheap-test discipline. The framework does
not care whether a channel existed in 2014. It cares which one
produces customers per dollar this quarter.

What does not change: one channel still drives most traction, the
Critical Path still sets the test targets, and testing still beats
predicting.

## Common mistakes

- Testing everything at once. A startup cannot fund 19 channels in
  parallel, and parallel tests produce noisy data [4][5].
- Relying on only familiar channels. Founders consider the channels
  they already know, which blinds them to the full range of
  options. This is channel bias [9].
- Ignoring underused channels. The most underutilized channels in
  an industry are often the most promising, because you gain a
  competitive advantage by acquiring customers in ways your
  competitors do not [8].
- Skipping the traction goal. Running tests without a Critical Path
  target leaves no way to judge which channel actually won [7].
- Stopping after the first test. Testing three inner-ring channels
  and moving on is not the end; the winner needs sustained focus to
  reach 70 percent of traction [5][6].
- Ranking AI-era channels by old metrics. Impressions and rankings
  flatter inbound while captured demand falls. Score every channel
  on customers per dollar [10][11].
