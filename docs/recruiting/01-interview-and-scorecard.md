# Interview loop and scorecard

An unstructured interview measures how much the panel likes a candidate. A structured loop measures how well the candidate runs the job. The gap between the two shows in the numbers.

A structured interview predicts job performance at a validity of about .51. An unstructured interview predicts at about .38. Pair a structured interview with a cognitive-ability test and validity reaches .63 <sup><a href="#sources">[372]</a></sup>. Hiring for the traits you cannot teach still matters, and you collect that evidence the same way <sup><a href="#sources">[262]</a></sup>.

This page covers the loop, the scorecard, and the debrief. Read [Recruiting](/docs/recruiting) for the hire decision, [SDRs](/docs/roles/sdrs) and [AEs](/docs/roles/aes) for the seats, and [Ramp and certification](/docs/enablement/ramp-and-certification) for the first 90 days.

## What a structured loop produces

A structured loop asks every candidate the same questions, scores the answers on one scale, and records the evidence behind each score <sup><a href="#sources">[373]</a></sup>.

| Element | What it decides | Artifact |
|---------|-----------------|----------|
| Competencies | What the role needs in year one | A scorecard, fixed before sourcing |
| Question set | How each competency gets tested | The same questions in the same order |
| Rubric | What each score means | An anchored scale from poor to outstanding |
| Evidence notes | What the candidate said | Written notes behind every rating |
| Debrief | The hire decision | Independent scores first, then one call |

Google runs structured interviews across the company and names four parts: vetted questions, recorded feedback, standardized rubrics, and trained interviewers <sup><a href="#sources">[373]</a></sup>. A rubric beats a gut call because every reviewer scores against the same words.

## Build the scorecard before you source

Write the scorecard before the first candidate. A scorecard names four to six competencies for the role, and it pulls them from the work, not from a template <sup><a href="#sources">[373]</a></sup>. Stage 2 Capital sets the same limit for the first GTM hire: define success for the next 9 to 12 months and keep the must-haves to five or fewer <sup><a href="#sources">[240]</a></sup>.

Collect the competencies from the hiring manager, the top performers in the seat, and the last two people who left it. A competency that no one can describe as a behavior does not belong on the card.

The five traits that correlate with B2B sales success are coachability, curiosity, prior success, intelligence, and work ethic <sup><a href="#sources">[262]</a></sup>. Turn each one into a question with a scored answer, or leave it off the card.

## Run the loop

1. Ask every candidate the same questions in the same order. Do not add a question mid-loop.
2. Take notes on what the candidate said, not your read of it. A quote survives the debrief; a summary does not.
3. Score each answer right after it lands, while the detail is fresh <sup><a href="#sources">[373]</a></sup>.
4. Give each competency one behavioral question about a past situation and one situational question about a future one.
5. Include a work sample. A work sample test predicts job performance at a validity of about .54 <sup><a href="#sources">[372]</a></sup>. For a sales seat, use a live cold email rewrite or an ICP cut, not a pitch about selling <sup><a href="#sources">[262]</a></sup>.

## Score independently, then debrief

Every interviewer submits a score and a note before the group meets. A live vote rewards the loudest voice in the room. An independent score rewards the evidence <sup><a href="#sources">[373]</a></sup>.

At the debrief, read the notes, not the adjectives. When two reviewers diverge, the gap itself is the finding: one of them saw a behavior the other missed. Close the loop by naming what the next interview would test.

## How the loop changes by stage

- **Seed.** The founder runs the loop alone and writes the first scorecard from the last ten calls. There is no committee yet.
- **First sales team.** One loop covers the SDR seat. Add a work sample, and score it against a written rubric.
- **Growth.** Split the loop by seat. An SDR interview tests the ICP and the first line; an AE interview tests discovery and the sales process <sup><a href="#sources">[262]</a></sup>.
- **Scale.** A hiring committee reads the packet and never meets the candidate in person, so the written evidence carries the decision <sup><a href="#sources">[373]</a></sup>.

## Failure modes

- **Charm as a signal.** The likable candidate scores high and the quiet one scores low, on the same evidence. Fix: score the answer, then discuss the person.
- **A different loop per candidate.** Each panel asks its own questions, so the scores do not compare. Fix: freeze the question set before the first interview.
- **Scores written after the debrief.** The group talks first and the numbers follow the loudest voice. Fix: collect independent scores in writing first.
- **Competencies copied from a template.** The card describes a generic seller, not this seat. Fix: pull the competencies from the hiring manager and the top performers.
- **No work sample.** The panel grades a story about selling instead of the selling. Fix: put one artifact in front of every candidate <sup><a href="#sources">[372]</a></sup>.

## Worked example: an SDR loop that separates the sample from the story

A team hires two SDRs in the same week. Both interview well. The first one talks through a clean discovery call from a past job. The second one rewrites the team's worst cold email and cuts a list of twenty accounts to six.

The panel scores both candidates on the same rubric. The story-teller scores high on communication and low on judgment. The rewriter scores high on judgment and average on communication. The scorecard shows which skill the ramp can teach and which one it cannot.

The team hires the rewriter and ramps the communication skill in month one. The story-teller was a real candidate for a different seat, not this one.

## Interview loop and scorecard checklist

```text
INTERVIEW LOOP AND SCORECARD CHECKLIST
[ ] Scorecard written before sourcing
[ ] Four to six competencies, pulled from the work
[ ] Same questions, same order, every candidate
[ ] Anchored rubric: poor, borderline, solid, outstanding
[ ] Written notes behind every rating
[ ] One work sample per candidate
[ ] Independent scores submitted before the debrief
[ ] One hire or no-hire call with a one-line rationale
```

## Agentic layer

An agent can draft the question set from the scorecard, collect the written notes into one packet, and flag a competency with no scored evidence. A human still runs the interview and makes the hire. Set the guardrail first: the agent organizes the evidence and never scores a person. Read [Agentic outbound](/docs/agentic/agentic-outbound) for the desk-level SOP.
