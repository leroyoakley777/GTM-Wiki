# Sourcing and pipeline

A pipeline is a channel mix, not a pile of resumes. The mix sets your cost per hire, your time to fill, and how long the hire stays. Choose the channels on purpose, then measure each one against the same numbers.

This page covers the channels, the measurement, and the failure modes. Read [Recruiting](/docs/recruiting) for the hire decision, [Interview loop and scorecard](./interview-and-scorecard) for the loop, and [SDRs](/docs/roles/sdrs) for the seat the pipeline feeds.

## What each channel buys

Four measures separate a strong channel from a cheap one. Cost per hire is what you pay to fill the seat. Time to fill is how long the seat stays open. Retention is how long the hire stays. Quality of hire is how well the hire performs, and few teams measure it: only 20 percent of organizations track quality of hire <sup><a href="#sources">[374]</a></sup>.

A channel that wins on cost and loses on retention is the expensive one. SHRM puts the average cost per hire at $5,475 for a nonexecutive role and $35,879 for an executive role, with a median time to fill a nonexecutive role of 39 calendar days <sup><a href="#sources">[374]</a></sup>. Those figures average every channel together. Your mix moves them.

## Read a channel by cost, speed, and stay

| Channel | How it fills the seat | Where it wins | Where it breaks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employee referral | A current employee vouches for a name | Retention | Caps out, and it favors the network you already hold |
| Inbound job board | The candidate answers a posting | Volume | Screening load, and little signal on fit |
| Outbound sourcing | A recruiter finds and contacts the candidate | Hard-to-fill and senior seats | Recruiter time, and a cold first message |
| Agency | A partner runs the search | Speed on a rare seat | Fee per hire, often 20 to 30 percent of salary |
| Internal move | An employee changes seat | Ramp time and retention | Needs a bench to move |

## Measure the channel, not the recruiter

Attribution tells you which channel produced the hire. Count a hire against the channel that first brought the candidate in, and keep the count for the full year. A channel with ten hires and a channel with one hire do not compare on a raw cost figure, so divide the spend by the count.

Referrals carry the strongest retention record. A study of more than 91,000 employees across 50-plus companies found that half of referral hires stay at least 38 months, against 22 months for non-referral hires, about 70 percent longer <sup><a href="#sources">[375]</a></sup>. Among companies with a referral program, 62 percent cut the time to fill a referred seat, and referred hires retain at twice the first-year rate <sup><a href="#sources">[376]</a></sup>. Most companies already trust the channel: 84 percent rate employee referrals as the most cost-effective way to find talent <sup><a href="#sources">[376]</a></sup>.

Build the program inside the company rather than renting it. Sales Acceleration Formula names a recruiting agency within the corporation as the cheaper path than an external agency or a corporate recruiting team <sup><a href="#sources">[262]</a></sup>.

## How sourcing differs by stage

- **Seed.** The founder sources from a personal network, and the first ten hires arrive through people they know. A referral program needs employees, so it comes later.
- **Series A desk.** One recruiter runs outbound sourcing for the SDR and AE seats, and the job board carries inbound volume. Track cost per hire by channel from the first requisition.
- **Scale.** Every channel gets a budget and a target, and the mix shifts each quarter toward the channels with the best retention. The recruiting budget sits near 26 percent of the HR budget at the average company <sup><a href="#sources">[374]</a></sup>.

## Worked example: the channel that looked cheap

A team fills six AE seats in a quarter. Five arrive from an agency at 25 percent of salary, and one arrives from a referral.

On cost per hire alone, the referral looks free and the agency looks expensive. The team reads retention instead. The referral hire stays past two years. Two agency hires leave inside a year, and the team pays the fee again to refill both seats.

The next quarter runs on a referral push plus outbound sourcing. The team pays a smaller fee on one hard seat and fills the rest from its own network. The mix cost less across the two quarters, and the seats stayed filled.

## Failure modes

- **The cheapest channel on paper.** A job board posts at no fee, so it looks free, and the screening load lands on the hiring manager. Fix: cost the screening hours into the channel.
- **One channel for every seat.** A senior seat and a junior seat do not source the same way. Fix: set the channel mix per seat, not per company.
- **A referral program with no owner.** Employees refer, the names sit unread, and participation falls. Fix: name an owner and answer every referral inside two business days.
- **Agency as the default.** The agency fills the seat and the team learns nothing about the market. Fix: run outbound sourcing on one seat per quarter to keep the market map current.
- **No quality measure.** The team tracks cost and time and never checks whether the hire worked. Only 20 percent of organizations track quality of hire <sup><a href="#sources">[374]</a></sup>. Fix: score the hire at six months against the scorecard from [Interview loop and scorecard](./interview-and-scorecard).

## Sourcing checklist

```text
SOURCING AND PIPELINE CHECKLIST
[ ] Channel mix set per seat, not per company
[ ] Cost per hire counted by channel
[ ] Time to fill counted by channel
[ ] Retention of each channel read at 12 months
[ ] Quality of hire scored at six months
[ ] Referral program owned, with a two-day reply rule
[ ] One seat per quarter sourced outbound to keep the market map
[ ] Channel mix reviewed every quarter
```

## Agentic layer

An agent can run the pipeline arithmetic and the first pass on inbound. It can pull each channel's cost, speed, and retention into one table, flag a channel whose retention fell, dedupe inbound applicants against the applicant system, and draft the first outreach for a sourced candidate. A human still makes the contact decision and runs the interview. Set the guardrail first: the agent organizes the pipeline and never rejects a person. Read [Agentic outbound](/docs/agentic/agentic-outbound) for the desk-level SOP.
