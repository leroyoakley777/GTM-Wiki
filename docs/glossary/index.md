# Glossary

The **GTM glossary** defines the terms, acronyms, and shorthand used across this wiki — and across the industry. If a page uses a term you don't recognize, it's probably here.

Terms are grouped by theme. Each entry is a first-principles definition, not a copy-pasted vendor line.

## People & Accounts

- **ICP — Ideal Customer Profile.** The description of the account type that gets the most value from your product and is the most profitable to serve. ICP is a company-level profile (industry, size, region, tech stack, revenue band), distinct from a persona, which is person-level.
- **Persona.** The individual buyer or user within an ICP account — e.g. VP Sales, RevOps lead, SDR. A product often has multiple personas: user, champion, economic buyer, technical buyer.
- **Champion.** An internal advocate who believes in your product and sells it on your behalf inside their org. Not the same as a buyer — a champion helps you navigate and close.
- **Buying committee.** The group of stakeholders who must sign off before a deal closes. Committees have grown meaningfully since 2020 (see [sales-cycle-benchmarks](../data/06-sales-cycle-benchmarks.md)) — more people, more sign-off, longer cycles.
- **Economic buyer.** The person with budget authority — the one who can actually say yes to the money.
- **Technical buyer / evaluator.** The person who assesses whether your product works technically — often security, IT, or engineering.

## Funnel & Pipeline

- **Funnel.** The top-to-bottom path an account takes from first touch to closed deal. Naming varies by company, but the shape is constant: awareness → interest → evaluation → decision → close.
- **MQL — Marketing Qualified Lead.** A lead marketing has scored or qualified and handed to sales. The threshold is arbitrary and varies by company — the score's only actual job is to decide "sales-worthy."
- **SQL — Sales Qualified Lead.** A lead sales has qualified as an actual opportunity worth pursuing. Where MQL is marketing's judgment, SQL is sales's.
- **Pipeline.** The sum of open opportunities at every stage, usually expressed in dollar value. Pipeline quality beats pipeline size — a full pipeline of wrong-fit accounts closes nothing.
- **Conversion rate.** The share of accounts that move from one stage to the next. Read it per stage, not as one blended number (see [conversion-rate-benchmarks](../data/00-conversion-rate-benchmarks.md)).
- **AOV — Average Order Value.** Average revenue per order or deal.

## Go-to-Market Models & Channels

- **GTM — Go-to-Market.** The entire strategy and motion by which a company brings a product to market and acquires customers: who you target, how you reach them, how you sell, and how you price.
- **Outbound.** The motion where you initiate — you pick the account and reach out. Cold email, cold call, sequences. Lower reply rates, but you control the target list (see [outbound-response-rates](../data/05-outbound-response-rates.md)).
- **Inbound.** The motion where the buyer initiates — they come to you via search, content, referrals, or word of mouth. Higher intent, but you don't control who arrives.
- **Product-led (PLG).** The motion where the product itself acquires, activates, and expands users — free tier, self-serve, in-product upgrade. Product-qualified signals replace marketing qualification.
- **PQL — Product Qualified Lead.** A user whose behavior inside the product signals buying intent — e.g. hit usage limits, invited teammates, upgraded. The product-led analog of an MQL (see [product-led](../channels/03-product-led.md)).
- **ABM — Account-Based Marketing.** Focused go-to-market on a named list of high-value accounts rather than broad market spray. Trades reach for precision (see [abm](../channels/09-abm.md)).
- **Motion / play.** The specific repeatable sequence you run to generate and close pipeline — e.g. "event-driven outbound," "founder-led sales," "land-and-expand."
- **Land-and-expand.** Win a small initial deal, then grow it inside the account over time. The standard SaaS wedge.

## Revenue Metrics

- **CAC — Customer Acquisition Cost.** Total sales + marketing cost divided by customers acquired in a period. A leading cost-efficiency metric.
- **LTV — Lifetime Value.** The total revenue a customer generates over their entire relationship. Often paired with CAC as the LTV:CAC ratio — a crude but common health check.
- **NRR — Net Revenue Retention.** Revenue retained from existing customers over a period, including expansion, contraction, and churn. Above 100% means the base grows on its own; it's the single best signal of product-market fit.
- **GRR — Gross Revenue Retention.** Revenue retained excluding expansion — pure churn + contraction. Always ≤ NRR.
- **ACV — Annual Contract Value.** The value of a contract annualized.
- **ARR — Annual Recurring Revenue.** The annualized value of recurring subscription revenue.
- **Churn.** The rate at which customers (logo churn) or revenue (dollar churn) leave. Logo churn counts accounts; dollar churn weights by revenue.
- **Expansion.** New revenue from existing customers — upsell, cross-sell, price increases. The growth engine that makes NRR > 100%.

## Operations & Infrastructure

- **RevOps — Revenue Operations.** The function that unifies marketing, sales, and customer-success operations — data, process, tooling, and compensation — so the revenue engine runs as one system rather than three silos. Small team, outsized leverage (see [revenue-operations](../foundations/06-revenue-operations.md)).
- **Tech stack.** The set of tools a GTM org runs on — CRM, outbound, analytics, conversation intelligence, RevOps (see [tools](../tools/index.md)).
- **CRM — Customer Relationship Management.** The system of record for accounts, contacts, opportunities, and pipeline. The source of truth for what's happening in the funnel.
- **Conversation intelligence.** Tooling that records and analyzes sales calls — talk-to-listen ratio, objection handling, coaching signals.
- **Sequence.** A pre-scripted, multi-step series of touches (email, call, social) sent over time to advance an outbound conversation.

## Agentic GTM

- **Agentic GTM.** GTM activity where autonomous AI agents — not humans — execute part of the motion: research, personalization, drafting, sequencing, follow-up. Agents handle the repeatable 80%; humans own judgment and relationship.
- **Guardrail.** A rule or constraint that bounds what an agent may do — what it can send, to whom, how often, and what it must not claim. Non-negotiable for anything customer-facing (see [guardrails-and-measurement](../agentic/03-guardrails-and-measurement.md)).
- **Agent-vs-human workflow.** The division of a motion into agent-executed and human-executed steps, decided per step — not a blanket either/or (see [agent-vs-human-workflows](../agentic/06-agent-vs-human-workflows.md)).
