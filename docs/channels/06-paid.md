# Paid Acquisition

**Paid is the only channel you can turn on and off with a credit card — and the only one that burns cash every second it is on.** It is the great scaler and the great eraser. Done right, paid lets you buy exactly as much pipeline as your sales team can close, then stop. Done badly, it converts cash into clicks and a dashboard that looks busy while the company quietly loses money.

This page is the *where* of paid: when it is the right call and how to judge whether the motion is actually profitable. It covers the paid motion broadly — search, social, and outbound display — because the economics, not the ad platform, are what decide.

---

## One Number That Matters Most

**Payback period on CAC — the number of months of gross margin it takes a new customer to repay what you spent to acquire them.** A healthy paid channel pays back in under 12 months; great ones in under 6. That single number — more than CTR, CPM, or cost-per-lead — is what tells you whether paid is a business or a donation.

Do not track "cost per lead" or "cost per click" as the score. Cheap clicks that never convert to paying customers are expensive. CAC payback against gross margin is revenue.

## Is Paid Even the Right Channel?

Use the [Channels Bullseye](../foundations/gtm-channels-bullseye) to pick your one best channel first. Paid wins when:

- **You have proven the offer organically first.** Paid amplifies what already works; it cannot invent demand for a broken offer. If free channels cannot convert, paid will just pay to fail faster.
- **The math closes.** CAC payback under your target, against actual gross margin, at a scale that matters. Model it before you spend a dollar.
- **You can scale supply faster than it saturates.** The moment every competitor bids up your keywords and clicks stop compounding, the economics shift.

If none of those are true, paid is the fastest way to lose money in GTM. It is the channel to add last, after the offer and the funnel are proven.

## Paid Funnel — Benchmarks That Diagnose

Each stage is where money is spent, so every one of them is a leak.

| Stage | Healthy range | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| CTR (paid) | 1–3%+ for search, 0.5–1%+ for display | Ad relevance + offer pull |
| Landing conversion | 2–5%+ | Whether the page earns the click |
| Lead → SQL | 20–40% | Whether the traffic fits the ICP |
| SQL → Closed-won | 15–30% | Whether sales can close what you buy |
| CAC payback | `<12 months` | Whether the whole loop is profitable |

The fatal trap is optimizing the top of the funnel — CTR and landing conversion — while the bottom leaks. You can have a beautiful 3% CTR and a broken SQL→won stage, and you are just buying volume to lose money at a higher rate.

---

## Two Ways Paid Dies

**1. Unprofitable unit economics that you ignore.** The dashboard shows signups climbing; the P&L shows cash draining. If CAC payback exceeds your target, you are not acquiring customers, you are renting them.

**2. Dependence.** You scale to the point where paid is a huge share of pipeline, then the auction gets more expensive or the budget gets cut, and the company craters. Paid should fund growth, never become the growth itself. It is the throttle, not the engine.

The engine is compounding — content, community, product-led, referrals. Paid accelerates whatever compounding channel you have. If there is no compounding channel underneath, paid is borrowing tomorrow's revenue today.

---

## Common failure modes

- **Scaling before proving payback.** Buying volume to "figure it out at scale" is the most expensive way to learn.
- **Optimizing clicks, not customers.** CTR is a vanity metric when the bottom of the funnel leaks.
- **No incrementality test.** You cannot tell paid's actual contribution without pausing it. Geo or time-based holdouts reveal whether paid is additive or just cannibalizing organic.
- **One channel dependence.** Search, social, and display saturate and swing on cost. Diversify or die with the auction.
- **Letting paid mask a broken funnel.** Paid traffic is the most expensive diagnostic you will ever run. Fix the funnel first; then spend.
