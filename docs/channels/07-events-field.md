# Events &amp; Field

**Goal:** turn in-person and live moments (conferences, user groups,
field events, webinars, sponsorship kits) into named-account pipeline
that closes, and prove it with attributed numbers, not badge counts.

Events spend the most to reach the fewest people. That is the honest
opening. For a high-ACV product with a concentrated buyer, the format
out-earns every other channel per conversation. For a cheap product with
a scattered buyer, it is a line-item donation to the conference economy.
This page tells you which situation you are in and how to run the motion
when it fits.

This page is the *where* and *how* of events and field: when the channel
is the right call, the funnel and its named-and-dated benchmarks, the
five-format stack, sequencing, objections, failure modes with fixes,
handoff to sales, the agentic SOP, and a case matrix. Every number
carries a named source and an "as of" date. Vendor-sourced stats are
labeled `(vendor source)`.

The execution layer lives elsewhere. For the direct-outreach component,
read the [Outbound from Zero playbook](/docs/playbooks/outbound-from-zero).
For the agent harness on top, read [Agentic Outbound](/docs/agentic/agentic-outbound).
For the account-concentration sibling, read [ABM](/docs/channels/abm).

## One Number That Matters Most

**Event-sourced closed-won revenue at 180 days, divided by all-in event
spend.** That is ROEI, return on event investment. It is the number that
tells leadership whether an event worked.

Every other metric is an input to it. Qualified conversations, meetings
booked, pipeline created, cost per meeting: each explains a slice. None
of them is the score. A badge scan is a data-entry row. A conversation is
a chance. Revenue that traces to the event within a defined window is the
outcome.

Do not track "leads collected" or "badge scans" as the score. 98% of
teams struggle to justify event spend to leadership, and 86% cannot
attribute ROI back to events (Vendelux 2026 B2B Events Survey, 120+
marketing leaders, `(vendor source)`). The teams that defend their
budgets close that attribution chain operationally, before the event
ends.

## What Events and Field Marketing Is

Events and field marketing is the live, in-person slice of the channel
map. It covers every format where a buyer shows up in a room, on a
screen, or at a booth. It spans five formats, each with a different
cost curve and a different conversion profile:

| Format | What it is | Cost profile | When it wins |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conferences / trade shows | Third-party event where you sponsor, exhibit, or attend | Highest all-in: exhibit $10,000–$30,000 per booth, staffing $2,500–$5,000 (Tradeshow Labs, 2025) | Dense ICP, you pre-book meetings |
| User groups | Recurring meetups of your or a community's users | Low; room, catering, travel | Retention, expansion, references |
| Field events | Owned dinners, roadshows, exec briefings in a metro | Medium per event, compounding across cities | Hard-to-reach execs, regional pipeline |
| Webinars | Owned live online session | Lowest per event; $72 CPL (Visitor Queue via Zoom, 2026) | Educating many, feeding pipeline at scale |
| Sponsorship kits | Buying logo placement and activations at someone else's event | Variable, often poor ROI alone | Brand reach, never as primary pipeline driver |

The formats are not interchangeable. A webinar reaches hundreds cheaply
and converts at about the same rate as other channels. A conference
reaches fewer people expensively but builds trust that shortens a sales
cycle. You pick the format by matching cost to deal size and buyer
concentration.

Field marketing is the subset that lives in a metro and a named list:
dinner programs, roadshows, exec briefings. It is ABM's in-person arm.
For the account-planning that pairs with it, read [ABM](/docs/channels/abm).

## When Events Is the Right Channel

Use the [Channels Bullseye](/docs/foundations/gtm-channels-bullseye) to
pick your one best channel before you commit. Events win when **all
three** are true:

- **High ACV and a committee buying process.** In-person trust matters
  when a deal runs many months and many stakeholders. The average B2B
  purchase now involves 13 stakeholders (Forrester, State of Business
  Buying 2024), and Gartner's 2025 research puts the committee at 9–11,
  up from 5–7 in 2017, reaching up to 25 for enterprise technology
  purchases. An event puts several of those people in one room at once.
- **Your ICP attends in density.** The event is only worth its cost if
  your buyer is in the room. Buying authority at trade shows runs 81% of
  attendees, and 72% of attendees plan to buy at least one product they
  see (CEIR). A 50,000-attendee mega-show is worthless if your buyer is
  not among them.
- **The format beats your alternative per meeting.** Compare cost per
  qualified meeting at the event against outbound's same number. Trade
  show leads run about $142 each (CEIR), and it takes 3.5 calls to close
  a trade show lead versus 4.5 for a cold lead (CEIR). Events win when
  the per-meeting economics hold.

If none of these is true, events are the wrong channel. A low-ACV
product with a broad buyer cannot justify the spend. Skip the show and
put the budget into [outbound](/docs/channels/outbound) or inbound
instead.

**Acceptance gate:** you can name the deal size and the events where
your ICP registers, or you stop here. No named events, no event
budget.

## Events Funnel and Benchmarks

The funnel runs from attendee to closed revenue. Each stage has a
named-and-dated benchmark so you can tell where an event leaks:

| Stage | Healthy benchmark | Source (as of) | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| Registrations → attendee | ~57% (webinars); 49% live | ON24 2025 Webinar Benchmarks; Zoom 2026 | Whether your promotion works |
| Lead creation → qualified | 5.50% in-person; 6.41% virtual; 4.82% other channels | HockeyStack 2025 (198 SaaS cos, 2.6M deals) | Whether your ICP is in the room |
| Creation → closed-won | 12.1% in-person 2025 avg; 14.2% virtual H1 2025 | HockeyStack 2025 | Whether conversations convert |
| Opportunity → close | 40% for event-sourced leads | HockeyStack 2025 | The strongest BOFU channel studied |
| Cost per trade show lead | ~$142 (range $100–$300) | CEIR; Momencio 2026 | Raw efficiency, before qualification |
| Cost per qualified lead | 3–5x the reported lead figure | Momencio 2026 | The number that matters, after removing non-buyers |

Read the HockeyStack numbers as the load-bearing core: events represent
roughly 6% of B2B deal volume but convert from creation to closed-won at
12.1%, versus 4.82% for other channels, and event-sourced leads close
from opportunity at 40% (HockeyStack 2025, `(vendor source)`). In
plain terms, events touch few deals but win a disproportionate share of
them.

**The honest comparison is events versus your other channels, not
events versus nothing.** Event cost per qualified meeting and win rate,
measured against outbound's same numbers. Events often win because the
trust is built up front, but only when the ICP is genuinely in the
room.

## Booth Versus Motion

A booth with a badge scanner is a passive bet on foot traffic. A field
motion is an engineered series of named conversations. The difference
decides the ROI.

**The engineered event runs four steps:**

1. **Pick the event for the ICP, not for the size.** Only 13% of teams
   rate their event selection as fully data-driven (Vendelux 2026,
   `(vendor source)`). The rest pick on organizer claims and
   reputation. Pick on verified attendee density.
2. **Pre-book meetings before the show opens.** Pre-event planning
   determines 76% of attendee agendas before arrival (Event Marketing
   Research, 2025). A booth that was not on the calendar before the show
   is not on the calendar at the show. 70% of teams cite lack of
   attendee-list visibility as their top barrier to pre-booking, and 55%
   start outreach under four weeks before an event, well short of the
   8–12 week cadence top programs run (Vendelux 2026, `(vendor source)`).
3. **Go with a target-account list.** 20 named accounts, a reason to
   meet each, and a follow-up plan already written. The event becomes a
   pipeline stage for named accounts, not a party.
4. **Treat every conversation as a CRM event.** Each meeting feeds the
   CRM with a source, a next step, and an owner, traced to an account.

The booth is just the venue. The pre-booked, named-account conversation
is the motion. Teams that staff pre-event outbound 10–12 weeks ahead hit
benchmarks. Teams that staff it in the final two weeks fall short
(Vendelux event examples, 2026, `(vendor source)`).

## Channel Stack and Benchmarks

### Conferences and trade shows

The highest-cost, highest-trust format. All-in exhibiting runs
$10,000–$30,000 per booth, plus $2,500–$5,000 to staff it (Tradeshow
Labs, 2025). The U.S. B2B trade show market reached $15.78 billion in
2024, first time above pre-COVID levels (CEIR Index, 2024).

Benchmarks:
- 91% of attendees leave with a more positive brand perception of the
  companies they engage with (ZoomInfo, 2026).
- 77% of exhibitors report positive ROI when measured properly (CEIR,
  via PureExhibits, 2026).
- 52% of marketers attribute at least half of their 2024 closed-won
  deals to events (HockeyStack 2025, `(vendor source)`).

Conferences fail when treated as a brand spend. They work when every
dollar is tied to a pre-booked, named-account meeting.

### User groups

Recurring meetups of your users or a community's. Low cost, high
retention value. They surface expansion opportunities and reference
customers. A customer-only dinner at a major conference, roughly $15,000
all-in, surfaced 6 expansion opportunities and 4 reference customers for
one B2B SaaS company (Vendelux event examples, 2026, `(vendor source)`).

### Field events

Owned dinners, roadshows, and exec briefings in a metro, aimed at a
named list. The format that reaches executives cold SDR cannot reach. A
cybersecurity company running quarterly dinners in five cities spent
about $280,000 across twenty dinners and generated $3.2 million in
event-sourced pipeline in year one (Vendelux event examples, 2026,
`(vendor source)`). For hard-to-reach executive buyer personas, field
programs beat inside sales on cost per ICP meeting.

### Webinars

The owned, live, low-cost format. Webinars convert registrations to
attendees at roughly 57% (ON24 2025 Webinar Benchmarks), at an average
cost per lead of $72 (Visitor Queue via Zoom, 2026). Virtual B2B events
converted from creation to closed-won at 14.2% in H1 2025 (HockeyStack
2025, `(vendor source)`). Webinars are the scale engine of the stack.
They rarely close a deal alone but feed the funnel cheaply.

### Sponsorship kits

Buying logo placement and a cheap activation at a big show. By itself,
poor ROI. Use sponsorship kits only as an entry ticket to a named-account
program (to get your people into rooms where your buyers already are),
never as the motion itself.

## Sequencing and Rollout

Events run on a timeline, not a week-of scramble. The highest-performing
programs run an 8–12 week cadence. Most teams run under four weeks
(Vendelux 2026, `(vendor source)`). A representative sequence:

| Window | Action |
|---|---|
| Week 12–10 | Verify attendee list, score ICP density, pick target accounts |
| Week 9–6 | Pre-event SDR outreach to named accounts, book meetings |
| Week 5–2 | Confirm the calendar, send invites to dinners and briefings |
| Week 1 | Logistics, booth or venue, exec availability |
| Event week | Run the meetings, capture every conversation in the CRM |
| Day +1 | First follow-up within 24 hours, next step booked |
| Week +1–2 | Second touch, CRM source and owner assigned |
| Day +90 / +180 | Measure closed-won revenue against all-in spend |

Rollout rule: start with one format and one event, prove the per-meeting
economics, then expand. A program that pre-books 13 show-floor meetings
and 4 post-show at a tier-2 conference is a healthy outcome for a
mid-market company (Vendelux event examples, 2026, `(vendor source)`).
Do not scale the calendar until one event clears the ROI gate.

## Objections, and How to Answer Them

| Reply | What it means | How to answer |
|---|---|---|
| "Events are too expensive" | They see all-in cost, not per-meeting value | Show cost per qualified meeting versus outbound ($400–$600 per cold lead) and the 40% opportunity-to-close rate |
| "We can't prove event ROI" | Measurement is not set up | It is the top reported barrier (62%, Forrester 2025). Close the attribution chain before the event, not after |
| "Nobody shows up" | Wrong event or weak promotion | Check ICP density and pre-booking. Fix selection, not the booth |
| "Badge scans are up, why cut the budget?" | They are measuring activity | Badge scans are data entry. Score is closed-won revenue at 180 days |
| "We go to the big show every year" | Habit, not strategy | Attendance is vanity. Buyer density in the room is the score |

**The rule:** answer the question with the economics, not a pitch. If
the objection is measurement, fix attribution. If it is cost, fix the
format. If the ICP is not at the event, stop going.

## Failure Modes and Fixes

- **Booth vanity.** Big booth, big badge count, no named-account
  conversations. *Fix:* measure on ICP-fit meetings booked pre-event and
  closed-won revenue at 180 days, not foot traffic.
- **No pre-booked meetings.** Every meeting at the event is an accident.
  *Fix:* staff pre-event outbound 8–12 weeks ahead. Pre-event planning
  sets 76% of agendas.
- **Wrong events.** Picking on organizer claims instead of verified
  attendee data. Only 13% of teams pick fully on data (Vendelux 2026).
  *Fix:* score events on ICP density before you spend.
- **No follow-up discipline.** The trust fades within days. A meeting
  without a booked next step within a week is a wasted flight. *Fix:*
  first follow-up within 24 hours, next step booked before the event
  ends.
- **Low ROI and nobody can say why.** 86% cannot attribute ROI to events
  (Vendelux 2026). *Fix:* trace every conversation to an account and a
  source in the CRM, then measure closed-won at 90 and 180 days.
- **Sponsorships with no motion behind them.** Logo placement alone.
  *Fix:* sponsorship kits are an entry ticket to a named-account program,
  never the strategy.
- **Treating events as brand.** "It is good for the brand" is what
  people say when the pipeline number does not work. Make it work or
  skip it.

## Handoff to Sales

The handoff is where events die most often. Fix it before you scale:

1. **Define the ready threshold.** Write down what makes a post-event
   meeting "ready": a named account, a live project, budget, or a
   rising intent score. Below it, the program keeps nurturing. Above it,
   sales takes over with the account plan.
2. **Every meeting traces to an account.** Tag each meeting to its
   target account, source, and next step. No source, no scale.
3. **Debrief within a week of the event.** What message won, which
   format converted, what objection stalled the account, what changed in
   event selection.
4. **Feed wins back into selection.** Best accounts and best formats
   become the look-alike pool for the next event round. Events are a
   loop, not a one-shot campaign.

**Acceptance gate:** the ready threshold is written, every meeting traces
to an account, and the post-event debrief rhythm exists. Skip this and
you will scale engagement nobody closes.

## Agentic Layer

Events are a strong candidate for an agent harness, because the
bottleneck is *thinking per meeting*: attendee scoring, account
selection, pre-event outreach, meeting-log capture, and follow-up.
Agents do that at volume. Humans keep judgment on the event list and the
send. The full treatment is [Agentic Outbound](/docs/agentic/agentic-outbound)
and the [Agentic Stack](/docs/agentic/agentic-stack). The reusable shape
is here.

**The rule that never changes:** the ROI gate stays, the execution layer
changes. Humans pick events and approve outreach. Agents remove the
research and follow-up bottleneck.

| Motion step | Agent does | Human approves |
|---|---|---|
| Event selection | Pulls verified attendee lists, scores ICP density per event | Picks the final event calendar |
| Account targeting | Matches the attendee list to target accounts, ranks by fit and intent | Approves the target-account list |
| Pre-event outreach | Writes and sequences reason-to-reach for named accounts, books meetings | Approves the message pattern |
| Meeting log | Captures each conversation, tags source, account, and next step in the CRM | Reviews the meeting log |
| Post-event follow-up | Sends the 24-hour and week-2 touches, books next steps | Approves the follow-up pattern |
| ROI reporting | Tracks closed-won revenue at 90/180 days against all-in spend | Reads the report, adjusts the calendar |

### One-prompt starter for running an event

Adapt to your stack:

```text
You are running an events and field program for {ICP} in {segment}. For
the event at {event name and date}, return a plan with: (1) an ICP-density
score for the event from the verified attendee list; (2) the 20 target
accounts to prioritize, ranked by fit and intent, each with a named buyer
problem and a one-line reason-to-reach grounded in a concrete signal;
(3) a pre-event outreach sequence for those accounts that books meetings
before the event; (4) a meeting-log template that captures each
conversation with source, account, and next step; (5) a post-event
follow-up sequence with a 24-hour touch and a week-2 touch. Flag any
account below the fit threshold. Return only accounts that pass the
fit gate.
```

### Guardrails

1. **Human approval before any send.** Agents draft outreach and rank
   accounts. Humans approve the message pattern and the final event
   calendar. A weak pattern scaled by an agent dies faster, not slower.
2. **Fit is non-negotiable.** Agents must not chase high-intent,
   low-fit accounts because they are loud. The fit gate is hard.
3. **Score the agent on closed-won revenue, not activity.** Measure
   event-sourced revenue at 180 days against all-in spend, not meetings
   logged or emails sent. See [GTM Metrics](/docs/foundations/gtm-metrics)
   for the measurement context.

## How the Motion Varies by Segment and Stage

Events behave differently by deal size and company maturity. There is no
single booth play.

| Dimension | Enterprise | SMB / mid-market |
|---|---|---|
| Format | Conferences, field dinners, exec briefings | Webinars, tier-2 conferences, user groups |
| Buying committee | 9–13 stakeholders (Gartner/Forrester, 2024–25) | 1–4 people |
| Sales cycle | 6–9+ months; over $100K ACV | ~90 days; under $25K ACV |
| Primary metric | Closed-won revenue at 180 days, ACV | Pipeline created, cost per meeting |
| Per-account effort | Deep, named-account plans | Semi-customized, reusable plays |

| Dimension | Launch (pre-PMF) | Mature |
|---|---|---|
| Job of events | Proof, reference customers, first logos | Net-new pipeline, expansion, churn prevention |
| Formats | Webinars, small user groups, exec dinners | Full stack: conferences, roadshows, customer events |
| ROI window | Longer; first wins justify the format | 90–180 day closed-won discipline |

The enterprise committee math is why events pay. With 9–13 stakeholders
in a buying group, an event is the rare place several of them sit in one
room at once (Gartner 2025). For SMB, the format must be cheap and the
pipeline must close inside the quarter.

**The transferable takeaway:** match the format to deal size, pre-book
named-account meetings before the show, measure closed-won revenue at
180 days against all-in spend, and treat events as a pipeline stage,
never a default line item.
