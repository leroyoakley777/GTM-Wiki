# Community-Led Growth

**Goal**: decide whether a group of users who talk to each other should be
your acquisition engine, then run it: seed the conversation, let members
help each other, route the resulting signals to sales and CS, and track
whether the group is pulling in revenue or just existing.

Community-led growth is the slowest channel and the one that compounds the
hardest. It does not deliver a spike of pipeline this quarter. It builds a
durable asset: a group of people who know your product, trust you, and
bring their colleagues in, and that asset keeps compounding for years.
The catch is structural: it only works for audiences that genuinely benefit
from talking to each other. Community around a product nobody needs to
discuss is an empty Slack group.

This page is the *where* and *how* of community-led growth. It covers when
the channel is right and when it is not, the participation and referral
funnel with named, dated benchmarks, the platform and moderation stack, a
four-phase rollout, objections, failure modes with fixes, the handoff to
sales and CS, how the motion shifts by segment and stage, a case matrix of
documented builds, and the agentic SOP that seeds content, moderates,
routes signals, and tracks community-sourced pipeline. Every number carries
a named source and an "as of" date. Aggregate and vendor-sourced stats are
labeled.

**Read the companion pages:** the [Channels
Bullseye](../foundations/gtm-channels-bullseye) decides whether community
is your one best channel. [Community-Led Growth Case
Studies](../case-studies/community-led) carries the documented Notion build.
[Funnel Benchmarks](../data/funnel) holds the multiplicative leakage math
this channel feeds into. Community and [Product-Led
Growth](../channels/product-led) often pair: the community feeds the free
tier that PLG converts.

## One Number That Matters Most

**Member-initiated referrals: the share of new signups who arrive because
a member brought them. A compounding community pushes this toward 30–50% of
new signups over time.** That is the number that proves the community is
doing acquisition work rather than just existing. People who come in because
a member referred them are the highest-intent, lowest-CAC users you will
ever acquire.

The range is a working planning target from first-hand builds across
2014–2026, not a published vendor benchmark: referral share is not a
standardized reporting category, so no single source publishes it. Treat it
as the direction to push, and measure your own cohort trend against it.

Two published data points anchor the economics behind it:

- **Members who join a B2B online community spend 19% more after joining**
  (aggregated across community-marketing studies, cited 2026).
- **Community-led deals close faster: 72% close within 90 days versus 42%
  of sales- and marketing-led deals** (aggregated across community ROI
  studies, cited 2026).

Do not track membership count as a score. Members who never speak, never
refer, and never convert are a mailing list, not a community. Bodies are
vanity. Referral share is revenue.

## Is Community Even the Right Channel?

Use the [Channels Bullseye](../foundations/gtm-channels-bullseye) to pick
your one best channel first. Community-led wins when **all three** hold:

- **Your users genuinely benefit from talking to each other.** The group
  must be useful to members independent of you. If they have no reason to
  stay, they leave, and you have nothing.
- **The product has a learning curve or a craft.** Tools for developers,
  marketers, operations people, and other practitioners create community
  because users teach each other. A craft means there are questions worth
  answering.
- **Your best customers are the people who answer the questions.** A
  community where you are the only helper is a support queue, and you will
  burn out running it.

Community is the **wrong** channel when none of those hold, and it fails
fast in three specific situations:

- **A commodity product with no practitioner craft.** Nobody needs to
  discuss a product that requires no skill to use. The group becomes a
  marketing feed and the members stop showing up.
- **You need pipeline this quarter.** Community compounds quarterly, not
  weekly. If revenue is due this month, run [Outbound](../channels/outbound)
  or [Paid](../channels/paid) while the community matures.
- **You are not prepared to feed it for 6–12 months.** A community that
  gets attention for two months and then dies is worse than none, because
  the members who trusted you with their time remember the ghost town.

If community is right, it is rarely the only channel. It pairs with inbound
(the community produces the content inbound ranks) and with PLG (the
community feeds the free tier). It is a compounding asset you run *while*
other motions fill the near-term pipe.

## Community Funnel: Benchmarks That Diagnose

Community is a funnel like any other, but the stages are behaviors, not CRM
statuses. Name each stage and measure the leak before you touch a single
campaign.

| Stage | What it is | What leaks it |
|---|---|---|
| Visitor → member | A person joins the group | No clear value to join for, no welcome, no reason to return |
| Member → active | A member speaks, replies, or reacts | No seed content, nobody to talk to, moderation buries newcomers |
| Active → referrer | A member brings a colleague in | No referral mechanic, no gratitude, no reason to share |
| Active → trial / customer | A member tries or buys the product | No bridge from community to product, sales ignores community signals |
| Customer → advocate | A customer teaches others and produces content | No ambassador program, no recognition, no path to superfan |

The leakage is multiplicative, not additive. Two stages at 50% each give
you 25% end to end, not 100%. This is the same arithmetic as the [Funnel
Benchmarks](../data/funnel) page, applied to behaviors instead of pipeline
stages.

### Participation: the honest engagement reality

The classic model is the **90-9-1 rule** from Jakob Nielsen (NN/g, 2006):
roughly 90% of members lurk, 9% contribute occasionally, and 1% create most
of the content. The rule is not a failure state. Lurking is healthy: the
90% are consuming value and gaining loyalty. The mistake is measuring
success by how many people *talk* when the job is whether the value
spreads.

The rule has shifted with modern platforms, and the updated data is worth
knowing because it changes what "healthy" means:

- **Small communities (under 5,000 members) show roughly 33% of members as
  creators and contributors**: about 3x the classic 90-9-1 expectation
  (Higher Logic, community-engagement research).
- **Medium and large communities (10,000–50,000 members) show roughly 20%
  of members participating**, split about evenly between creating content
  and reacting to it (Higher Logic, community-engagement research).
- **The top ~10% of contributors still generate roughly 56% of all
  community content** in a large cross-community study, and hyper-
  contributors average around 2.7% of members (Lithium, analysis of 143
  communities).

Read these together: participation is **higher and more evenly spread in
small communities** and collapses toward a small creator class as the group
scales. That is expected. Your job at scale is not to force the 90% to
speak: it is to make the 1% who do excellent work visible, and to keep the
lurkers converting downstream.

### Revenue funnel: the benchmarks that tie to business

| Stage | Benchmark | Source (as of) |
|---|---|---|
| Community members spend more | +19% spend after joining a B2B online community | Aggregate of community-marketing studies, cited 2026 |
| Community members convert faster | 58% of companies say community members convert faster than typical leads | Aggregate of community-marketing studies, cited 2026 |
| Community-led deals close speed | 72% close within 90 days vs 42% for sales/marketing-led | Aggregate of community ROI studies, cited 2026 |
| Dedicated community program | +21% higher revenue growth vs companies without one | CMX community study, 2022 |
| Word-of-mouth conversion lift | Community-driven word-of-mouth lifts conversions by ~22% | Aggregate of community-marketing studies, cited 2026 |

The honest caveat on these four aggregate rows: they are secondary-sourced
roundups, not a single clean study, and the definitions differ. Treat them
as directional evidence that community moves the bottom line, not as precise
rates to optimize against. The number you actually control is your own
member-to-customer conversion by cohort, tracked monthly.

**The power metric is referral share** (the 30–50% planning range above).
Everything before it (joins, activity, answers) is necessary but not
sufficient. A busy community that never refers is a club, not a channel.

## Three Kinds of Community Value

**1. Acquisition.** Members refer their colleagues because the group
genuinely helped them. This is the flywheel: happy member → referral → new
member → happy member. Referral share is the score.

**2. Retention.** Members who are embedded in the community churn less.
Their professional network lives there, on top of the product. The
community is a switching cost the product alone cannot provide.

**3. Product insight.** The group is the cheapest, most honest focus group
you will ever have. Members tell you exactly what to build, free, at scale,
and they answer each other before your roadmap meetings even happen.

A community that only serves one of these leaves two-thirds of its value on
the table. Engineer for all three, and measure each separately.

## Channel Stack: Platforms, Moderation, Analytics

Community tooling spans the space you host, the moderation that keeps it
livable, and the analytics that tie it to revenue. This is one working
stack, with what each layer does and how to wire it.

| Layer | Tool (this guide) | What it does | How to wire it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discussion forum | **Discourse** | Long-lived threaded discussion, searchable answers, strong moderation tooling, 9/10 discussion score in a 2026 platform comparison | Default for a Q&amp;A and knowledge hub. Seed it before you open it. Let solved threads become your docs |
| Paid / curated community | **Circle** | Monetizable, gated membership community with courses and events | Use when the community is a product in itself or you want paid-membership value capture |
| Realtime / chat | **Discord** or **Slack** | Immediate Q&amp;A and community hangout | Discord for broad developer/public groups, Slack for private B2B customer groups. Use channels, not one giant room |
| Moderation | **Discord AutoMod**, **Sift**, or **Hivebrite** moderation | Spam filtering, toxicity flags, new-member screening | Auto-filter first, human review flags. New members approve fast or they never return |
| Analytics | **Common Room** or a platform-native analytics layer | Engagement, active-member share, and community-sourced revenue attribution | Instrument the join event and every member-to-trial touch. Split engagement from revenue |
| CRM integration | **Salesforce** or **HubSpot** | Records community signals on the account, routes qualified members to sales | Pipe member-activity and trial events into the CRM so the handoff below has a source to act on |
| Content seeding | A research + writing agent, or your editorial calendar | Starts the conversations you want to see so members have something to answer | Draft 3–5 seed threads a week on questions your ICP actually asks. A human approves before post |

**The wiring rule:** every layer must hand off cleanly. Discourse answers
become docs, analytics flags the active members, the CRM records the
signals, sales and CS act on them, and the loop feeds back. A platform that
holds data in a silo is a bottleneck. See [Agentic
Stack](../agentic/agentic-stack) for the automation wiring at depth.

**The platform decision in one line:** forum for answers that compound,
chat for energy that does not, paid community only when membership is a
product. Most B2B communities run a forum for durable Q&amp;A and a chat for
the pulse, with the forum feeding the docs.

## Sequencing: the Four-Phase Build

Community is a flywheel, not a campaign. Sequence it so each phase produces
a measurable signal before the next:

**Phase 0: Validate the value.** Before you open any space, confirm your
users have questions worth answering. Watch existing channels: support
tickets, social replies, competitor forums: and list the 20 questions your
ICP asks most. If the list is empty or trivial, community is the wrong
channel. Gate: you have a written list of questions your members will
genuinely want answered.

**Phase 1: Seed and instrument.** Stand up the space, seed the first
conversations yourself, and ship analytics on the join event and every
engagement touch. Define "active member" in one sentence and measure the
share. Gate: you can report active-member share and participation rates on
live traffic.

**Phase 2: Convert.** Build the bridge from community to product: a trial
gate, a product mention, an ambassador who onboards newcomers. Start routing
member signals into the CRM. Gate: you can attribute a member to a trial or
a revenue-influenced account, and the referral-share trend is visible.

**Phase 3: Compound.** Formalize the ambassadors, turn the best member
answers into content and docs, and close the loop with CS and product.
Gate: referral share is moving toward the 30–50% planning range, and
retention among community members is measurably above non-members.

Each phase has an acceptance gate because the cost of skipping grows. Skip
Phase 1 and Phase 2 becomes guessing. Skip Phase 2 and Phase 3 compounds a
community that is popular but produces no revenue.

## How the Motion Shifts by Segment and Stage

Community-led looks different depending on who you sell to and how mature
the program is. This matrix keeps expectations honest.

| Dimension | SMB / self-serve | Enterprise / mid-market |
|---|---|---|
| Primary member | Practitioner and individual user | Buyer, champion, and power user |
| Group's job | Teach craft, drive referrals and word-of-mouth | Build the buyer network and the champion base that expands the deal |
| Platform | Public Discord, Discourse forum, or Slack | Private Slack or gated Circle, plus an analyst-facing knowledge hub |
| Key metric | Member-initiated referral share | Community-influenced pipeline and NRR among engaged accounts |
| Sales role | Rare, acts on high-signal member referrals | Central, surfaces the champion and expansion intent |
| Risk | Ghost town: nobody shows up | Moderation burn and ROI skepticism from a small-deal-count base |

| Dimension | Early (0–12 months) | Mature (12+ months) |
|---|---|---|
| Job | Prove the questions exist and the group has life | Turn the group into a revenue engine and retention asset |
| Metric | Active-member share and participation rate | Referral share, community-influenced pipeline, NRR delta |
| Staffing | Founder or one operator seeding and moderating | A community lead plus a moderation layer and an ambassador program |
| Expectation | Near-zero direct pipeline, with the value being the flywheel forming | The community is a named source of pipeline and a retention lever |

The failure that spans all four cells: **treating the enterprise motion as
a bigger SMB motion.** Enterprise community is a private, gated, moderated
buyer network with a champion strategy, not a public Discord with more
seats. Match the platform and the metric to the cell, not to one generic
community plan.

## Case Matrix: Documented Builds

The full documented Notion build lives on [Community-Led Growth Case
Studies](../case-studies/community-led). The operating matrix:

| Company | Motion | Documented result | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Notion | Ambassador program + community hub + superfan enablement | 20M users and 1M+ community members, hired its biggest fan as Head of Community at 15 employees | Michelle Goodall community case study |
| Notion | Community as organic content and referral flywheel | Community-created content expanded the organic footprint and fuelled demand without a paid acquisition push | Michelle Goodall, BetterMode |
| Notion | Community + influencer growth | Grew from ~1M to ~30M users through community and creator enablement | Ben Lang (former Head of Community), Passionfroot AMA |
| CMX-studied companies | Dedicated community program | +21% higher revenue growth vs companies without one | CMX community study, 2022 |

The transferable pattern, not the scale: **find where your users already
convene, enable your superfans early, and treat community as an ecosystem
that feeds content, referrals, and product feedback.** Notion went to Reddit,
Discord, Facebook, and Meetup where its fans already lived, formalized the
natural ambassadors, and used the hub as both a product use case and a
knowledge base. The mechanism transfers. The 20M-user scale does not: Notion
had a category-defining product and category-defining timing.

## Objections and Edge Cases

| Objection / situation | What it means | How to answer |
|---|---|---|
| "Community takes too long to pay" | True by design | Run it alongside outbound or paid while it matures. It is the compounding asset, not the quarter's pipe |
| "We'll look dead with two people talking" | The group has no seed | Seed 3–5 genuine questions a week before you open the doors. Nobody talks into an empty room |
| "Nobody will speak, 90-9-1 proves it" | Lurking is normal and healthy | Lurkers consume value and convert downstream. Engineer for the 1% who create, and route the 90% to trial |
| "Community will become a support queue" | You are the only helper | Get your best customers answering. If you stay the only answerer, you built a ticket system |
| "We can't justify the headcount" | Community is understaffed by default | Start with a founder or one operator and phase 0–1 only. Scale staff when referral share proves the value |
| "How do we tie it to revenue" | Attribution is the hard part | Instrument the member-to-trial touch from day one and track community-influenced pipeline, not joins |
| "Our product has no craft to discuss" | Community is the wrong channel | Do not force it. Pick [Inbound](../channels/inbound) or [Product-Led Growth](../channels/product-led) instead |
| "Members keep leaving" | Value is not landing | The group is not useful independent of you. Fix the seed content and the welcome before you buy more members |

**The edge case that bites hardest:** a team launches a Slack group, buys
1,000 members, never seeds a conversation, measures nothing, and concludes
community failed. That was never community: it was a broadcast feed with
permissions. Community is the members talking to each other, and you can
measure whether that happens.

## Failure Modes and Fixes

- **Ghost town.** Nobody talks, so nobody returns. Fix: seed genuine
  conversations before you open the doors, and keep seeding until members
  answer each other. A community dies of silence faster than of anything
  else.
- **Vanity metrics.** You report membership count and post volume, not
  behavior and revenue. Fix: score active-member share, referral share, and
  community-influenced pipeline. Bodies are decoration.
- **No revenue tie.** The group is lively but produces nothing you can
  attribute. Fix: instrument the member-to-trial touch and track
  community-influenced pipeline from the first week, not after a year.
- **Moderation burn.** One person reads everything, works nights, and
  burns out. Fix: automate spam filtering, publish the rules, recruit and
  reward moderators from your best members, and give them clear authority.
- **Buying membership.** Paying people to join produces an inactive crowd
  that dilutes the signal and never refers. Fix: let membership grow from
  value and referral, and treat a paid join as a red flag.
- **You being the only helper.** Every question waits on you, so you build
  a ticket system and burn out. Fix: get your best customers answering and
  make the answer visible.
- **Faking it with a marketing channel.** A "community" that is a
  broadcast feed is a mailing list with extra steps. Fix: members talk, or
  it is not a community.
- **Expecting a spike.** Community compounds quarterly, not weekly. Fix:
  set the expectation that near-term pipe comes from other channels while
  the flywheel forms.

## Handoff to Sales and CS: Where Community Dies or Compounds

Most community programs fail the handoff, then blame the channel. Fix the
handoff before you scale it:

1. **Define the qualified community handoff in writing.** Write down who
   counts: active member + a budget or buying signal + expressed intent.
   If you cannot write it, the handoff is a lottery.
2. **Route on community signal, not on join.** Sales should touch members
   who have demonstrated intent, not every new join. A member who asked a
   buying question, hit a usage threshold, or engaged with pricing content
   is the signal.
3. **Track the source.** Every meeting, trial, and expansion must trace
   back to the community touch that generated it. No source, no scale.
4. **Debrief every two weeks.** Which member behavior predicted a deal,
   which objection killed the close, which ambassador drove the most
   referrals. Feed the answer back into the seeding and the ambassador
   program.
5. **Hand the active base to CS, not just sales.** The community is a
   retention asset. Give CS the engaged-member list and the expansion
   intent, and measure NRR among community members against non-members.

**The rule:** the community keeps winning the top of the funnel, and sales
and CS convert the members the behavior has already qualified. When sales
starts working every join, you have stopped being community-led and become
a lead-gen list that happens to have chat.

## Agentic Layer: the SOP That Runs the Flywheel

Community is where agents earn their keep on the *seeding, moderation, and
routing* side, because the volume of messages is enormous and the per-message
attention budget is near zero. The full operating model is on [Agentic
GTM](../agentic/agentic-gtm-overview). The reusable shape:

| Motion step | Agent does | Human approves |
|---|---|---|
| Seed content | Drafts 3–5 threads a week on the questions your ICP actually asks, with a source and a named base | The thread list and the drafts before they post |
| Moderate | Flags spam, toxicity, and rule breaks against the published policy, then surfaces flagged items to a human | The moderation policy once, and every ban or high-stakes action |
| Route signals | Watches for member buying and expansion behavior, attaches it to the account, routes qualified members to sales or CS | The routing policy, not every handoff |
| Track pipeline | Attributes every member-to-trial touch and reports community-influenced pipeline and referral share | The definitions and the weekly report before it goes to leadership |
| Nurture ambassadors | Identifies rising contributors, drafts recognition and onboarding for the ambassador program, tracks their output | The ambassador criteria and the recognition play |

**The one-prompt starter for running a community agent** (adapt to your
stack):

```
You are running the community flywheel for {company}, a {category} for
{ICP}. Each week:
1. From the forum and chat data, list the 5 questions members asked that
   our docs do not answer. For the top 3, draft a seed thread that names
   the question, the best current answer, and an invitation for members to
   add theirs. Skip questions with no buyer intent.
2. Flag any new thread that breaks the moderation policy or any reply
   that is spam or abusive. Do not act on the flag. Surface it for a human.
3. Watch member activity for buying and expansion signals: a member who
   asked a pricing or evaluation question, hit a usage threshold, or
   engaged with our product links. For each, attach the evidence to the
   account record and mark it for the sales or CS queue.
4. Report the week's numbers: new members, active-member share, threads,
   questions answered by members, referrals attributed, and
   community-influenced pipeline. Never fabricate a member count or a
   revenue figure.
Return a weekly report: threads seeded, flags raised, members routed, and
the one funnel stage with the largest leak.
```

**Three guardrails that keep it honest** (from [Guardrails &amp;
Measurement](../agentic/guardrails-and-measurement)):

1. **Signal coverage is complete.** If the agent misses a member's buying
   signal because you never instrumented the touch, the routing is fake.
   Instrument the member-to-trial touch before you automate.
2. **Human approval on the policy, not every action.** Approve the
   moderation policy and the routing policy once, let the agent execute,
   and review outcomes weekly. Never let an agent ban a member or send a
   sales handoff unread.
3. **Never sell to passive members at scale.** An agent that routes every
   join to sales turns a community into a lead-gen spray. The buying
   signal stays the gate.

**Track community-sourced pipeline by cohort, not joins.** A member is a
start, not a customer. Referral share and community-influenced revenue are
the scores, and they are the numbers that survive a budget review.
