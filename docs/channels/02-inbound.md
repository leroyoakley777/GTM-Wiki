# Inbound

**Inbound is the compounding channel — the one that pays you back in quarter four for content you published in quarter one.** It is the right primary channel when your buyers search, read, and evaluate on their own timeline. It is slower to start than outbound and slower to stop than paid, and that is exactly its value: inbound builds an asset you own.

This page is the *where* of inbound — when it is the right call and how to judge the funnel. It pairs with the [GTM Strategy Framework](../foundations/gtm-strategy-framework) for positioning, and with the [Funnel benchmarks](../data/funnel) for the leakage math.

---

## One Number That Matters Most

**AI-referral traffic converts at ~5.8% — above organic search (4.9%), email (4.9%), and comparable to paid search (5.4%).** The reason is not that AI is a magic channel; it is that the AI pre-qualifies the visitor before it sends the click. This is the single most important shift in inbound today: buyers do their research with an AI assistant, then arrive already further along in the buying process. 37% of marketers say leads are more informed thanks to AI, and nearly 70% say leads now come later in the buying process after AI-assisted research.

---

## Is Inbound Even the Right Channel?

Inbound wins when your buyers have a research-driven journey and a category they will actively search or ask about. It is the wrong channel when the buying problem is so novel that nobody is searching for it yet.

- **There is search volume or an active question.** Buyers must be asking something your content can answer. If you are inventing the category, inbound will starve while you wait.
- **You can sustain a content cadence.** Inbound rewards consistency over brilliance. A blog post a week for a year beats three brilliant posts and silence.
- **You are willing to be patient.** Inbound is the compounding channel. Expect the first meaningful organic lift in months, not weeks. Run it alongside a faster channel (outbound or partnerships) while it matures.

## Inbound Funnel — Benchmarks That Diagnose

The inbound funnel is a leaky set of stages. Each one compounds on the previous, so a small improvement upstream is worth more than a big improvement downstream.

| Stage | Benchmark | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Organic visitor → lead | **1–3% B2B SaaS** (median ~1.9%) | Does your content turn readers into known leads? |
| Lead → MQL | **~40%** | Scoring quality and form design |
| MQL → SQL | **~38%** | Fit of the leads being produced |
| SQL → opportunity | **~44%** | Sales qualification |
| Opportunity → customer | **~36%** | Close rate on qualified pipeline |

**The compounding reality:** a blended B2B SaaS funnel converts ~1.4% of visitors to leads, then ~2–4% of leads to closed customers. That sounds dismal — until each deal is worth tens of thousands in ACV. The funnel is not a defect; it is the shape of the channel. The leverage is upstream: every +0.5–1.0 point of visitor-to-lead conversion compounds all the way down.

The most-cited single number, "the average B2B conversion rate is ~5%", is a trap — it depends entirely on what is being counted. A pure visitor-to-lead rate on a B2B SaaS site is ~1.4–1.9%; a blended form/call/chat rate across industries is ~5%. They answer different questions. Know which one you are looking at.

## How Inbound Compounds

- **Website, blog, and SEO remain the #1 ROI channel** for B2B — ahead of paid social and every other line. 74% of marketers say content marketing generated demand/leads; 62% say it nurtured them.
- **Every +0.5–1.0 point of conversion compounds pipeline and reduces CAC.** This is why CRO is the second-most-used optimization technique among marketers — improving conversion on traffic you already own is cheaper than buying more traffic.
- **Email on the back end is a compounding multiplier.** Email converts at ~4.9% (Ruler Analytics) and is a top ROI channel for B2B. Capture the email, then let it do the long-game nurturing that single blog visits cannot.
- **You own the asset.** Unlike paid, inbound traffic is not rented. The content, the rankings, and the email list keep paying after the campaign ends.

---

## How to Read These Numbers

1. **Match the metric to the question.** Visitor-to-lead tells you about content + CRO. MQL-to-SQL tells you about fit and scoring. Opportunity-to-customer tells you about sales. Do not diagnose the whole channel from one stage.
2. **Fix upstream first.** Because the funnel multiplies, a 1-point lift at visitor-to-lead is worth more than a 1-point lift at opportunity-to-customer. When in doubt, work on traffic quality and conversion before sales-process polish.
3. **Plan for the AI-assisted buyer.** Leads arrive later and more informed. Content must answer the deep questions AI assistants skip, and sales must handle a buyer who has already done the homework.

---

## Pattern Behind Inbound

Inbound is the only major channel that compounds because it builds an owned asset. The pattern that wins is boring and repeatable: consistent content aimed at real buyer questions, captured on your site, converted with a clear next step, and nurtured by email until the buyer is ready to talk. The channel rewards teams that treat content as infrastructure rather than as campaign bursts.
