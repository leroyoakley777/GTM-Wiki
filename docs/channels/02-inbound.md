# Inbound

Decide whether inbound deserves your energy, then run it as a
compounding engine rather than a content calendar that never pays out.
This page covers the whole surface: when inbound is the right call
(including the AI-assistant shift that changed the rules), the funnel
with named dated benchmarks, the AI-referral and organic numbers that
now matter, content strategy and cadence, tools by role, a first-90-days
sequence, an SMB vs enterprise case matrix, objections and edge cases,
failure modes with fixes, the handoff to sales, and the agentic SOP that
keeps the machine running. Every figure carries a named source and an
"as of" date.

Inbound is the compounding channel: it pays you back in quarter four for
content you published in quarter one. It starts slower than
[outbound](../channels/outbound) and takes longer to stop than paid, and
that is exactly its value: it builds an owned asset (rankings, an email
list, citations) that keeps earning after the campaign ends.

**Read the companion pages first:** the [Channels
Bullseye](../foundations/gtm-channels-bullseye) tells you whether
inbound is your one best channel. [GTM Strategy
Framework](../foundations/gtm-strategy-framework) positions the offer
this channel sells. [Funnel
Benchmarks](../data/funnel) and [Conversion Rate
Benchmarks](../data/conversion-rate-benchmarks) carry the full stage
tables this page points at. [Agentic
Outbound](../agentic/agentic-outbound) is the same ideas on the outbound
side, if that is where the use sits instead.

## One number that matters most

**AI-referral traffic converts at ~5.8%, above organic search (4.9%),
email (4.9%), and roughly even with paid search (5.4%)**, per Ruler
Analytics, conversion benchmarks across 5M+ conversions and 13
industries (2026). An AI referral is a click an assistant already
researched and vetted before it sent the visitor. The visitor arrives
further along the buying process, which is why the rate runs high.

That single number is the read on where inbound is heading. Buyers do
their research with an assistant, then arrive more informed and later in
the journey. 37% of marketers say leads are more informed thanks to AI,
and nearly 70% say leads now come later in the buying process after
AI-assisted research (HubSpot, State of Marketing). The job of inbound
is no longer to seen by everyone. It is to be the answer the
assistant chooses to cite.

## When inbound is the right channel

Inbound wins when your buyers follow a research-driven journey and sit
in a category they will actively search, read, or ask about. It is the
wrong call when the problem is so new that nobody is looking for it yet.

The three conditions that make inbound the right primary channel:

- **There is search volume or a live question.** Buyers must be asking
  something your content can answer. If you are inventing the category,
  inbound starves while you wait for demand that is not there.
- **You can sustain a cadence.** Inbound rewards consistency over
  brilliance. A post a week for a year beats three brilliant posts and
  silence. Cadence is the strategy. Individual pieces are the tactics.
- **You can be patient and pair it with a faster channel.** Expect the
  first meaningful organic lift in months, not weeks. Run inbound
  alongside outbound or paid while it matures, and let it take over as
  the primary source of pipeline later.

When inbound is the **wrong** call, with a hard edge:

- The buying problem is so novel that search and assistants return
  nothing useful. Outbound, community, or events (see [Events &
  Field](../channels/events-field)) build the category before inbound
  can.
- Your TAM is too small to justify the SEO cost. Content SEO is
  front-loaded and compounds, but only if the pool of searches is deep
  enough to pay for the writing.
- You have a short runway and need pipeline this quarter. Inbound will
  not deliver it. See [Outbound from
  Zero](../playbooks/outbound-from-zero) for the fast channel.

## AI-assistant buyer shift (the rule change)

This is the biggest change to inbound in a generation, and it reshapes
every number below. The trend lines, all named and dated:

- **Gartner (Feb 2024)** forecast traditional search-engine volume to
  drop 25% by 2026 as AI chatbots and virtual agents capture queries.
- **Bain & Company (Feb 2025)** measured organic web traffic down
  15–25% across sectors as answers move into zero-click surfaces.
- **Onely / ABM Agency (2025)** found 73% of B2B websites lost
  significant organic traffic between 2024 and 2025, with an average
  decline of 34% year over year.
- **Seer Interactive (Nov 2025)** analyzed 3,119 queries across 42
  organizations and measured a 61% drop in organic CTR on queries where
  Google AI Overviews appear, from 1.76% to 0.61%.
- **NP Digital (Oct 2025)** tracked B2B organic leads down 47% from
  January to October 2025, as 57% of B2B searches ended without a
  website click (up from 35% in 2024).

One counterpoint keeps the panic honest: **Graphite / Similarweb (2026)**
looked at the top 40,000 US sites and found Google organic traffic down
just 2.5% year over year. Broad aggregates hide a long-tail collapse.
The most defensible read is that the floor fell out of low-value,
informational queries that used to flood in, while brand and high-intent
search held. Inbound is not dead: the kind of inbound that wins changed.

What the shift means in practice:

1. **Impressions are worth less than being cited.** An assistant that
   answers the buyer's question in the chat window consumes the traffic
   a click would have carried. Winning now means getting your content
   quoted in the answer, not just ranked in a list.
2. **The visitors who arrive are worth more.** Because the assistant
   pre-qualified them, the residual click converts higher: the 5.8%
   AI-referral rate (Ruler, 2026) over 4.9% for organic.
3. **Deep answers beat broad articles.** Assistants cite the page that
   fully and structurally answers the question, not the one that fluffs
   around it. Content written for a person who already read the summary
   must add the detail the summary skipped.
4. **You must be findable by the assistant's sources.** Structured
   content, clear headers, factual specificity, and a reputation for
   accuracy: these are what make an assistant pick you over a
   competitor.

The strategic response is to push the same budget further up the
funnel, into the pages and formats an assistant actually reads and
trusts, while accepting lower raw traffic in exchange for higher
conversion per visitor.

## Inbound funnel, with named dated benchmarks

The funnel is a leaky production line. Each stage multiplies the one
above it, so a small upstream fix is worth more than a large downstream
one. These rates are aligned with the full tables on [Funnel
Benchmarks](../data/funnel) and [Conversion Rate
Benchmarks](../data/conversion-rate-benchmarks):

| Stage | Median / typical | Top quartile / good | Source (as of) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visitor → Lead | 1.4–2.6% (B2B SaaS ~1.4–1.9%) | 4.5% | Ruler Analytics 2024 (2.6% avg, 4.5% top quartile). First Page Sage 1.4% SMB. SalesHive 2026: 2.3% avg, ~2.9% median website |
| Lead → MQL | 20–41% | 40%+ | Landbase 2026 (31% avg, 39% B2B SaaS). First Page Sage 36–47% by industry |
| MQL → SQL | 13–21% | n/a | Salesforce State of Sales 2024 (13%). thedigitalbloom 2025 (15–21%). SalesHive 2026 (13%) |
| SQL → Opportunity | 30–59% | n/a | SalesHive 2026 (30–59%). Zeliq 30–50%. Ebsta/Pavilion 2024 (47%) |
| Opportunity → Customer | 20–35% | n/a | Ebsta/Pavilion 2024 (21% avg, down 4–6 points from 2021). Zeliq 20–35%. First Page Sage 31–43% |

**MQL → SQL is the largest, most consistent leak in B2B SaaS.** Three
independent sources land near the same point: Salesforce (13%, 2024),
SalesHive (13%, 2026), MarketJoy (15%, 2024–25). Fix qualification and
you protect every stage below it. Pouring more top-of-funnel volume into
a stage that leaks at 13% is buying more waste.

**The leakage math, worked out.** Take 10,000 visitors through the
mid-range rates (SalesHive, 2026):

| Stage | Rate | In | Out | Lost here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Visitor → Lead | 2.3% | 10,000 | ~230 leads | ~9,770 |
| Lead → MQL | 31% | 230 | ~71 MQLs | ~159 |
| MQL → SQL | 13% | 71 | ~9 SQLs | ~62 |
| SQL → Opportunity | 45% (mid of 30–59%) | 9 | ~4 opps | ~5 |
| Opportunity → Close | 26% (mid of 22–30%) | 4 | **~1 customer** | ~3 |

**10,000 visitors → roughly 1 customer.** That is the honest shape of
the channel, and it is fine: each deal is worth tens of thousands in
ACV. The 100 → 10 → 2 rule (SalesHive, 2026) is the planning tool: 100
marketing leads → ~10 opportunities → ~2 deals. Agree to that math with
sales before the quarter, and pipeline review stops being a fight about
"why so few customers" and becomes a search for which gate leaked.

### How to read each stage: healthy vs leaking

- **Visitor → Lead (2.3% avg, SalesHive 2026).** Healthy is 2–4% on
  your core segment, stable week over week. Below ~1.5%, or flat while
  traffic climbs, is a leak. A rising visitor count with a dropping
  conversion usually means the traffic mix changed (lower-intent sources
  added), not that the page broke. Fix positioning and page clarity
  before buying more traffic: doubling traffic on a 1% page doubles a
  leak.
- **MQL → SQL (13%, Salesforce 2024 / SalesHive 2026).** This is the
  gate. A high MQL count with a low SQL rate is the tell that your
  scoring rewards engagement over fit. Fix the definition of an MQL, not
  the scoring threshold.
- **Opportunity → Customer (21% avg, Ebsta/Pavilion 2024).** Below ~20%
  is a sales-process problem: discovery, value, or champion. Note this
  rate has fallen 4–6 points since 2021 as buyers consolidate vendors
  and committees grow.

**Read the full stage tables and the healthy-vs-leak reads on** [Funnel
Benchmarks](../data/funnel) **and the bowtie table on** [Conversion Rate
Benchmarks](../data/conversion-rate-benchmarks).

## AI-referral and organic benchmarks (the numbers that now matter)

Because the assistant now sits between buyer and site, the inbound
metrics split into "traffic you earn from search" and "clicks an
assistant sends you." Track both, and weight them by conversion.

### Conversion by source

| Source | Conversion rate | Source (as of) |
|---|---|---|
| AI referral | ~5.8% | Ruler Analytics (2026), 5M+ conversions, 13 industries |
| Organic search | ~4.9% | Ruler Analytics (2026) |
| Email | ~4.9% | Ruler Analytics (2026) |
| Paid search | ~5.4% | Ruler Analytics (2026) |

The lesson is not "abandon organic for AI." It is that a visitor who
arrives through an assistant is worth more than one who arrives through
search, so the same content budget should chase citation as hard as it
chases ranking.

### Cost math that justifies inbound

- **Content marketing / SEO CPL: ~$35 ($15–75).** Compare paid social at
  ~$152, display at ~$180, trade shows at ~$395 (The Starr Conspiracy,
  analysis of DashThis / Cognism / Klipfolio campaign data, 2025. Median B2B CPL across all channels ~$116).
- **Content generates 3x more leads than outbound and costs 62% less**
  (DemandSage, 2026).
- **SEO / organic runs ~$30 per qualified lead** (Zeliq, 2025), and the
  effective CPL keeps falling each quarter as content compounds (Zeliq,
  2025).
- SalesHive (2026) puts fully-loaded content/SEO CPL at $80–200 over
  time: the range widens because early spend is infrastructure before
  it is acquisition. CPL starts high and falls as rankings build.

### Organic ranking in practice

- **Less than 2% of new pages reach the Google top 10 within a year. **
  roughly 5% of pages that earn at least one backlink do (Ahrefs,
  Content Explorer study of 1B+ pages). Most pages never rank. The
  pages that win tend to be older and to hold backlinks.
- **Low-keyword-difficulty terms (KD under 25) often rank within 4–6
  weeks** on a fresh site (Ahrefs / SalesHive SEO guidance, 2026). That
  is where a new inbound program earns its first wins, not on the
  head terms your competitors have owned for years.
- **96.6% of Google clicks go to first-page results** (Ahrefs, 2026).
  There is no consolation prize for page two.

Strategy that follows from the numbers: win the long-tail, low-KD
queries in months 1–6 to build the traffic base, and write the deep,
structured, citable answers that assistants quote, because that is where
the conversion multiplier lives.

## Content strategy and cadence

Cadence is the whole game. A post a week for a year compounds. Three
brilliant posts and silence builds nothing that lasts.

### Cadence table (what "sustained" looks like)

| Asset | Cadence | Job | First payoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| SEO blog posts (long-tail, KD under 25) | 2–4 / month | Win low-KD queries, build topical authority | 4–6 weeks (Ahrefs/SalesHive, 2026) |
| Pillar / cluster content | 1 / quarter | Own a theme, feed the cluster with posts | 3–6 months |
| Deep citable answers (the AI referral bait) | 1–2 / month | Get quoted by assistants, convert at 5.8% | 2–4 months |
| Comparison / alternatives content | 1 / month | Capture high-intent "X vs Y" searches | 2–3 months |
| Email nurture | weekly | Convert the captured lead into a meeting | immediate on leads |

### Rules that make content convert

1. **Capture the email on every asset.** A visitor who leaves without
   leaving an address is a number with no future. Every post needs one
   clear next step: a guide download, a template, a webinar signup.
   Email on the back end is the compounding multiplier: it converts at
   ~4.9% (Ruler, 2026) and is a top ROI channel for B2B (HubSpot, State
   of Marketing).
2. **Write for the post-AI reader.** Your page will often be read after
   the assistant already gave the summary. Add the depth, the caveats,
   the worked examples, and the specifics the summary skipped. That is
   what earns the citation and the residual click.
3. **Answer the question fully and structurally.** Clear headers, a
   direct answer up top, factual specifics with dates and sources. This
   is what assistants extract from and trust.
4. **Bottom-funnel content converts.** Comparison pages, alternatives
   pages, and "how to solve problem X with or without a tool" capture
   buyers already evaluating. Publish them early, not as an afterthought.
5. **Repurpose relentlessly.** One deep piece becomes a post, a
   newsletter, a LinkedIn thread, a slide deck. The marginal cost of
   each repurposed asset is near zero.

### Where website, blog, and SEO sit

Website, blog, and SEO remain the **#1 ROI channel for B2B**, ahead of
paid social and every other line (HubSpot, State of Marketing). 74% of
marketers say content marketing generated demand and leads. 62% say it
nurtured them (HubSpot). CRO is the second-most-used optimization
technique among marketers precisely because improving conversion on
traffic you already own is cheaper than buying more traffic.

## Tools by role (a stack that works end to end)

Inbound tooling spans a few layers. This is one working stack, with what
each tool does and how to wire it.

| Role | Tool (this guide) | What it does | How to wire it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content / marketing lead | CMS (Webflow / WordPress / a docs site) + Google Search Console | Hosts content, shows what search already sends you | Publish through it, connect Search Console to Analytics to see which pages earn which traffic |
| SEO | Ahrefs (or Semrush) | Keyword research, KD scores, content-gap analysis, rank tracking | Target KD under 25 in months 1–6. Run a content-gap analysis against competitors to build the roadmap |
| Analytics | GA4 + Search Console | Traffic, conversions by source, per-page performance | Tag every form and CTA as an event. Split AI-referral out from organic |
| CRO | VWO / Optimizely (or PostHog) + a heatmap tool | A/B test pages, see where visitors drop | Test the highest-traffic pages first. Fix one page at a time |
| Form / capture | Typeform / a native form + a newsletter tool | Turn readers into known leads | One clear CTA per asset, a working email capture, instant confirm |
| Email nurture | A marketing automation platform (HubSpot, Klaviyo, Loops) | Sequences, drip, re-engagement | Route every captured lead into a nurture track keyed to the asset they downloaded |
| Routing / SLA | A revenue platform (HubSpot / Salesforce) + routing rules | Assign leads to SDRs, time-stamp response | Alert SDRs within minutes of a high-intent form. Enforce the response SLA below |
| AI content layer | A research+writing agent (or your LLM stack) with human approval | Drafts briefs, drafts and repurposes content, answers the deep questions | Agent drafts from a research brief. A human approves before publish. Never publish agent output unread |

**The wiring rule:** every layer must hand off cleanly. Analytics feeds
CRO decisions, capture feeds email, email feeds routing, routing feeds
sales. A tool that holds data in a silo is a bottleneck. See [Agentic
Stack](../agentic/agentic-stack) for the automation wiring at depth.

## First 90 days: the sequence

Inbound has a ramp, not a sprint. Here is the month-by-month build that
gets a compounding engine running without burning out the writer.

**Month 1: foundation.**
1. Audit what already ranks in Search Console. Find the low-KD, high-relevance terms in your space with Ahrefs.
2. Fix positioning and the home page / one conversion path first (see [Positioning & Messaging](../foundations/positioning-and-messaging)). A leaky page poisons everything downstream.
3. Publish 2–4 long-tail SEO posts. Set up email capture on every one.
4. Stand up the email nurture track.
5. Gate: every post has a working capture and a clear next step.

**Month 2: cadence and clusters.**
1. Publish 4 SEO posts + 1 deep citable answer + 1 comparison page.
2. Build a pillar/cluster structure around one theme.
3. Run the first CRO tests on the highest-traffic pages.
4. Watch for the first low-KD rankings (4–6 week window, Ahrefs/SalesHive 2026). Measure visitor → lead on the new pages.
5. Gate: two pages converting above your visitor → lead median (2–4%, SalesHive 2026).

**Month 3: double down.**
1. See which posts gained traction. Write the sequels and internal links to deepen them.
2. Add a second cluster if the first shows life.
3. Route inbound leads into the sales handoff with the response SLA (below).
4. Measure the full funnel stage by stage against the table above. Fix the worst leak before adding volume.
5. Gate: at least one page ranking top 10, and a visitor → lead rate you can quote.

At month 6, revisit: inbound should be a measurable share of pipeline,
not a content treadmill. If traffic is up and pipeline is flat, the leak
is downstream (qualification or handoff), not the writing.

## Case matrix: SMB vs enterprise, first 3 months vs mature

Inbound behaves differently by company size and by maturity. This matrix
keeps expectations honest.

| | SMB (ACV ~$1–5k, self-serve / low-touch) | Enterprise (ACV $50k+, committee sale) |
|---|---|---|
| **First 3 months** | Goal: low-KD long-tail rankings + an email list. Expect modest traffic, near-zero pipeline. Winner: bottom-funnel "how to / alternatives" content. Measure visitor → lead and list growth. | Goal: category authority + high-intent capture. Expect slow. Sales cycles make pipeline long. Winner: comparison and "how we think about X" content that reaches the champion early. Measure demo requests and champion content. |
| **Mature (12+ months)** | Goal: scale the list and the free-to-paid path. Inbound can be 40–60% of new pipeline. Winner: email nurture + lifecycle content. Watch CAC fall every quarter as rankings compound. | Goal: inbound as trust layer feeding a longer, heavier sale. Inbound typically 20–40% of pipeline. The rest from ABM and outbound. Winner: analyst-grade depth, case studies, proof. Measure influenced pipeline, not just touched. |

The one constant across both: **inbound compounds only if you stay on
cadence.** The enterprise motion takes longer to pay and is harder to
attribute, but it wins on deal value per visitor. The SMB motion pays
sooner and shows in volume. Both die the same way: burst publishing and
no capture.

## Objections and edge cases

| "Inbound won't work because…" | The honest answer |
|---|---|
| "Nobody searches for our category yet." | Then inbound is the wrong channel today. Outbound, community, or events build the category first. Revisit inbound when the questions exist. |
| "AI killed our SEO traffic." | The long-tail floor fell out (NP Digital, Oct 2025: B2B organic leads −47%). But AI-referral converts higher (5.8%, Ruler 2026). Pivot the same budget to being the cited answer, not just ranked. |
| "Content takes too long to pay." | True. Run inbound alongside outbound or paid while it matures, and let it take over. Do not drop it at month 2 when outbound is printing. |
| "We can't publish a post a week." | Then publish 2 a month, but never miss a month. Consistency beats volume. Or use an agent to draft from research briefs and repurpose one deep piece across formats. |
| "Our buyers don't read blogs." | They read comparisons, analyst content, and proof. Publish the formats your buyer actually consumes, not a blog for its own sake. |
| "Every keyword is too competitive." | Target KD under 25 long-tail terms (rank in 4–6 weeks, Ahrefs/SalesHive 2026). Win the tail, then build authority toward the head. |
| "We have no budget for SEO tools." | Search Console and GA4 are free. Start with those, manual capture, and your own newsletter. Add paid tools only when you can act on what they would tell you. |

## Failure modes and fixes

- **Bursts instead of cadence.** Three great posts and silence builds
  nothing. Fix: set a calendar you will not break, even if output drops.
- **Publishing without capture.** Visitors leave with nothing and are
  gone forever. Fix: one clear next step and a working email form on
  every asset.
- **No email back end.** Traffic in, no nurture, no compounding. Fix:
  stand up the email track in month 1, not month 6.
- **Measuring traffic, not pipeline.** A traffic spike that never
  converts is a party, not a channel. Fix: score on visitor → lead and
  pipeline influenced, not pageviews.
- **Optimizing bottom-funnel while the top leaks.** Polishing the close
  while visitor → lead sits at 1% ignores the multiplicative math. Fix:
  fix the worst leak first, which is usually MQL → SQL (13%, Salesforce
  2024).
- **Ignoring the AI shift.** Chasing zero-click-immune volume while
  assistants take the long tail. Fix: publish deep citable answers and
  structure content to be quoted.
- **Slow lead follow-up.** A lead answered in an hour is already cold.
  Fix: the SLA below.
- **No attribution.** You cannot tell which asset, cluster, or source
  drove the deal, so you cannot scale the winner. Fix: tag every source
  and route through revenue platform reporting.
- **Treating a 1.4% visitor → lead rate as failure.** It is near the
  B2B SaaS median (First Page Sage). It is the shape of the channel:
  the use is upstream and downstream, not panic.

## Handoff to sales

The handoff is where inbound dies most often. A captured lead that no
one calls back within a day is a wasted asset.

- **Speed to lead is the biggest lever.** Responding to a new lead
  within 5 minutes makes you ~10x more likely to make contact than
  waiting an hour, and ~21x more likely to qualify it (SalesHive, 2026,
  aggregating the lead-response research). Route high-intent inbound
  leads to an SDR within minutes: this is where the agentic layer pays
  for itself.
- **Define the meeting standard in writing.** Who counts as a qualified
  inbound meeting? If you cannot say, "inbound meetings" is meaningless.
- **Track the source.** Every deal must trace back to a source and,
  ideally, a piece of content. No source, no scaling the winner.
- **Debrief every 2 weeks.** Which asset produced leads, which leads
  qualified, which objection killed the deal. Feed the answer back into
  content.
- **Define MQL vs SQL against the funnel table.** A high MQL count with
  a low SQL rate means scoring rewards engagement over fit (the 13%
  gate, Salesforce 2024). Align marketing and sales on the definitions
  before arguing about the numbers.

The inbound motion is a loop, not a line: content → capture → nurture →
handoff → close → case study → the next piece of content that wins the
next buyer.

## Agentic layer (the SOP)

Inbound is where agents remove the throughput bottleneck (research,
drafting, repurposing, triage, and routing), while a human keeps
judgment and approval. The rule is the same as outbound: **the gate
stays, the execution layer changes.** See [Guardrails &
Measurement](../agentic/guardrails-and-measurement) for the full
measurement treatment.

| Motion step | Agent does | Human approves |
|---|---|---|
| Keyword / gap research | Surfaces low-KD terms, runs content-gap analysis, prioritizes against ranking potential | Signs off on the roadmap |
| Content drafting | Drafts from a research brief. Structures headers. Writes the deep answers | Edits and approves before any publish |
| Repurposing | Spins one deep piece into post, newsletter, thread, deck | Approves the pattern, not every keystroke |
| Capture → nurture | Routes every captured lead into the right nurture track, personalizes email | Sets the nurture strategy |
| Lead triage / routing | Classifies intent, routes high-intent leads to SDRs within minutes, drafts first touch | Approves the hot-lead SLA and messaging |
| Reporting | Watches the funnel stage by stage, flags the worst leak weekly | Reviews the leak, decides the fix |

### One-prompt starter for running inbound

Adapt this to your stack. It encodes the whole play as an SOP:

```
You are running an inbound engine for {company}, a {category} serving
{ICP}. Each week:
1. From {keyword tool} and {analytics}, list the top 10 low-KD terms
   (KD under 25) with existing ranking potential. Skip terms with no
   buyer intent behind them.
2. For the top 3, write a one-line angle and a research brief that
   names the buyer, the question, and the deep answer an AI assistant
   would quote.
3. Draft one piece per brief: a direct answer up top, structured
   headers, worked specifics with dates and sources, and one clear
   call to action that captures an email.
4. Repurpose each piece into a newsletter section and a LinkedIn
   thread.
5. Flag any page converting below {your visitor-to-lead target} for a
   CRO review. Report the funnel stages with numbers. Never fabricate
   traffic, ranking, or conversion figures.
Return a weekly report: terms targeted, pieces published, captures,
and the funnel stage with the largest leak.
```

### Guardrails that keep it honest

1. **Human approval before publish.** An agent-drafted piece shipped
   unread is how a brand publishes noise and an AI assistant stops
   citing it. Drafts are drafts.
2. **Brief coverage over volume.** If most pieces lack a grounded
   research brief, the content is filler, and buyers and assistants
   both notice.
3. **Report funnel, not output.** Pageviews and posts published are
   vanity. The score is pipeline influenced and cost per customer.
4. **Keep the citation bar high.** Structured, factual, sourced answers
   earn the AI referrals that convert at 5.8% (Ruler, 2026). Fabricated
   or shallow content kills the exact advantage that makes inbound work
   now.
