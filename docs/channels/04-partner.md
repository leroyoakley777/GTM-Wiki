# Partner / Channel

**Partnerships let another company's sales team and distribution sell your
product.** Done right, it is the cheapest pipeline you will ever source.
Done badly, it is a calendar full of partner calls that never close a deal.

This page is the *where* of partnerships: when the channel is the right
call, the four partner types, the comp model that makes a partner's effort
worth their time, how to source and onboard partners, the benchmarks that
tell you the motion is healthy, and the failure modes that kill it. Every
number carries a named source and an "as of" date.

**Read the companion pages first:** the [Channels
Bullseye](../foundations/gtm-channels-bullseye) tells you *which* channel
deserves your energy. [Referral & Viral](./referral-viral) is the
customer-as-distributor cousin of this page. [Product-Led
Growth](./product-led) pairs with the integration-partner motion.

---

## One Number That Matters Most

**Qualified partner-sourced pipeline as a share of new pipeline: a healthy
target is 10–20% for a mature program.** That is the number that tells you
the motion is contributing and not just occupying time.

Context for what "healthy" means in the wild:

- The **category median** for partner-sourced revenue in B2B SaaS sits at
  **24%**, and **top-quartile companies exceed 40%** ([SaaS Mag](https://www.saasmag.com/ecosystem-led-growth-saas-revenue-engine/), 2026).
- **Mid-market and enterprise companies report 35% of new pipeline is
  partner-influenced or sourced** ([PartnerStack Research Lab,
  2026](https://partnerstack.com/resources/research-lab/charts/partner-influenced-revenue-varies-across-companies-with-larger-companies-seeing-more-pipeline-from-partnerships)).
- **69% of B2B SaaS leaders plan to increase investment in partnerships**
  ([PartnerStack, State of Partnerships in GTM
  2026](https://partnerstack.com/resources/research-lab/the-state-of-partnerships-in-gtm-2026)).

Do not track "number of partners signed" as a score. Signed but inactive
partners are decoration. Partner-sourced pipeline is revenue.

## Is Partnerships Even the Right Channel?

Run the [Channels Bullseye](../foundations/gtm-channels-bullseye) and pick
your one best channel before you add partnerships. Partnerships win when
**all three** are true:

- **Your product complements an existing tool the customer already buys.**
  The natural partner is the product sitting next to yours in their stack.
  They get stickier, you get their distribution.
- **There is a clear integration, referral, or co-sell motion.** A partner
  you cannot integrate with, reference, or co-sell is a logo, not a channel.
- **The partner's customer base overlaps your ICP.** If the ICPs do not
  overlap, there is no deal at the end of the partnership.

If any of those is false, partnerships will consume energy for zero
pipeline. A partnership with no shared customer is a friendship, not a
channel.

**The timing gate:** partnerships pay off after product-market fit, when the
product already has distribution and a market the partner can amplify. A
pre-PMF product with no proof has nothing a partner can sell. Do direct
first. See the [GTM Strategy
Framework](../foundations/gtm-strategy-framework) for the sequencing.

## Four Partner Types

Each type is a different job with a different comp model and a different
definition of activation. Most failed partnerships are a category error:
they expect co-sell economics from a tech partnership with no shared
customer, or they treat an integration as if it were a reseller.

### 1. Integration / technology partners

The partner builds on top of you (or you on them), and mutual customers get
a better product. Pipeline shows up as integration-driven signups and as
the partner's sales team selling the joint story. This is the motion behind
most product-led virality and ecosystem-led growth.

- Comp is usually **non-monetary at first**: mutual product value, joint
  co-marketing, a featured listing on each other's integration marketplace.
- Some programs attach a small revenue-share to the MRR the integration
  drives. The integration is not the win. The shared customer is.
- Measure **integration-driven attach**: how many mutual customers take both
  products. See [Product-Led Growth](./product-led).

### 2. Referral partners

A partner sends you leads or customers in exchange for a commission on the
deals that close. No reselling, no delivery burden. The partner earns for
the introduction, you keep the customer relationship.

- **Wharton research found referral leads convert at roughly 30% higher
  rate and carry about 16% higher lifetime value than leads from other
  channels** (cited via [Expando, 2025](https://www.expando.ai/blogs/guide-to-b2b-referral-partner-programs)).
- Referral and affiliate programs are the same economics. B2B SaaS affiliate
  programs contribute **10–20% of MRR, up to 50% for specialized tools**
  ([Rewardful, SaaS Affiliate Benchmarks,
  2025](https://www.rewardful.com/articles/saas-affiliate-program-benchmarks)).

### 3. Resellers

A partner buys your product and resells it to their book of business. This
is an indirect sales force. It only works when the partner has the
relationships **and** the margin makes the effort worth their time, which
means your product needs enough price headroom to share.

- **SaaS channel partner margins run 20–30%** as a useful guideline
  ([DealHub](https://dealhub.io/glossary/partner-margin/), 2026).
- Reseller commission ranges hold **10–40%**, with mid-size B2B SaaS most
  often at 20–30%.
- Resellers carry the sales cost, so your gross margin must survive the
  discount. Thin-margin products do not survive reselling.

### 4. Channel / distribution partners

Distributors and cloud-marketplace partners. The partner owns a category,
a geography, or a marketplace (AWS, Salesforce AppExchange, HubSpot
ecosystem) and lists or transacts your product there.

- Marketplace partners typically take a **15–25%** cut of transactions plus
  their fee.
- These are the deepest pipelines and the slowest to stand up. Treat a
  marketplace listing as a compounding channel, not a quick win.

**The category error to avoid:** an integration partner is not a reseller.
Expect co-sell volume from an integration that no customer asked for and you
will be disappointed twice: in pipeline and in morale.

## Deal and Comp Model: What Makes It Worth Their Time

Comp is the whole game. A partner has their own quota, their own customers,
their own priorities. Your product is one of a hundred things competing for
their attention. The comp model decides how much attention they give it.

| Model | What it pays | Typical rate | Source (as of) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Referral / affiliate commission | One-time or first-year MRR share | 20% of first-year value | [HubSpot Upmarket Referral, 2026](https://www.hubspot.com/solutions-partners-tiers-and-benefits-2026) |
| Revenue share on recurring | % of monthly recurring net revenue | 20% of MRR, paid up to 3 years | [HubSpot Solutions Partner policy, 2026](https://www.hubspot.com/partners/solutions-program-policies) |
| Reseller margin | Discount off list price | 20–30% | [DealHub, 2026](https://dealhub.io/glossary/partner-margin/) |
| Marketplace / distributor fee | Cut of each transaction | 15–25% | AWS / Salesforce AppExchange norms |
| Non-monetary | Co-marketing, listings, referrals | $0 cash | Standard for early integrations |

**The rules that make comp work:**

- **Pay for outcome, not for the introduction.** Tie commission to
  closed-won revenue, not to leads submitted. A partner who sends leads that
  never close is burning your pipeline hygiene.
- **One source of truth on attribution.** Decide before the first deal how
  a partner gets credit. Only **42% of companies use multi-touch
  attribution** across the funnel: the rest guess, and guessing breaks
  trust ([PartnerStack, 2026](https://partnerstack.com/resources/research-lab/the-state-of-partnerships-in-gtm-2026)). Attribution splits in the wild: **multi-touch 42%,
  first-touch 31%, last-touch 19%, no tracking 8%** (same report).
- **Do not discount for a partner who brings no deals.** If you cut your
  price for a partner with zero pipeline, you have only cut your own margin.
- **Tie comp to partner tier.** Partners who co-sell and close earn more
  than partners who only refer. Differentiate or you pay the same for very
  different work.

## Partnership Funnel: Benchmarks That Diagnose

Each stage answers a different question. A weak stage caps the whole
motion, and the order matters: activation before adoption, adoption before
co-sell, co-sell before pipeline share. Optimizing pipeline share while
partners are not even launched is measuring noise.

| Stage | Healthy range | Source (as of) | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| Partner activation rate | **>50%** of signed partners launch | [Hamster Garage, 2026](https://www.hamstergarage.com/article/affiliate-agency-onboarding-playbook) | Whether onboarding worked |
| Partner-sourced revenue share | **10–20%** healthy (24% median, 40% top-quartile) | [SaaS Mag, 2026](https://www.saasmag.com/ecosystem-led-growth-saas-revenue-engine/) | Whether the channel matters |
| Referral conversion lift | **~30% higher** than other channels | [Wharton via Expando, 2025](https://www.expando.ai/blogs/guide-to-b2b-referral-partner-programs) | Whether referral leads are worth pursuing |
| Affiliate / referral MRR contribution | **10–20%** of MRR (up to 50% specialized) | [Rewardful, 2025](https://www.rewardful.com/articles/saas-affiliate-program-benchmarks) | Whether referral economics hold |
| Partner-influenced share of new pipeline | **~35%** (mid-market/enterprise) | [PartnerStack, 2026](https://partnerstack.com/resources/research-lab/charts/partner-influenced-revenue-varies-across-companies-with-larger-companies-seeing-more-pipeline-from-partnerships) | What a strong program reaches |
| Cost per acquired lead | **30–40% lower** via partnerships than paid ads | [Genesys Growth, 2026](https://genesysgrowth.com/blog/customer-acquisition-cost-benchmarks-for-marketing-leaders) | The core economic argument |

**The activation number matters most.** Most affiliate programs see only
**~10% activation**, while top programs with strong onboarding reach **50%
or higher** ([Hamster Garage,
2026](https://www.hamstergarage.com/article/affiliate-agency-onboarding-playbook)). And a program sitting at **30% activation has a recruitment
problem or an onboarding-design problem: most of those partners should
never have been signed** ([PartnerStandard](https://pro.partnerstandard.com/guides/partner-onboarding)).

## Why Partnerships Compound (When They Work)

- **The partner sells without your cost.** Their sales team, their
  relationships, their quota. Comp converts your fixed sales cost into a
  variable one paid only on closed deals.
- **Trust transfers.** Referral leads convert at ~30% higher rates because
  the buyer already trusts the referrer ([Wharton via Expando,
  2025](https://www.expando.ai/blogs/guide-to-b2b-referral-partner-programs)).
- **Sales cycles shorten.** **48% of respondents say alignment with partners
  shortens sales cycles, and 23% say it helps land bigger deals**
  ([PartnerStack, 2026](https://partnerstack.com/resources/research-lab/the-state-of-partnerships-in-gtm-2026)).
- **Top reps already lean on it.** **Top-performing sales reps generate more
  revenue from partner referrals than from any other single source**
  ([PartnerStack Research Lab, 2026](https://partnerstack.com/resources/research-lab/charts/partner-driven-represents-highest-revenue-source-for-top-performing-sales-reps)).
- **It lowers CAC.** Partnership-sourced leads cost **30–40% less** than
  paid-acquisition leads ([Genesys Growth, 2026](https://genesysgrowth.com/blog/customer-acquisition-cost-benchmarks-for-marketing-leaders)).

## How to Source and Qualify Partners

Recruitment is the decision about whether to bring a partner in at all. A
bad qualification at this stage cannot be fixed by onboarding
([PartnerStandard](https://pro.partnerstandard.com/guides/qualifying-channel-partners-with-the-4c-method)).

**The 4C qualification gate: every partner must clear all four.**

1. **Customer base**: the partner's customers overlap your ICP. No shared
   customer, no deal at the end.
2. **Credibility**: the partner's brand is trusted by the buyers you want.
   A random logo in a similar category is not credibility.
3. **Capability**: the partner can sell, implement, or deliver
   your product. An integration partner without engineers is a wallflower.
4. **Commitment**: the partner has skin in the game: a named owner, a
   joint plan, a revenue target. Vague enthusiasm is not commitment.

**Where to find partners:**

- **Your own customer stack.** The tools your best customers already pay
  for alongside you are the highest-converting partners. Ask customers what
  they run with your product.
- **The partner's existing ecosystem.** Every tool has its own partner
  program. Find where your market buys and list there.
- **Competitive adjacency.** Partners who serve the same ICP without
  competing on your product are ideal.
- **Inbound and outbound like any deal.** Partner recruitment is a sales
  motion. Reach out to named owners at target partners with a specific
  joint-customer angle, not a generic "let's partner."

**The row template for a partner pipeline**: every target carries: company,
partner type (integration / referral / reseller / channel), ICP-overlap
score (1–5), named contact, the joint customer or motion you will build, and
the comp model you are offering. No joint motion, no outreach.

## Onboard Partners (the 30/60/90)

Onboarding is the bounded first 30–90 days after signing, when the partner
goes from contract-signed to able to win without you in the room. It ends
when the partner can sell solo, not when the calendar runs out
([PartnerStandard](https://pro.partnerstandard.com/guides/partner-onboarding)).

| Phase | Days | Goal | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alignment and access | 1–30 | Partner states your value in their own words | Partner records back your positioning in their own words |
| First proof | 31–60 | Demo, first co-sell, first deal registration | Partner logs a qualified opportunity |
| Solo motion | 61–90 | Partner sells solo | Partner runs the full motion without you on the call |

**The rules that keep onboarding honest:**

- **Do not confuse activity with progress.** Training completion and portal
  logins are not activation. The milestone is an outcome: a recorded
  positioning in the partner's own words, a demo delivered solo, a qualified
  opportunity logged.
- **Segment by partner type.** A referral partner and a reseller need
  different tracks. One generic onboarding for all four types wastes
  everyone's time.
- **The portal is a delivery surface, not the program.** Run the workflow
  manually with the first few partners. Pick the PRM tool once the process
  is proven.
- **Include the economics.** By day 60 the partner must be able to explain
  the comp model and how MDF and deal registration work. A partner who does
  not understand their own economics will not sell.

## Sequencing: When and How to Layer It In

Partnerships are not a first channel. They compound on top of an existing
market. The order:

1. **Post-PMF, post-first-revenue.** Prove the product and the direct motion
   first. A partner needs a proven product to amplify.
2. **Start with integrations**: they cost no margin and build the mutual-
   customer proof. One or two strong integration partners create the
   reference that makes referral and reseller conversations credible.
3. **Add referral partners** once you have proof and a comp model. Referrals
   are the cheapest incremental pipeline and the fastest to stand up.
4. **Add resellers last.** Resellers need margin, enablement, and a
   recognized brand to resell. Stand these up only after referrals show
   partner demand is real.

**The one-program rule.** Run the motion through one owner and one funnel.
Splitting partners across siloed teams is the top blocker: **37% of
companies cite misalignment between teams as the biggest blocker to
partner-driven revenue, followed by no clear program (20%) and lack of
visibility (14%)** ([PartnerStack, 2026](https://partnerstack.com/resources/research-lab/the-state-of-partnerships-in-gtm-2026)).

## Objections: and How to Answer Them

| Objection | What it means | How to answer |
|---|---|---|
| "We already partner with [competitor]" | They have a relationship | Ask what is working and what is not. Differentiate on the joint motion, not on dislodging the existing partner. |
| "Our customers do not ask for your product" | No shared demand | Build the mutual-customer case first: find customers who run both, then go back. Do not pitch integration to a partner whose book never touches yours. |
| "The margin is not enough" | Comp is too thin | Increase margin or change the partner type. If you cannot pay reseller margin, make it a referral deal instead. |
| "We do not have the resources to enable us" | Commitment gap | Qualify harder. A partner without a named owner and time is not a partner. |
| "Let's keep in touch" | Soft no | Set a trigger and a follow-up date. Move on. |
| No response at all | The default | Re-trigger on a signal: a new customer, a marketplace listing, a product launch. |

**Answer the question, do not pitch harder.**

## Common Failure Modes: and the Fix

| Failure mode | Why it happens | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Signing partners then ignoring them | A logo feels like progress | Give every signed partner a launch date and a joint revenue target. Activation is the gate. |
| Building an integration nobody's customer asked for | The integration becomes the goal | The shared customer is the goal. Validate that the same ICP wants both products before building. |
| No co-sell discipline | Partner sales teams will not sell an unknown product | Make it trivial: one-page proof, a joint story, deal registration, comp that counts toward their quota. |
| Chasing logos over pipeline | Partner count is an easy vanity metric | Score partner-sourced pipeline. Logos without revenue are decoration. |
| Giving away margin with nothing in return | Discounting to win the deal | Discount only for partners who bring registered deals. Otherwise you cut your own price. |
| Attribution guessing | No multi-touch attribution | Only 42% of companies use multi-touch. Decide the model before the first deal and record it. |
| Treating integration partners as resellers | Category confusion | Match the comp and the expectation to the partner type. |

## Case Matrix: Product Partner vs Services Partner

Two distinct partner categories, two different value exchanges. Most
programs fail by blurring them.

| Dimension | Product (ISV / integration) partner | Services (agency / consultant / MSP) partner |
|---|---|---|
| What they bring | Distribution, a complementary product, technical integration | Implementation, expertise, customer trust, delivery |
| The value exchange | Mutual product value + joint pipeline | You provide the product, they provide the delivery and the relationships |
| Comp model | Non-monetary or small revenue-share on attached MRR | Referral commission, or a services/implementation margin |
| The risk | They build the integration and nobody buys it | They take the customer and own the relationship, squeezing your margin |
| Activation | Integration shipped + shared customers | First implementation delivered + repeat referrals |
| Example | A CRM integrating a native billing API | A digital agency implementing and reselling your product |
| Best first move | One anchor integration with a shared ICP | One agency whose clients match your ICP |

**The services-partner caution:** a services partner who owns the customer
can disintermediate you. Keep the comp tied to your product's recurring
revenue, not to their services fee, so their incentive stays aligned with
keeping your product in the account.

## Handoff to Sales

The handoff is where partner pipeline dies most often. Fix it before you
scale:

1. **Define the qualified-partner-meeting standard.** Write down who counts.
   If you cannot, "partner-sourced meetings" is meaningless.
2. **Track the source.** Every partner deal must trace back to a partner and
   a program. No source, no scale.
3. **Attribution before the first deal.** Pick the model, write it down, and
   tell the partner how they get paid before any deal closes.
4. **Debrief every 2 weeks.** Which partner type converts, which comp tier
   wins, which objection kills deals.
5. **Feed wins back into sourcing.** Your best partners become the profile
   for the next cohort. The motion is a loop.

## Agentic Layer: the SOP to Run a Partner Motion

Partnerships are relationship-heavy but process-thin, which makes them a
good fit for an agent that does the tracking, the sourcing, and the
enablement while a human owns the relationships. **49% of B2B SaaS senior
leaders say they want AI for better partner and account targeting**
([PartnerStack, 2026](https://partnerstack.com/resources/research-lab/the-state-of-partnerships-in-gtm-2026)).

The rule that never changes: **the gate stays, the execution layer
changes.** Humans keep judgment and relationships. Agents remove the
tracking bottleneck.

| Motion step | Agent does | Human approves |
|---|---|---|
| Sourcing | Scores target partners against the 4C gate, matches ICP overlap from customer-stack data | Approves the outreach list |
| Outreach | Drafts a specific joint-customer angle per partner | Approves the pattern, sends the first few |
| Onboarding | Tracks 30/60/90 milestones, flags partners stuck in phase one, drafts enablement checklists | Runs the kickoff and the positioning workshop |
| Attribution | Records every deal touch, applies the agreed attribution model, flags disputed credit | Resolves attribution disputes |
| Reporting | Builds the funnel table, flags activation under 50%, surfaces dead partners | Decides whether to reactivate or retire |

**The one-prompt starter for partner sourcing** (adapt to your stack):

```
You are sourcing integration and referral partners for {company}, a
{product} serving {ICP}. For each target partner, return a row with:
company, partner type (integration / referral / reseller / channel),
ICP-overlap score (1–5), named contact, and the one joint motion we could
build (the shared customer, not a generic pitch). Apply the 4C gate:
customer base, credibility, capability, commitment. Skip any partner that
fails a gate. Return only rows that pass all four.
```

**Three guardrails that keep it honest:**

1. **Human owns the relationship.** An agent can track and draft, but it cannot
   run the kickoff or the positioning workshop. The relationship is the
   channel.
2. **Activation below 50% is a flag, not a number.** If signed partners do
   not launch, stop sourcing and fix onboarding first.
3. **Attribution is written down.** Do not let an agent guess who gets
   credit. The model is set before the first deal.
