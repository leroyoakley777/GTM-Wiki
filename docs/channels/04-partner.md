# Partner / Channel

**Partnerships are leverage — they let another company's sales team sell your product.** They are not a default and they are not a "growth hack." A partnership only compounds when there is genuine mutual value: your product makes their customers better and theirs make yours better. Done right, it is the cheapest pipeline you will ever source. Done badly, it is a calendar full of partner calls that never close a deal.

This page is the *where* of partnerships: when they are the right call and how to judge whether the motion is healthy. It covers tech partnerships, channel/reseller relationships, and the integration partners that drive product-led virality.

---

## One Number That Matters Most

**Qualified partner-sourced pipeline as a share of total new pipeline — a healthy target is 10–20% for a mature partner program.** That is the number that tells you the motion is actually contributing and not just occupying time. Below that, the program is a tax. Above it, you are leaving upside on the table and should invest more.

Do not track "number of partners signed" as a score. Signed but inactive partners are decoration. Partner-sourced pipeline is revenue.

---

## Is Partnerships Even the Right Channel?

Use the [Channels Bullseye](../foundations/gtm-channels-bullseye) to pick your one best channel first. Partnerships win when:

- **Your product complements rather than replaces an existing tool.** The natural partner is the tool your customer already buys. They get stickier, you get their distribution.
- **There is a clear integration or co-sell motion.** A partner you cannot integrate with, reference, or co-sell is a logo, not a channel.
- **The partner's customer is your customer.** If the ICPs do not overlap, there is no deal at the end of the partnership.

If none of those are true, partnerships will consume energy for zero pipeline. A partnership with no shared customer is a friendship, not a channel.

## Partnership Funnel — Benchmarks That Diagnose

Each stage answers a different question. A weak stage caps the whole motion.

| Stage | Healthy range | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Partner activation rate | >50% of signed partners launch | Whether the onboarding worked |
| Integration adoption | 10–30% of mutual customers | Whether the product is actually complementary |
| Co-sell conversion | 5–15% of partner-referred leads | Whether the partner can sell |
| Partner-sourced share of new pipeline | 10–20% | Whether the channel matters |
| Partner-sourced CAC | Often 30–50% lower than direct | The core economic argument |

The order matters. Activation comes before adoption, adoption before co-sell, co-sell before pipeline share. Trying to optimize pipeline share while partners are not even launched is measuring noise.

## Two Partnership Motions

**1. Tech / integration partnerships.** The partner builds on top of you (or you on them), and mutual customers get a better product. Pipeline shows up as integration-driven signups and as the partner's sales team selling the joint story. This is the motion behind most modern product-led virality.

**2. Channel / reseller partnerships.** The partner resells or co-sells your product to their book of business. This is an indirect sales force. It only works when the partner has the relationships and the economics are worth their time — which means your product needs a price point with enough margin to share.

Most failed partnerships are a category error: they expect co-sell economics from a tech partnership with no shared customer, or they treat an integration as if it were a reseller relationship.

---

## Common failure modes

- **Signing partners, then ignoring them.** A logo without an activation plan is a cost. Every signed partner needs a launch date and a joint revenue target.
- **Building integration nobody's customer asked for.** The integration is not the win; the shared customer is. Validate that the same ICP wants both products.
- **No co-sell discipline.** The partner's sales team will not sell your product unless you make it trivially easy — and worth their quota.
- **Chasing logos over pipeline.** The number of partners you can name is vanity. Partner-sourced revenue is the score.
- **Giving away margin with nothing in return.** If you discount for a partner who brings no deals, you have just cut your own price.
