# Copywriting

This section is the home for how GTM turns a product into words a buyer can act on. It covers product positioning, messaging, and the frameworks that structure copy. It does not ship recommended email copy, cold sequences, or paste-ready outreach.

Outbound, ads, landing pages, and decks all consume this work. None of them should invent it.

## What sits here

Work the layers in order. Each one constrains the next.

| Layer | Job | Page |
|-------|-----|------|
| Audience | Who the reader is and why they should care | [ICP definition](/docs/foundations/icp-definition) |
| Positioning | The place you occupy in the buyer's mind versus alternatives | [Positioning](/docs/copywriting/positioning) |
| Messaging | The claim, the frame, and the proof | [Messaging](/docs/copywriting/messaging) |
| Copywriting | The move order that takes a reader from a state to an action | [Frameworks](/docs/copywriting/frameworks) |

Most weak copy fails at audience, positioning, or messaging and then blames the sentence. You cannot write your way out of a fuzzy ICP or an unclear claim.

The long-form strategy treatment of positioning still lives in [Positioning and messaging](/docs/foundations/positioning-and-messaging). Use that page to set the strategy. Use this section to turn the strategy into a writable claim and a framework.

## What this section will not do

- Recommend a cold email, a follow-up, a breakup note, or a subject line.
- Ship a paste-ready sequence for outbound, LinkedIn, or phone.
- Treat copywriting as a substitute for offer, ICP, or infrastructure.

If you need the outbound motion (list, deliverability, sequence structure, reply handling), open [Outbound](/docs/channels/outbound). Write the words from this section. Run the motion from that one.

## Why the split exists

Outbound is a channel. Copywriting is a craft that every channel uses. Mixing them produces two failures:

1. **Channel pages fill up with sample emails.** Operators paste them. Prospects see the same template from ten vendors.
2. **Copy gets written before the claim exists.** A clever first line cannot carry a product nobody wants. Positioning and messaging have to land first.

Keep sample emails out of this wiki. Keep frameworks, principles, and claim structure in.

## How to use this section

1. Name the reader. If you cannot, stop and run [ICP definition](/docs/foundations/icp-definition).
2. Write the positioning: category, alternatives, unique value, proof. Use [Positioning](/docs/copywriting/positioning).
3. Compress that into one claim plus proof. Use [Messaging](/docs/copywriting/messaging).
4. Pick a framework from the reader's decision problem, not from a favorite formula. Use [Frameworks](/docs/copywriting/frameworks).
5. Fill the framework's move order. Do not skip to a finished email and reverse-engineer the claim.

A reader who finishes these pages can write. They will not leave with a recommended email to send.
