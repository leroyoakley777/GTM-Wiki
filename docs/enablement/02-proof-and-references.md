# Proof pack and references

A proof pack is the small set of customer stories a rep can say aloud from memory. It is not a folder of logos and it is not a slide appendix. The proof pack answers the buyer's real question at the moment doubt appears: has someone like me already won with this?

Enablement owns the proof pack because a story with no named outcome is a claim, and proof beats claims <sup><a href="#sources">[224]</a></sup>. This page pairs with [Enablement](/docs/enablement) for the artifacts a rep carries, [Ramp and certification](./ramp-and-certification) for the gate where a rep proves the stories, and [Competitive analysis](/docs/foundations/competitive-analysis) for the win/loss input that keeps them current.

## What a proof pack governs

A working proof pack decides four things a case-study library does not:

- **Which story a rep reaches for.** One story per buying objection, not twenty stories sorted by logo.
- **Whether the number is real.** A named customer and a named outcome, or the story stays out.
- **When the story is current.** A result from two years ago describes a different product and a different team.
- **What the buyer can verify.** A reference call, a public case study, or a review quote. Each carries a different weight.

Revenue Operations treats commercial asset management as a pillar next to enablement and operations, because an asset that no one can find or trust does no work <sup><a href="#sources">[259]</a></sup>.

## Anatomy of a proof pack

Keep one row per story. A rep reads the row, not the case study.

| Field | What it holds | Done looks like |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|
| Customer | Named company and the buyer's role | "Ramp, VP Sales", not "a fintech" |
| Before | The state that made them look | A number with a date |
| Change | What they ran with you | One sentence a rep can repeat |
| Result | The outcome and its as-of date | "cycle time down 22 percent, as of Q1 2026" |
| Objection it answers | The doubt this story removes | "too small to matter", or "we tried that" |
| Proof type | Reference, public case, or quote | Tells the rep what they can promise |

Made to Stick names the same mechanism. A credible message leans on vivid, specific detail and testable proof, not on vague praise <sup><a href="#sources">[265]</a></sup>. Revenue Builders puts the same bar on the seller: replace the logo dump with relevant customer examples and remove buyer doubt through credible proof points <sup><a href="#sources">[336]</a></sup>. A row that reads "great results" carries no weight with a buyer.

## Collect the number, or cut the story

Most teams collect logos and skip the number. Run one rule instead: every story carries a named customer, a named outcome, and an as-of date. A rep who cannot say the number under pressure cannot use the story at all.

Three inputs feed each row:

1. **The closed-won record.** Pull the before-state from the first call, not from memory after the deal closed.
2. **The buyer's own words.** Ask the champion what changed, and record their sentence. The customer is the hero of the story and the vendor is the guide <sup><a href="#sources">[256]</a></sup>.
3. **The win/loss finding.** Feed each loss reason into the story that answers it. Findings that never reach the battlecard never reach the rep <sup><a href="#sources">[293]</a></sup>.

Buyers do more research alone before they talk to a rep, so the rep meets them later and holds less of the conversation <sup><a href="#sources">[122]</a></sup>. A current, named story does the work the rep no longer gets to do live.

## Proof type sets what the rep can promise

Match the claim to the proof. A rep who promises more than the pack supports creates a reference call the team cannot deliver.

- **Reference call.** Strongest proof, highest cost. The customer talks to the buyer directly. Reserve it for late-stage deals and warn the customer first.
- **Public case study.** Named customer, named number, published. The rep sends it without asking anyone.
- **Quote or review.** Lightest proof. Useful early, weak on its own.
- **Internal win story.** No name attached. Use it to build confidence, never as evidence.

A differentiated feature is only valuable when a customer can prove it against the alternative they would otherwise use <sup><a href="#sources">[257]</a></sup>. The proof type is how the rep proves it.

## Worked example: one proof pack for a mid-market segment

A team sells a sales-engagement tool to mid-market SaaS companies. The enablement lead builds one proof pack for that segment.

The pack holds three rows. Row one answers "we are too small for you": a 40-person company cut its follow-up time in half, with a public case study. Row two answers "we already use a bigger tool": a 200-person company switched after a failed renewal, with a reference call on request. Row three answers "our reps will not adopt it": a 90-person company reached 80 percent weekly adoption in one quarter, with a champion quote.

The rep carries three stories, not a library. Each row names the customer, the outcome, and the date. Each row answers one doubt. The rep can say all three from memory on a call.

## How the proof pack differs by stage

- **Seed.** The founder is the proof. The first ten customers often come from founder relationships, so the founder tells those stories live and writes them down for the next hire <sup><a href="#sources">[119]</a></sup>.
- **First sales team.** One proof pack per segment, three to five rows. One owner keeps it current. The rep certifies on the stories at the ramp gate.
- **Growth.** The pack splits by segment and by objection. A public case study joins the reference call. Win/loss feeds the pack every month <sup><a href="#sources">[293]</a></sup>.
- **Scale.** Enablement owns the pack and the reference program. Marketing owns published case studies. Product marketing owns the competitive notes that sit beside it <sup><a href="#sources">[292]</a></sup>.

## Failure modes

- **Logo dump.** A wall of customer logos with no outcome. The buyer learns nothing and the rep cannot say anything. Fix: one row per story, with a named number.
- **Stale story.** A result from two years ago describes a product the buyer will not get. Fix: stamp every row with an as-of date and retire it at 12 months.
- **Unpromised reference.** A rep offers a reference call the customer never agreed to. Fix: mark each row with its proof type and get written consent before a call.
- **Proof with no owner.** The pack grows, ages, and loses trust. Fix: name one owner who reviews it every month.
- **A story the rep cannot say.** The row lives in a slide the rep never opens. Fix: keep the pack to rows a rep can repeat from memory.

## Agentic layer

An agent can keep the proof pack honest. It can pull the before-state from the call record, draft the row in the pack format, flag a row whose as-of date passed 12 months, and match each new win/loss finding to the story it updates. A human still approves the number and asks the customer for the reference. Set the guardrail first: the agent drafts and flags, and never contacts a customer or promises a reference.
