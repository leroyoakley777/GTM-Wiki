# Ramp and Certification

Ramp is the time from a rep's first day to full quota. Certification is the proof that the rep can run the path the team already agreed on. Enablement owns both, because a rep who ramps on a stale path learns the wrong motion at full speed.

A new SDR takes about 3.1 months to ramp and stays about 1.8 years, so a slow or wrong ramp wastes a large share of the seat <sup><a href="#sources">[242]</a></sup>. A structured onboarding program cuts the ramp to 6 to 8 weeks <sup><a href="#sources">[242]</a></sup>. The gap between those two numbers is the work this page covers.

This page is the operator view. It pairs with [Enablement](/docs/enablement) for the artifacts a rep certifies on, [Coaching and one-on-ones](/docs/culture/coaching-and-one-on-ones) for the weekly habit that holds the ramp, [SDRs](/docs/roles/sdrs) for the seat, and [Outbound from zero](/docs/playbooks/outbound-from-zero) for the motion the rep runs.

## What ramp and certification govern

Ramp and certification decide four things a job description cannot:

- **When a rep carries quota.** A graduated ramp leaves room to learn before the number counts. A day-one quota forces the rep to guess.
- **What the rep learns first.** The certified path, or whatever the rep finds alone.
- **Whether the ramp is measured.** Time to first qualified meeting is a number. "Settling in" is not.
- **Who stays past month six.** Average SDR turnover runs near 40 percent <sup><a href="#sources">[244]</a></sup>. A rep lost over comp costs the ramp investment twice <sup><a href="#sources">[337]</a></sup>.

Quota attainment across B2B sat at 27 to 30 percent in 2024 <sup><a href="#sources">[15]</a></sup>, and RepVue put it at about 43 to 47 percent in the same period <sup><a href="#sources">[92]</a></sup>. A ramp that ends in a fair, reachable quota is the difference between a rep who stays and a rep who leaves.

## Certify on the current path

Certification turns training into a measurable baseline. Exams and certifications add predictability to sales training and create a baseline to measure and iterate from <sup><a href="#sources">[262]</a></sup>. A two-day kickoff does not. Certify on the path the team runs today, not the library the team built last year.

Set one gate per artifact:

| Gate | What the rep proves | Artifact to certify on |
|------|---------------------|------------------------|
| Day 5 | States the ICP and its disqualifiers | ICP and disqualifier page |
| Day 10 | Writes a signal-led first line | Email patterns page |
| Week 3 | Runs discovery and logs a clean next step | Talk track and CRM fields |
| Week 6 | Answers the common objections | Competitive notes |
| Month 3 | Carries the full quota on the path | Full sequence and handoff |

Train every rep the same way and hold every rep to the same process. Sales can be predictable when the team runs repeatable, metric-driven training <sup><a href="#sources">[262]</a></sup>.

## Ramp by stage

- **Seed.** The founder ramps the first rep by sitting next to them on live calls. There is no written program, and the founder is the certification. Do not hire a head of enablement to write fiction about a motion that still changes weekly.
- **First sales team.** One written path and one certification. Use a graduated quota: light in month one, about half in month two, full in month three. Day-one quota kills new hires.
- **Growth.** Ramp splits by seat. An SDR certifies on the ICP and the outbound sequence <sup><a href="#sources">[251]</a></sup>. An AE certifies on discovery and the sales process. Each seat gets its own ramp plan.
- **Scale.** Enablement owns certification and the ramp plan. RevOps owns the quota-ramp mechanics. Stage 2 Capital puts enablement specialization after GTM fit, not before <sup><a href="#sources">[299]</a></sup>.

## Failure modes

- **Day-one quota.** A rep who owes full quota in week one learns to spray. Fix: use a graduated ramp.
- **Kickoff as ramp.** A two-day event is not a 90-day skill plan <sup><a href="#sources">[262]</a></sup>. Fix: certify at gates across the ramp.
- **Certifying on a stale path.** The rep learns last year's motion at full speed. Fix: certify on the current best path and retire the old one.
- **No ramp metric.** Nobody measures time to first qualified meeting, so a slow ramp stays hidden until the rep leaves. Fix: track time to first meeting for every rep.
- **A plan that breaks during ramp.** A rep lost over comp costs the full ramp investment twice <sup><a href="#sources">[337]</a></sup>. Fix: settle the plan before the rep starts.

## Worked example: a ramp that reaches quota in month three

A team hires two SDRs with no ramp plan. Both start at full quota. One quits in month two. The other books 40 meetings in the quarter and holds half of them. The team pays the ramp cost twice and blames the hires.

The manager writes a 90-day plan instead. Month one carries no quota, and the rep certifies on the ICP and the first-line patterns. Month two carries half quota, and the rep adds discovery. Month three carries full quota. The manager measures time to first qualified meeting and reviews the pace every Friday. A ramped outbound rep produces 10 to 20 excellent leads a month <sup><a href="#sources">[251]</a></sup>, and the plan is how the rep reaches that rate on schedule.

## Ramp and certification checklist

```text
90-DAY RAMP AND CERTIFICATION CHECKLIST
[ ] One written path the rep certifies on
[ ] Certification gates at day 5, day 10, week 3, week 6, month 3
[ ] Graduated quota: none in month 1, half in month 2, full in month 3
[ ] Time to first qualified meeting tracked for every rep
[ ] One skill per gate, never the whole library at once
[ ] Stale path retired when the motion changes
[ ] Plan and quota settled before the rep starts
[ ] Manager reviews ramp pace every Friday
```

## Agentic layer

An agent can build a ramp plan per seat, track time to first qualified meeting, and flag a rep whose ramp runs off pace. It can also flag a certification that has gone stale against the current path. A human still owns the certification, the quota ramp, and the decision to keep a rep. Set the guardrail first: the agent reports the pace and never changes a quota or a comp number. See [Agentic outbound](/docs/agentic/agentic-outbound) for the desk-level SOP.
