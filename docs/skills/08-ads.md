# Ads

Ads is the skill of buying pipeline on paid platforms and holding that spend to a known payback. You run it when you have budget to deploy, a payback target to hold, and a motion that can convert paid visitors into pipeline.

Paid is the channel you can switch on and off with a budget change, and it burns cash from the second it is on [1]. Unlike most other channels, it buys pipeline directly, so the sizing question is simple: spend up to what your sales team can close, and stop when a customer stops repaying its acquisition cost. The discipline is the payback, not the spend.

## When to use this skill

Run ads when:

- You have budget and a payback target you can name.
- Your ICP is reachable on a paid platform and your sales team can absorb the pipeline.
- You can build a landing page that converts the paid traffic you buy.
- You need to test demand fast or scale a proven motion.

Do not run it when you have no payback discipline. Ads without a payback target is a way to buy clicks and a dashboard that looks busy. The skill exists to make the spend produce pipeline at a known return, not to maximize spend [1].

## Definition

Ads is the repeatable process of deploying budget against a payback target across paid platforms. It combines channel and platform selection, creative and landing-page match, spend and performance management, and a measurement loop that reads pipeline and payback, not clicks. The qualified lead or the meeting is the unit of output, not the impression.

## Why it matters

Paid is the fastest channel to a named result, and the cost is real and rising. B2B paid-search customer acquisition runs about $1,180 per customer, Meta around $890, and TikTok around $540, against $348 for organic [2]. The channel converts cash into pipeline on a schedule, which is why it suits motions that need speed, but it only earns that schedule if the payback holds.

The benchmark reality sets the bar. Paid click-through is single digits at best across platforms: Google search CTR around 3.17%, display around 0.46% [3], LinkedIn CPC climbing from $10.48 to $15.72 across 2025 [4]. A healthy motion watches CPL and payback per channel, not raw clicks, and it prunes the platform that cannot deliver a customer inside the payback window [2].

## Mechanics

### 1. Set the payback target before you spend

The number that governs everything is the target CAC and its payback window. A common bar is 12 months payback, tightened from the 18-24 month norm of prior cycles [2]. Before any budget deploys, name:

- **The target CAC.** The most you will pay for a customer and still hit the payback.
- **The payback window.** The months of gross margin it takes to repay the acquisition.
- **The LTV floor.** The minimum customer value that justifies the spend, with LTV:CAC at least 3:1 [2].

The budget is then sized by what the sales team can close, not by what the platform will sell you.

### 2. Pick the platform and channel by where the buyer is

Match the platform to where your ICP decides, using the channel bullseye [5]:

- **Google search.** High-intent queries, the buyer actively looking. Highest CTR, highest cost [3].
- **LinkedIn.** B2B professional targeting, the buyer by role and company. Rising CPC [4].
- **Meta.** Broad reach and interest targeting, best for awareness and retargeting.
- **Display and programmatic.** Reach and retargeting at lower cost, with CPMs from $1.50 to $45 depending on format [6].

You do not need every platform. You need the one where your ICP is deciding and the one that can deliver inside the payback.

### 3. Match the creative and the landing page

Paid traffic converts only when the promise and the landing page agree. One claim, one landing page:

- **The ad names the problem and the outcome.** The creative leads with the claim, not a logo.
- **The landing page answers the ad.** The same claim, the proof, and one call to action [7].
- **The creative matches the platform.** Short-form on Meta, professional and precise on LinkedIn, intent-matched on search.

A mismatch is the cheapest way to burn spend: the ad promises one thing and the page delivers another, and the visitor leaves.

### 4. Manage spend to the number

Hold every dollar to the payback. Review the campaign on pipeline and payback, not on impressions:

- **Track CAC and payback per channel.** Kill the channel that cannot deliver a customer inside the window [2].
- **Prune invalid traffic.** Brands lose 15-25% of annual ad spend to non-human traffic, and search campaigns see 14-22% invalid traffic [8].
- **Scale the winner.** Move budget toward the platform and audience that clears the target, and cap the one that does not.

The discipline is to stop spending on what does not repay, early and without sentiment.

### 5. Measure the loop

Read pipeline and payback, and close the loop:

- **CPL and CAC by channel.** What a qualified lead and a customer cost on each platform [2].
- **Payback realized.** Whether the acquired customer repaid the acquisition inside the window.
- **Pipeline absorbed.** Whether the sales team actually closed what the spend produced.

A campaign that buys cheap clicks and no pipeline is not a win. The number that matters is whether the customer repaid the spend.

## Artifact: the budget and payback plan

Set the target before any spend.

```text
Target CAC:              $<max you will pay for a customer>
Payback window:          <months of gross margin to repay, e.g. 12>
LTV floor:               <min value, LTV:CAC >= 3:1>

Channel        | Target CPL | Target CAC | Budget | Payback check
---------------|------------|------------|--------|--------------
Google search  | $X         | $1,180 [2] | $Y     | kill if > 12 mo
LinkedIn       | $X         | $Y         | $Z     | kill if > 12 mo
Meta           | $X         | $890 [2]   | $Y     | kill if > 12 mo
Display        | $X         | $Y         | $Z     | kill if > 12 mo
```

Review each channel on realized payback, not impressions. Scale the winner, kill the laggard.

## Worked example

A B2B SaaS sells a GTM-metrics tool to heads of revenue operations at roughly $30K ACV. It sets a 12-month payback target and a target CAC it will not exceed. With a 30% gross margin on $30K ACV, it can afford roughly $7,500 of CAC per customer and repay in 12 months; the LTV:CAC floor keeps it well above 3:1 [2].

It deploys budget across Google search, LinkedIn, and Meta. Search carries the highest intent and the highest CAC [3]; LinkedIn targets the exact role; Meta retargets site visitors. It tracks CPL and realized CAC per channel and prunes invalid traffic [8].

If search delivers a $1,180 CAC and a customer repays inside 12 months [2], it scales search. If a platform shows a CAC that would not repay inside the window, it caps that platform regardless of the click count. The budget moves toward the channel that clears the payback and away from the one that does not. The payback plan, not the dashboard, decides.

## Failure modes and how to fix them

- **No payback target.** You spend because you have budget, and the dashboard looks busy. Fix: name the target CAC and window before any spend [1][2].
- **Spending what the platform sells you.** You size budget by what the platform suggests, not what the team can close. Fix: size by pipeline capacity and payback.
- **Creative and page mismatch.** The ad promises one claim and the page delivers another. Fix: one claim, one landing page, matched [7].
- **Vanity clicks.** You measure impressions and CTR instead of pipeline and payback. Fix: track CAC and realized payback per channel [2].
- **Ignoring invalid traffic.** A slice of spend goes to bots. Fix: monitor invalid traffic and prune [8].
- **Chasing every platform.** You run everywhere and optimize nothing. Fix: pick the platform where the ICP decides and clear the payback there [5].

## How the skill differs by segment and maturity

- **By segment.** SMB self-serve motions convert cheaper traffic and shorter payback. Enterprise motions use LinkedIn and intent to reach a buying group and accept a higher CAC against a larger ACV [4].
- **By maturity.** A new motion should test two platforms against the payback before scaling. A mature motion runs a multi-platform mix with the agent reallocating budget to the winner.
- **By channel pairing.** Ads buy the pipeline that the [landing and funnel](../foundations/07-gtm-funnel.md) converts; pair the creative with the [messaging](./messaging) foundation so the claim is consistent. Ads also feed [inbound](../channels/02-inbound.md) and [ABM](./abm) retargeting for high-value accounts.

## Agentic layer

An agent can manage spend, reallocate budget, prune underperforming campaigns, and log the payback loop, but it cannot decide the payback target or the claim. Give it the target CAC, the channel plan, and the payback window; it will run spend to the number. Keep the payback decision and the creative strategy with you.

**SOP: run spend to payback.**

```text
Given: a payback target, a channel plan, a claim, and a landing page.
1. Confirm the target CAC and payback window before any spend.
2. Deploy budget across the chosen platforms; match the creative to the page.
3. Track CPL, realized CAC, and invalid traffic per channel.
4. Scale the channel that clears the payback; cap the one that does not.
5. Route qualified leads to the sales team; log pipeline absorbed.
6. Kill any channel that cannot deliver a customer inside the payback window.
Never scale spend without a realized payback. Never count clicks as pipeline.
```

**Prompt: review a campaign against payback.**

```text
You are a performance marketing operator. Given a channel, its spend, CPL, and
realized CAC against the payback target, recommend the decision.
Scale, hold, or kill the channel based on whether it delivers a customer inside
the payback window. One clear recommendation. No hype.
Output the decision and one line of reasoning.
```

Keep the human in the loop for the payback target, the claim, and any budget increase beyond the plan. The agent runs spend and reports; you own the number.

## Run it today

Name your target CAC, payback window, and LTV floor. Pick the one platform where your ICP decides. Match the creative to one landing page with one claim. Run a test spend to the payback number and review it on realized CAC, not clicks. The first customer that repays inside the window is the proof the channel works.
