# Messaging

Messaging is the skill of turning who you sell to into a claim a writer can fill. You run it before any email, landing page, ad, or pitch goes out. The words are the last layer, not the first.

The full treatment lives in the [Copywriting](/docs/copywriting) section: [positioning](/docs/copywriting/positioning), [messaging](/docs/copywriting/messaging), and [frameworks](/docs/copywriting/frameworks). This page is the skill view: when to run it, the artifact, the failure modes, and the agentic SOP. It will not recommend email copy.

## When to use this skill

Run messaging when you are about to write something a buyer reads:

- A landing page, homepage, or pricing page.
- An ad, a social post, or a launch note.
- A sales deck or a demo script.
- Outbound, after the list and the reason-to-reach already exist. See [Outbound](/docs/channels/outbound).

Do not start with the words. Start with who you are writing to and what you are claiming. A framework applied to a muddy claim produces muddy copy that is easier to read and no more convincing.

## Definition

Messaging is the repeatable process of converting audience insight and a value claim into reader-facing meaning, then handing a framework to a writer. The meeting, the click, or the reply is the unit of output, not the sentence.

## Why it matters

Copy is where GTM decisions become reader-facing. A defined ICP drives 68% higher win rates and 30% more marketing-sourced revenue than an undefined one [1], but the claim still has to reach the buyer in words they act on. Positioning that is clear internally produces nothing until it is written for a specific reader.

The stakes are measurable. A signal-based, personalized cold email reaches 15-25% reply rates versus low single digits for a generic blast [2]; 50-125 word emails earn about 50% higher reply rates than long templated sends [3]. The same offer, different structure, different result. The frameworks exist because the difference is structure, not talent. They live in [Frameworks](/docs/copywriting/frameworks), not as a swipe file of emails.

## Artifact: claim card plus framework pick

Fill this before anyone writes a sentence.

```text
Audience:  [who, and why they should care]
Claim:     [one outcome]
Proof:     [named result, or stop]
Objection: [the doubt] / [the true reply]
Surface:   [page, ad, deck, in-app. Not "pasteable email."]
Framework: [from the decision grid]
```

Pick the framework from the reader's decision problem. The grid and the move orders are on [Frameworks](/docs/copywriting/frameworks). This skill page will not fill the framework with a recommended email.

## Failure modes and how to fix them

- **Writing before the layers.** Fluent and unconvincing. Fix: name the reader and the claim in one line each before you write.
- **Framework as decoration.** PAS on a muddy claim. Fix: [positioning](/docs/copywriting/positioning) and [messaging](/docs/copywriting/messaging) first.
- **One framework for everything.** PAS on a high-ticket offer that needs trust, or PASTOR on a 60-word ad. Fix: pick from the decision grid [7].
- **Agitation into hype.** Overstating the pain until the reader disbelieves the copy. Fix: agitate with the concrete cost [9].
- **Asking the wiki for an email to send.** Outbound copy is written per row from a reason-to-reach. This wiki does not publish sample emails.

## How the skill differs by segment and maturity

- **By segment.** SMB buyers decide faster and respond to shorter, benefit-led structures (BAB, 4Ps). Enterprise involves a committee: FAB for the technical proof, PASTOR for the economic buyer's trust [4].
- **By maturity.** A new motion should test one framework across a small set and read conversion data before scaling. A mature motion can map frameworks to surfaces.
- **By channel pairing.** Pair this skill with [outbound](./outbound) for the channel mechanics, [prospecting](./prospecting) for the audience layer, and [Copywriting](/docs/copywriting) for the craft.

## Agentic layer

An agent can fill labeled beats from a claim card. It cannot decide who you are selling to or what you are claiming. It must not return a recommended cold email.

```text
SOP: fill a framework from a claim card

Given: a named reader, a value claim, a proof point, and a target surface
(homepage, ad, deck, in-app). Not an email body.
1. Choose the framework from the reader's decision problem.
2. Fill the framework's move order with the audience line, the claim, and the proof.
3. Keep one claim. One ask at the end.
4. Return labeled beats, not a finished email.
Never invent a proof point.
Never output a cold email, subject line, or outreach sequence.
```

Keep the human in the loop for the audience definition, the value claim, any compliance-sensitive surface, and the final read.
