# RevOps

Revenue operations is the seat that owns the machine behind revenue: the
data, the process, the tooling, and the forecast that every revenue team
plans against. The seat exists so sellers sell and leaders plan on one set
of numbers.

This page is the operator's guide to the role itself. It covers what the
seat does week to week, where it reports, how the career ladder and pay
work, and when a company should build it. For the functional deep dive, the
math, and the failure modes of the revenue operations function, read
[Revenue Operations](../../foundations/06-revenue-operations.md). This page
is about the person in the seat.

---

## Definition

Revenue operations owns the operating system that connects sales, marketing,
and customer success. Its job is not to add sellers. Its job is to make the
sellers a company has convert more effort into pipeline, and pipeline into
closed revenue, measured on one set of numbers
([Revenue Operations, foundations/06](../../foundations/06-revenue-operations.md)).

The seat is distinct from a sales operations seat. Sales operations supports
the sales team only. Revenue operations aligns sales, marketing, and customer
success around shared revenue outcomes
([GTMnow / GTMfund](https://gtmnow.com/revenue-operations-career/), 2026).
Sales operations is a function inside one team. Revenue operations is the
structure that spans several.

The role is project-based, not a fixed checklist. The workload is never the
same two weeks in a row. One week is a territory model. The next is a forecast
cleanup. The next is a tool rollout that touches every team
([Revenue Operations Alliance](https://www.revenueoperationsalliance.com/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-revops-lead/), 2025).

Four responsibilities define the seat:

- **Process.** Define funnel stages, lead handoffs, and service-level
  agreements so marketing and sales agree on what a qualified lead is and
  when it moves.
- **Data.** Keep a single source of truth for pipeline, conversion by stage
  and source, forecast accuracy, and quota attainment.
- **Tooling.** Run the stack that routes leads, scores intent, and automates
  follow-up.
- **Forecasting.** Turn the funnel math into a number leadership can commit
  to.

## Why the seat exists

Revenue operations grew out of a failure mode. Marketing, sales, and customer
success each ran their own tools, their own data, and their own priorities.
Forecasts missed. Territories overlapped or left gaps. Compensation disputes
eroded trust. No single function owned the seams between teams
([Fullcast](https://www.fullcast.com/content/revops-career-path/), 2026).

Revenue operations closes those seams. The payoff shows up in the org and in
the market:

- Organizations with a revenue operations function grow revenue roughly three
  times faster than those without one
  ([Fullcast](https://www.fullcast.com/content/revops-career-path/), 2026).
- Public companies with revenue operations show about 71 percent higher stock
  performance than peers without it
  ([Fullcast](https://www.fullcast.com/content/revops-career-path/), 2026).
- Gartner projects 75 percent of the highest-growth companies will run on a
  revenue operations model by 2026, up from under 30 percent a few years
  earlier ([GTMnow / GTMfund](https://gtmnow.com/revenue-operations-career/), 2026).
- Demand for the seat is high. More than 174,000 revenue operations positions
  sat open on one job board alone
  ([Fullcast](https://www.fullcast.com/content/revops-career-path/), 2026).

The seat scales the whole revenue org, not one team. It does this with a
headcount that stays small relative to sellers. That is the model: spend here
compounds across every seller
([Revenue Operations, foundations/06](../../foundations/06-revenue-operations.md)).

## Operating rhythm

Revenue operations is a running loop, not a report. A working week has a
reliable shape even when the specific project changes
([Revenue Operations Alliance](https://www.revenueoperationsalliance.com/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-revops-lead/), 2025).

**Weekly.**

- Review the sprint or project board. Close out last week's tasks and open
  this week's.
- Prioritize work against sales goals and company goals for the year and the
  quarter. Backlog anything that does not move a goal.
- Run weekly one-on-ones with the manager plus syncs with sales, marketing,
  and customer success. Revenue operations sits at the intersection of teams,
  so meetings are part of the job.
- Review pipeline coverage and forecast changes. Flag what moved since last
  week.

**Monthly.**

- Recompute coverage by segment and source.
- Review lead quality and routing. Confirm handoff service-level agreements
  are holding.
- Report attainment against quota. Flag at-risk reps before the quarter ends,
  not after.

**Quarterly.**

- Reset quotas and refresh win-rate assumptions from the last four quarters.
- Re-derive the coverage target from the updated win rate.
- Reconcile the funnel stages and definitions with what actually happened.

**On demand.**

- Design a process workflow for a new initiative.
- Test and deploy CRM automation: fields, validation rules, flows.
- Build reports and dashboards.
- Evaluate a new tool before it enters the stack.
- Troubleshoot a broken field, a failing automation, or a report that does not
  match reality.

The priority test for any request is one question: does this impact pipeline
generation or deal closing? If it does not, it waits
([Revenue Operations Alliance](https://www.revenueoperationsalliance.com/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-revops-lead/), 2025).

## Where the seat reports

Revenue operations can only do its job if it sits above any single function.
An operations seat buried under sales optimizes sales. A seat under marketing
optimizes marketing. Revenue operations must be neutral enough to act as judge
and jury on rules of engagement, service-level agreements between functions,
planning and goals, and compensation design. That neutrality is what lets it
solve for the whole company instead of over-optimizing one function
([Stage 2 Capital](https://www.stage2.capital/blog/revops-when-how-and-who-to-hire), 2023).

In practice the seat reports to the CEO, the COO, or the revenue leader who
owns the whole engine. Early it often reports to the founder. As it scales it
finds a home under the chief revenue officer or the chief operating officer.
The reporting line matters less than the mandate. If the seat has no authority
over the shared system, it is a dashboard builder, not revenue operations
([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/revenue-operations/when-to-hire-revops), 2026).

## Career ladder

Revenue operations has a defined progression from analyst to executive. Each
level builds on the last: the scope widens, the influence grows, and pay
follows ([Fullcast](https://www.fullcast.com/content/revops-career-path/), 2026).

**Entry, 0 to 3 years: analyst or coordinator.**

Typical titles: revenue operations analyst, revenue operations coordinator,
sales operations analyst. The value here is accuracy and reliability. The work
is CRM administration, keeping data clean, building reports and dashboards,
documenting process, and providing front-line tool support.

Skills: spreadsheets, a CRM such as Salesforce or HubSpot, basic data
analysis, close attention to detail.

**Mid, 3 to 7 years: manager or senior analyst.**

The shift is from executing process to owning it. The work is territory and
quota planning, forecasting workflows, commission plan administration, and
driving cross-functional projects from scoping through implementation.

Skills: strategic thinking, project management, data visualization,
stakeholder management, and understanding how tools connect across the
revenue lifecycle.

**Senior, 7 to 12 years: director or head of revenue operations.**

The focus shifts to changing how the organization works. The work is
go-to-market strategy, revenue process across the whole funnel, executive
insight, team leadership, and ownership of the technology stack.

Skills: strategic planning, executive communication, team management, leading
organizational change, and practical automation skill.

**Executive, 12 plus years: VP of revenue operations or chief revenue officer.**

The decisions shape company-wide revenue strategy. The work is board-level
reporting, cross-functional alignment, organizational design, and building
leadership teams.

Skills: executive presence, profit and loss management, organizational
design, and strategic vision.

The path runs through the same domain the whole way. A person who learns to
run reports and keep the CRM clean at the entry level can, with the right
scope and skill growth, own the entire revenue operating system at the top.

## Compensation

Pay tracks the ladder. Figures are 2026 United States averages from
Glassdoor, compiled by GTMnow. They vary by industry, location, experience,
and company stage
([GTMnow / GTMfund](https://gtmnow.com/revenue-operations-career/), 2026).

| Level | 2026 US average | Typical range |
|-------|-----------------|---------------|
| Revenue operations specialist | $77,694 | $62,000 - $99,000 |
| Revenue operations analyst | $90,247 | $73,000 - $113,000 |
| Revenue operations manager | $128,166 | $102,000 - $163,000 |
| Director of revenue operations | $216,415 | $171,000 - $279,000 |

Growth-stage senior leaders run higher than the averages above. Head of
revenue operations at a startup or growth company commonly lands in the
$150,000 to $220,000 range with meaningful equity. Senior executives at
larger companies reach the $200,000 to $300,000 plus total compensation band.
Use the Glassdoor figures as the base, then add stage and equity premiums.

## How to execute in the seat

Revenue operations runs on a small set of habits that separate the seat that
moves revenue from the seat that just builds dashboards.

**Document everything.** Documentation prevents knowledge loss and keeps
processes scalable. Use knowledge base pages for written guides, workflows
for processes with branches, and short videos for tutorials. If a change is
not documented, it does not survive the next hire
([Revenue Operations Alliance](https://www.revenueoperationsalliance.com/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-revops-lead/), 2025).

**Say no to shiny ideas.** A large part of the job is pushing back on ideas
that sound good in theory but clutter the system. Every new field, workflow,
and tool adds complexity that every team pays for. Protect the system from
growth by addition ([Revenue Operations Alliance](https://www.revenueoperationsalliance.com/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-revops-lead/), 2025).

**Track changes across teams before you make them.** A change to opportunity
types in the CRM is not just a sales problem. It affects marketing
attribution, customer success workflows, and revenue reporting. Trace the
blast radius of every system change before deploying it.

**Marry the data, the money, and the strategy.** The highest-value work
translates cross-team data into concrete steps that improve revenue or
profitability. The seat that only reports is replaceable. The seat that
recommends what to do with the number is not
([GTMnow / GTMfund](https://gtmnow.com/revenue-operations-career/), 2026).

**Build for depth over breadth as the team scales.** An early revenue
operations person is a generalist by necessity. As the team grows, specialize:
one person for enablement, one for the technology stack, one for insights.
Specialists deepen each area instead of spreading thin
([Stage 2 Capital](https://www.stage2.capital/blog/revops-when-how-and-who-to-hire), 2023).

**Pull the agentic lever on volume work.** Data reconciliation, lead routing,
coverage reporting, and forecast rollup are structured and repeatable. Agents
can do the volume while a human sets the model and approves the output. The
full treatment is in [Agentic GTM](../../agentic/00-agentic-gtm-overview.md)
and [Revenue Operations, foundations/06](../../foundations/06-revenue-operations.md).

## When a company builds the seat

Revenue operations is not the first operations hire every company makes. It
is the hire a company makes when the revenue system is shared enough that no
single function can fix it alone
([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/revenue-operations/when-to-hire-revops), 2026).

Hire revenue operations when revenue problems cross team boundaries: lifecycle
definitions, source of truth, routing, handoffs, forecast trust, attribution,
or revenue cadence. Use this test
([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/revenue-operations/when-to-hire-revops), 2026):

| Pain | First hire |
|------|------------|
| Multiple teams use the same fields differently | Revenue operations |
| Finance rebuilds revenue reports outside the operating system | Revenue operations |
| Handoffs fail between marketing, sales, customer success, and finance | Revenue operations |
| Problem is mostly quota, territory, and sales process | Sales operations may be enough |
| Problem is mostly campaign operations and attribution | Marketing operations may be enough |
| Problem is mostly onboarding, renewal, and health | Customer success operations may be enough |

The stages map cleanly onto revenue operations maturity:

- **Product-market fit.** Outsource revenue operations as a service, or let a
  founding generalist own the CRM and basic tracking. Do not overbuild.
- **Go-to-market fit.** Bring on a director-plus revenue operations lead
  alongside the first sales leader. The revenue leader should not spend ten
  hours a week in the CRM doing workflow and dashboard work
  ([Stage 2 Capital](https://www.stage2.capital/blog/revops-when-how-and-who-to-hire), 2023).
- **Scale.** Specialize the team into operations, enablement, project
  management, technology stack, and insights
  ([Stage 2 Capital](https://www.stage2.capital/blog/revops-when-how-and-who-to-hire), 2023).

**Hiring too early** creates a title without a mandate. You are probably too
early if one founder still owns most sales, marketing is not a real
acquisition channel, the CRM has few meaningful records, or the sales process
changes every month. At that stage a lightweight process owner beats a revenue
operations hire ([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/revenue-operations/when-to-hire-revops), 2026).

**Hiring too late is more common and more expensive.** Every broken definition
becomes embedded in reports, dashboards, workflows, and team habits. The first
hire then spends months untangling historical mess before improving the
system. Definition debt, reporting debt, handoff debt, tooling debt, meeting
debt, and hiring debt all accrue while a company waits
([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/revenue-operations/when-to-hire-revops), 2026).

**Before opening the role, run a 30-day proof.** Assign one owner, even
part-time, and have them produce the four core outputs. If a single person can
hold the process together for thirty days, the company knows what it is hiring
for. If they cannot, the company knows the real bottleneck
([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/revenue-operations/when-to-hire-revops), 2026).

## Common mistakes

**1. Hiring a dashboard analyst when you need a system owner.** The first
revenue operations hire needs authority over the shared operating system, not
just responsibility for dashboards. If the before-and-after test only yields
better dashboards, the role is under-scoped
([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/revenue-operations/when-to-hire-revops), 2026).

**2. Confusing sales operations with revenue operations.** Sales operations is
the right first hire under roughly $10 million in ARR with a small seller
base. Revenue operations is for when the motion spans marketing, sales, and
customer success. Matching the title to the pain is most of the hiring
decision ([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/revenue-operations/when-to-hire-revops), 2026).

**3. Hiring too late and inheriting cleanup debt.** Waiting until the forecast
is a joke and finance has a shadow spreadsheet forces the first hire into a
year of untangling before they can add value. Hire when the pain crosses teams,
not after it has hardened into process
([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/revenue-operations/when-to-hire-revops), 2026).

**4. Building dashboards nobody acts on.** A fifty-metric dashboard is
decoration. Revenue operations earns its keep when the numbers drive a
decision with an owner. Attach a decision to each metric or drop the metric.

**5. Automating a weak process.** Tooling that routes leads into a broken
funnel just produces faster wrong. Fix the process manually first, then
automate it.

**6. Never saying no.** A revenue operations person who approves every request
for a new field, workflow, or tool grows the system into a tangle every team
pays for. The job includes refusing complexity
([Revenue Operations Alliance](https://www.revenueoperationsalliance.com/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-revops-lead/), 2025).

**7. Skimping on documentation.** Undocumented process dies with the person
who built it. Every change that is not written down becomes a support ticket
for the next hire
([Revenue Operations Alliance](https://www.revenueoperationsalliance.com/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-revops-lead/), 2025).
