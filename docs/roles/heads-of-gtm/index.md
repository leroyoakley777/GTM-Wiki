# Heads of GTM

A head of GTM is the executive who owns the system that connects every
revenue function to a single number. Sales leaders own quota. Marketing
leaders own demand. A head of GTM owns the machine that turns both into
revenue ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).

This page is the operator's guide to the seat. It covers what the role
actually does week to week, how it differs from a sales VP or a CMO, the
operating rhythm, when a company should hire one, and the failure modes
that kill GTM leaders. For the functional deep dives, read the foundations:
[What is GTM](../../foundations/00-what-is-gtm.md), the
[GTM strategy framework](../../foundations/00-gtm-strategy-framework.md),
and [Revenue Operations](../../foundations/06-revenue-operations.md). This
page is about the person in the seat.

---

## Definition

A head of GTM is the executive who directs how a company brings products
to market and drives revenue growth across sales, marketing, RevOps, and
customer success ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).
The seat exists to own the seams between functions, not any single function.

The title changes by stage. Same mandate, different names:

- **Founding GTM Lead.** The first GTM hire at a startup. Builds the engine
  from scratch: cold outreach, pricing, early hiring, tech stack
  ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).
- **Head of GTM / GTM Lead.** The growth-stage version. One person owns the
  full motion across the org
  ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).
- **VP of Go-to-Market.** Mid-market and enterprise. Usually reports to the
  CRO or CEO ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).
- **Chief Revenue Officer (CRO).** The version where the mandate spans
  marketing and customer success, not just sales
  ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).

The title matters less than the mandate. A GTM leader without authority
across functions is a strategist with a deck
([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).
The reporting line matters less than the power to move resources between
teams. If a head of GTM cannot reallocate budget or headcount, they do not
hold the seat. They hold a title.

### How the seat differs from adjacent roles

- **Sales VP owns quota.** The number. The team. The forecast. The head of
  GTM owns the system that produces the number.
- **CMO owns demand.** The pipeline at the top of the funnel. The head of
  GTM owns pipeline all the way through to closed revenue.
- **RevOps owns the machine.** Data, process, tooling. The head of GTM
  owns the direction of that machine and what it is built to do
  ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).

The clean way to read it: each function leader owns a silo. The head of
GTM owns the seams and the shared operating rhythm that makes the silos
work as one ([GTM Partners](https://gtmpartners.com/), 2025).

## Why the seat exists

The seat exists because revenue is a system problem, not a function
problem. A great sales team with weak marketing starves. A great marketing
team with weak sales wastes budget. A great product with a misaligned
motion goes nowhere. Someone has to own the whole funnel.

The failure mode the seat solves is fragmentation. Marketing, sales, and
customer success each run their own tools, their own data, and their own
priorities. Forecasts miss. Territories overlap or leave gaps. No single
function owns the seams between teams
([Fullcast](https://www.fullcast.com/content/revops-career-path/), 2026).

Three things get fixed when a real GTM leader sits in the seat:

1. **One definition of a customer.** Shared ICPs and unified lead scoring
   so sales and marketing agree on who is qualified
   ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).
2. **One set of numbers.** A single source of truth for pipeline,
   conversion by stage, and forecast accuracy
   ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).
3. **One operating rhythm.** Joint planning and service-level agreements
   make sales and marketing alignment the default state, not a project
   ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).

The payoff is compounding. When functions share one ICP, one funnel, and
one forecast, every hire and every dollar of spend multiplies instead of
fights. That is why the seat scales: it removes the drag that appears the
moment more than one person sells ([From Impossible to Inevitable](
https://www.saastr.com/), 2016).

## Framework

Run the seat as an operating system, not a task list. The GTM operating
system has eight pillars: market, positioning, sales, marketing, customer
success, revenue ops, data, and leadership
([GTM Partners](https://gtmpartners.com/), 2025). The head of GTM does not
do all eight. They make the eight run as one.

The operating frame breaks into four loops:

### 1. Strategy loop

Decide which markets to enter, which segments to prioritize, how to
position against competitors, and which motions to run
([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).
This is quarterly work, not annual. Markets shift faster than a planning
cycle. Revisit ICP, TAM, positioning, and pricing every quarter
([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).

### 2. Allocation loop

Decide how resources flow between functions based on what is working
([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).
This is the highest-leverage work in the seat. The head of GTM moves
budget and headcount toward the motion that produces revenue and away from
the motion that does not.

### 3. Execution loop

Turn strategy into action across the funnel. Field enablement gives
sellers the messaging frameworks, battle cards, and objection playbooks
they need to execute
([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).
RevOps direction keeps the machine aligned to the strategy.

### 4. Measurement loop

Measure outcomes, not activity. Move from volume metrics to outcome
metrics that track real buyer progress
([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).
Make signal velocity, the frequency of high-intent behaviors within a
window, a core KPI for frontline operators
([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).

The four loops run in sequence. Strategy sets allocation. Allocation sets
execution. Measurement feeds back into strategy. A head of GTM who skips a
loop runs a motion on instinct.

### Three archetypes

The strongest GTM leaders blend three profiles. Each fits a stage:

| Archetype | Strength | Best fit |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| Sales-driven operator | Fast execution, pipeline intuition, direct accountability | Early-stage, limited resources |
| Data-focused strategist | Scalable process, strong forecasting, data-driven allocation | Growth and enterprise, complex cycles |
| Cross-functional orchestrator | Org alignment, holistic strategy, internal influence | Multiple products, segments, or motions in parallel |

The operator mindset dominates early. The strategist and orchestrator
profiles become critical as the organization scales
([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).

## How to execute

### When to hire the first head of GTM

The first GTM hire is rarely a head of GTM. It is usually a seller, a
marketer, or a CS person, whichever complements the founding team's gaps
([Stage 2 Capital](https://www.stage2.capital/blog/guide-to-hiring-your-first-gtm-leader-what-startups-need-to-know), 2024).
A head of GTM is the hire that comes after the motion works, when you have
pattern recognition to teach it
([Techstars](https://www.techstars.com/blog/founder-advice/founders-your-first-sales-hire-is-probably-a-mistake), 2026).

Hire the head of GTM when the problems cross team boundaries. When
multiple teams use the same fields differently, when the forecast is not
trusted, when handoffs fail between marketing and sales and sales and CS,
you need a seat that owns the seams
([Rework](https://www.rework.com/), 2026). Hire too early and you get a
title without a mandate. Hire too late and you get cleanup debt.

### 90-day operating plan

A new head of GTM gets 90 days. Run them in thirds:

- **Days 1-30: Diagnose.** Do not change anything. Map the funnel, the
  numbers, the tools, the handoffs. Find where revenue leaks. Write down
  what each function thinks the other is doing wrong.
- **Days 31-60: Pick the highest-leverage fix.** One problem, not five.
  Usually it is the handoff, the forecast, or the definition of a qualified
  lead. Fix it end to end with authority.
- **Days 61-90: Build the rhythm.** Stand up the weekly cadence: pipeline
  review, forecast call, and a feedback loop that turns rep insights into
  updated targeting
  ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).

### Weekly rhythm

- **Monday: forecast.** Review pipeline against the committed number. Name
  the risks out loud.
- **Weekly: pipeline review.** Conversion by stage and source. Win rate by
  segment. Where the funnel leaks, fix it this week
  ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).
- **Monthly: strategy check.** Revisit ICP, positioning, pricing. Kill what
  is not working. Double what is
  ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).
- **Quarterly: allocation.** Move budget and headcount toward the motion
  that produces revenue
  ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).

### Three skills that decide the seat

1. **Strategic analysis.** Defining the ICP, sizing the TAM, mapping
   competitive positioning, setting pricing. This drives every downstream
   decision about budget and targeting
   ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).
2. **Data and AI fluency.** Interpreting intent signals, evaluating AI
   outputs, and knowing when to override automation
   ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).
   A leader who cannot evaluate AI recommendations either trusts bad
   outputs blindly or rejects good ones. Both cost revenue.
3. **Cross-functional influence.** Translating between engineering,
   marketing, sales, and finance, each with its own language and metrics
   ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).

A GTM leader who cannot get a VP of Engineering to prioritize a product
change, or a CMO to reallocate budget, runs the strategy they have, not
the one they need
([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).

## Common mistakes

- **Strategy without authority.** A GTM leader who cannot move budget,
  headcount, or priorities is a deck writer. Fix the mandate or the seat
  does not exist ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).
- **Automating broken processes.** Dropping AI onto a broken funnel
  automates chaos faster. Redesign the workflow first, then automate
  ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).
- **Hiring the head before the motion works.** The first GTM hire is a
  seller, a marketer, or CS, not an executive. A head of GTM with no
  playbook to scale is an expensive passenger
  ([Stage 2 Capital](https://www.stage2.capital/blog/guide-to-hiring-your-first-gtm-leader-what-startups-need-to-know), 2024).
- **Hiring too senior too early.** A big-company leader is unlikely to
  find your first five customers. Hire for your stage, not the stage you
  want ([Stage 2 Capital](https://www.stage2.capital/blog/guide-to-hiring-your-first-gtm-leader-what-startups-need-to-know), 2024).
- **Measuring activity, not outcomes.** Volume metrics reward motion, not
  revenue. Move to outcomes and signal velocity
  ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).
- **Trusting stale data.** Roughly 70% of B2B contact data goes stale
  annually as people change jobs and companies restructure. Automation on
  stale data amplifies errors at scale
  ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/gtm-leader-guide), 2026).
- **Running every motion at once.** Stacking channels and motions dilutes
  focus. Pick the winner and double down. Most startups get the majority
  of traction from a single channel
  ([Traction](https://www.tractionbook.com/), 2014).
