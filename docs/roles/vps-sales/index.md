# VPs of Sales

A VP of Sales owns quota, the team that carries it, and the forecast the company is willing to say out loud. A head of GTM owns the system across marketing, sales, and success [300]. Do not confuse the seats. If you only have one leader, they are doing both jobs and should say so.

This page is the operator guide to the sales seat. Pair it with [Heads of GTM](/docs/roles/heads-of-gtm), [AEs](/docs/roles/aes), [SDRs](/docs/roles/sdrs), and [Outbound](/docs/channels/outbound).

## What the seat owns

- **The number.** Quota design, distribution, and the courage to set it slightly tight rather than fantasy-high. Over-aggressive quotas drive out A-players [279].
- **The forecast.** Stage definitions that match reality. Opportunity-to-close around 21% is a common B2B base [29]. If your funnel assumes 40% and delivers 15%, the VP is not forecasting. They are wishing.
- **The motion mix.** Outbound, inbound, partner. Pipeline coverage healthy bands sit near 3-4x mid-market and 4-5x enterprise [9]. Coverage without conversion is inventory.
- **The inspection system.** Call reviews, opportunity reviews, and hiring scorecards. Dashboard theater is not the job [336].

## Team design

Build in this order: prove the motion, hire the AE or founder-closer, add SDRs only when meetings are the constraint. A fully-loaded SDR costs about $139,120/year [39]. That hire is a multiplier on a working engine, not a substitute for one.

Quota attainment across B2B sat at 27-30% in 2024 [15]. If the whole team misses, look at ICP, offer, and stage math before you look at individual character. If one person carries 80% of revenue, you do not have a team. You have a key-person risk. Top-seller concentration is common. The top 14% of sellers producing 80% of revenue is a documented pattern [339]. The VP's job is to raise the middle without flattening the top.

## Operating rhythm

| Cadence | Forum | Output |
|---------|-------|--------|
| Weekly | Pipeline review on real deals | Pull-ins with evidence, slips with reasons |
| Weekly | Outbound inspection sample | First lines and holds, not send volume |
| Monthly | Forecast lock | A number the CFO can use |
| Quarterly | Territory and quota | Who owns which accounts and why |

Comp is the operating system for GTM, not an HR derivative [337]. If the plan pays on meetings booked with no quality gate, you will get meetings nobody should have taken.

## How the seat differs by stage

- **First VP.** Still in the field. Still reviewing first lines. Still closing a slice.
- **Scale VP.** Multi-team. Forecast integrity and manager quality become the product.
- **Enterprise VP.** Buying committees, longer cycles, multi-thread discipline. Win rates fall as ACV rises [8]. Plan for it.

## Failure modes

- **Managing the dashboard instead of the work** [336].
- **Hiring SDRs to rescue a broken close rate** [328].
- **Sandbagging as a culture.** Quiet extra pipeline that appears on day 28.
- **Being liked by everyone.** The seat requires respect and exits [279].
- **Copying last company's playbook onto this ACV.**

## Agentic layer

Agents can prep deal reviews, flag stalled stages, and draft forecast notes. The VP still owns the number said to the board. See [Agentic GTM overview](/docs/agentic/agentic-gtm-overview).
