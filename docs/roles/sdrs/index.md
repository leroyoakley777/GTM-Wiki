# SDRs

Sales development is the top of the funnel. It turns a defined list of
accounts into qualified conversations for account executives. Done well, it
is a repeatable meeting machine. Done badly, it is a numbers grind that burns
people out and floods the pipeline with garbage.

This page is the operator's guide to the role. It covers what the job is, the
metrics that gate it, how to run it, and the mistakes that quietly kill
pipeline.

---

## Definition

A sales development representative (SDR) books qualified meetings for account
executives. The job runs on phone, email, and LinkedIn in coordinated
sequences against a tight ideal customer profile (SalesHive, 2026). An SDR
fills the top of the pipeline and hands AEs context-rich opportunities to
close (Crunchbase, 2025).

The role splits into two motions:

- **Outbound SDR.** Opens new cold accounts that had no prior exposure to the
  vendor. Usually gets a set of accounts or verticals to penetrate. More
  targeted, more research-heavy.
- **Inbound SDR.** Filters, qualifies, and converts marketing leads into sales
  conversations. Follows up on ebook downloads, webinar attendees, and demo
  requests.

The two jobs need different skills, cadences, and service-level agreements.
Separate them when the volume justifies it. If you cannot split the roles,
give outbound reps protected prospecting time and defend inbound response
speed aggressively (SalesHive, 2026).

The SDR is distinct from the AE. An AE closes. An SDR finds and qualifies.
When founders blur the line, they usually hire closers and call them
prospectors, which collapses the top of the funnel (see
[founders](../founders/index.md) and [AEs](../aes/index.md)).

## Why it matters

SDRs carry real revenue weight. SDR teams generate 30 to 45 percent of new
revenue in many companies (Crunchbase, 2025). A single SDR in SaaS produces
roughly $3 million in pipeline per year (Crunchbase via The Bridge Group,
2025).

Pipeline generated varies by average contract value, but SDRs contribute 46
to 73 percent of pipeline conversion in most orgs (SalesHive, 2026). That is
the machine feeding every AE downstream. When the SDR motion breaks, the
whole funnel starves.

The role also compounds. An SDR who learns to research, qualify, and book at
speed becomes a better AE. The pipeline skills are the same skills, one stage
later. This makes the SDR desk the training ground for the revenue org.

## Metrics that gate the role

Measure outcomes, not activity. Activity volume tells you people are busy.
Outcome metrics tell you the machine works.

| Metric | What it tells you | Benchmark |
|--------|-------------------|-----------|
| Meetings booked | Volume the rep produces | 15 per month, outbound |
| Show rate | Whether booked meetings are real | 80% |
| Meetings held | Net productive conversations | 12 per month |
| SAL to SQL conversion | Whether AEs accept the handoff | Track, no fixed floor |
| Qualified pipeline created | Revenue value of the work | Varies by ACV |
| Dial-to-meeting | Cold-call efficiency | ~2.3% |

The benchmark for a productive outbound SDR is 15 booked meetings per month
with an 80% show rate, which nets 12 meetings actually held. Top performers
book 20 to 25 (SalesHive, 2026). Outbound reps deliver 15 sales-accepted
leads (SALs) per month with about 20 percent drop-off, so the team sees 12
attended meetings (Crunchbase, 2025). One in two SALs leads to a next step in
the sales process.

For inbound, conversion depends on lead intent. Low-intent leads (ebook
downloads, webinar attendees) convert from lead to meeting at 5 to 10
percent. High-intent leads (demo requests, meeting inquiries) convert at 75
to 80 percent (Crunchbase, 2025). Know your intent mix before you judge the
inbound team.

The cold reality underneath: about 83% of SDRs fail to consistently hit quota
each month (SalesHive, 2026). Cold-call connect rates run 3 to 10 percent,
and only about 2.3 percent of dials convert to a booked meeting. Reps log
roughly 94 activities a day, yet spend only about two hours actively selling.
The rest goes to research and admin. That gap is where systems win.

Quota attainment across B2B sales is low and falling. Roughly 43 to 47
percent of reps hit quota (RepVue Cloud Sales Index, Q4 2024). That is the
bar the SDR desk has to beat with a tight process, not heroics.

## SDR operating framework

Run the role as a loop, not a series of chores.

1. **Target.** Work from a defined ICP and account list. Nothing else. A rep
   who chases anything that moves books nothing worth closing. See
   [ICP definition](../../foundations/02-icp-definition.md).
2. **Research.** Build a brief before the first touch. Company, trigger,
   persona, one relevant angle. Research is the base of every good message.
   See [prospecting](../../skills/01-prospecting.md).
3. **Outreach.** Run multi-channel sequences. Email, call, and social in a
   coordinated cadence lift engagement far beyond single-channel effort
   (SalesHive, 2026). See [outbound](../../channels/01-outbound.md) and the
   [outbound flow](../../flows/01-outbound.md).
4. **Qualify.** Filter hard. The rep qualifies the lead in or out before it
   reaches an AE. Clean disqualification protects AE time and win rate.
5. **Hand off.** Pass a context-rich handoff, not a name. The AE needs the
   research, the trigger, and the objection already surfaced.
6. **Loop back.** Capture what worked. Feed messaging, list quality, and
   timing back into the sequence.

The loop is the machine. Every step feeds the next. When one step degrades,
the whole output degrades. The teams that outperform are the ones with the
tightest systems, not the ones dialing the most (SalesHive, 2026).

## How to execute

### Ramp

The average SDR ramp is 3.1 months. Tenure is only about 1.8 years, so a new
hire gets 15 to 17 months of peak productivity before walking out the door
(SalesHive, 2026). Structure compresses ramp. Teams with structured onboarding
get reps productive about 37 percent faster and cut ramp to 6 to 8 weeks.

Use a graduated quota ramp:

- **Month one: learn.** Zero quota. ICP, product, messaging, tools. Reps
  should make live calls by day four, not sit in a classroom for two weeks.
- **Month two: practice.** Ramp to about 50 percent quota. Add complex
  accounts gradually.
- **Month three: perform.** Target 75 percent of quota by week seven and 100
  percent by week twelve.

Setting full quota on day one is one of the most damaging mistakes a manager
can make (SalesHive, 2026).

### Daily motion

An SDR runs a fixed rhythm. Morning research and list building. Scheduled
blocks of calls and outreach. Same-time follow-up. Two hours of selling time
is the real budget, so protect it. Any meeting, CRM entry, or admin task
steals from it. See the [outbound-from-zero playbook](../../playbooks/outbound-from-zero.md)
for the full standing-up sequence.

### Qualification

Qualify in or out fast. The SDR's job is to protect the AE's calendar, not to
fill it. A booked meeting with no fit is a waste of two calendars. Apply the
ICP filters before the meeting is set. See the
[sales process flow](../../flows/sales-process-meddpicc) for the stage
gates the AE runs after the handoff.

### Compensation

Pay a base that dominates. Standard SDR comp is 60 to 70 percent base and 30
to 40 percent commission (Martal, 2026). The 2026 baseline is roughly a
$60,000 median base and $85,000 median OTE (SalesHive, 2026). Base salary by
level runs about $50 to $60K entry, $60 to $75K mid, and $70 to $90K senior
(SV Academy, 2026).

Commission-heavy plans drive burnout, poor lead quality, and turnover
(Martal, 2026). Average SDR turnover runs about 40 percent a year (Martal,
2026). Pay on what the rep controls: meetings held and SQLs accepted by AEs,
not closed-won deals they cannot influence.

### Cost

An in-house SDR carries a fully loaded annual cost of roughly $98K to $173K
plus a three-month ramp, and institutional knowledge walks out the door every
14 to 18 months with turnover (SalesHive, 2026). Vendor sources put a fully
loaded SDR near $139,120 per year (AiSDR, 2026). Know the number before you
hire. Outsourced teams trade control for speed and avoid the ramp.

## Agentic layer

This is where the SDR role is changing fastest. AI-augmented reps handle
volume a human cannot. A manual SDR works 50 to 80 contacts a day; an
AI-augmented rep handles 1,000 or more (SuperAGI, 2026). That shifts the
human's job from dialing to judgment: picking the list, reviewing the
brief, and running the qualified conversation.

The trade-off is real. Autonomous agents convert meetings to opportunities at
a lower rate than strong human SDRs (about 15 percent versus 25 percent in
one vendor comparison), but they run at far higher volume. The economics work
differently. An AI-augmented SDR reaches roughly three times more prospects
with tailored messages than an unaided rep (LevelUpLeads via Smartlead, 2026).

See [agentic outbound](../../flows/02-agentic-outbound.md) for the workflow,
and the [agent-vs-human decision](../../skills/04-signals.md) for what to
automate versus what to keep on a human.

The honest reading: AI removes the rote prospecting volume, but the
qualification judgment and the relationship still need a person. The SDR who
uses the tools to buy back selling time is worth more, not less.

## Career path

SDR is an entry role with a defined ladder:

- SDR to senior SDR, then to AE or to a specialized desk.
- The pipeline skills transfer directly to closing. Research, qualification,
  and objection handling are the AE's core toolkit.
- Some SDRs route into RevOps or sales enablement instead of closing. Both
  are real paths. See [RevOps](../revops/index.md).

Do not let an SDR stagnate. The role burns out fast when there is no upward
path. Promote on outcome, not tenure. A rep who books 20 qualified meetings
a month for a year is ready to close.

## Common mistakes

### 1. Pay on activity, not outcome

Rewarding dials and emails rewards busywork. Pay on meetings held and SQLs
accepted. The rep controls those. See [RevOps](../revops/index.md) for the
compensation plan behind the motion.

### 2. Full quota on day one

Day-one quota kills new hires. Use the graduated ramp. Structure beats speed.

### 3. No tight ICP

A rep chasing everything books nothing. Enforce the ICP. See
[ICP definition](../../foundations/02-icp-definition.md).

### 4. One channel

Email-only or call-only outreach caps results. Multi-channel lifts engagement
sharply (SalesHive, 2026). Run the coordinated sequence.

### 5. Qualify nothing

Booking unqualified meetings burns AE time and crushes win rate. Disqualify
hard and early.

### 6. Ignore the drop-off

If show rate falls, the qualification is weak or the meeting value is unclear.
If SAL-to-SQL conversion falls, the AE is not accepting the handoff. Diagnose
the metric, not the rep.

### 7. No upward path

High turnover is partly a design failure. Give reps a ladder or they leave.
Average tenure of 1.8 years and 40 percent annual turnover are structural,
not inevitable (SalesHive, 2026; Martal, 2026).
