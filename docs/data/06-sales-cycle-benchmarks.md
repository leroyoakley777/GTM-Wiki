# Sales Cycle Benchmarks

**A single "average sales cycle" describes no deal.** This page pairs with the [GTM Funnel](../foundations/07-gtm-funnel.md) and [GTM Metrics](../data/gtm-metrics) — the funnel gives you the stages, metrics gives you the numbers to steer with, and this page tells you *how long each stage actually runs*.

The median B2B SaaS cycle is **84 days** — but that number is close to meaningless on its own. Length tracks deal size and buying-committee size, not the calendar. An SMB deal closes in 2–4 weeks; an enterprise deal runs a quarter or more. Plan per segment, never on one average.

---

## The One Number That Matters Most

**Buying committees grew ~26% since 2020 — from 5.4 to 6.8 stakeholders (and 11–17 for enterprise).**

Every added reviewer adds review cycles, and every cycle adds days. Committee size is now a stronger predictor of cycle length than price alone. If your forecast assumes a deal will close in a quarter but the buyer now has 12 stakeholders to get through, it slips — and your forecast was wrong, not your seller.

## The Insight That Matters: The Committee and the Stage, Not the Calendar

- **Cycle scales with deal size — plan per segment, never on one average.** An SMB deal closes in 2–4 weeks; an enterprise deal runs a quarter or more. Blending a 21-day SMB deal with a 280-day enterprise deal into one "84-day average" describes neither. If a mid-market deal runs 180 days, that is a process problem, not a sales problem.
- **Buying committees grew ~26% since 2020** — from 5.4 to 6.8 stakeholders, and 11–17 for enterprise. Every added reviewer adds review cycles, and every cycle adds days. Committee size is now a stronger predictor of cycle length than price alone.
- **The back half eats the time.** Security, legal, and procurement reviews — SOC 2, vendor risk, order-form redlines — dominate the second half of deals above ~$25K. Where 9–12 stakeholders are involved, legal and security review alone can run 35–40% of total cycle time.
- **A longer cycle is a forecast risk.** If your model assumes an 84-day cycle but enterprise deals actually take 160+, they slip the quarter. Budget the security/procurement stage explicitly or your forecast lies.
- **Expansion is the fast, high-win-rate path.** Existing-customer deals close in ~52 days on average and sellers call them 2.5x easier than new logos. When cycles stretch, expansion revenue is the counterweight.

---

## How to Read These Numbers

1. **Pick one operating definition of cycle start** — first touch vs opportunity created vs qualified. Sources differ on this alone by weeks. Decide yours and be consistent; that is what makes your own trend comparable.
2. **Budget the back half explicitly.** For any deal above ~$25K, add security + legal + procurement time to your forecast. If you are not naming that stage, your enterprise forecast is a guess.
3. **Size your pipeline coverage by segment cycle.** A 90-day enterprise funnel needs more coverage than a 21-day SMB funnel to produce the same revenue in a quarter — the cycle length is the reason.
4. **Benchmark against your own history before judging.** Deal-size bands vary between sources (Optifai vs Focus Digital). Use these as directional, and let your own close data be the reference.

---

## The Pattern Behind the Numbers

Deals do not get longer because sales is slower — they get longer because buyers are more complex. Committees grew 26% since 2020, security and procurement gate the back half of every substantial deal, and cycle length tracks that complexity, not the calendar. The teams that forecast well do not chase a single average; they segment by deal size, budget the back half, and use expansion deals as the fast, high-win-rate counterweight to a stretching new-logo cycle.
