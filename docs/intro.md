# Welcome to the GTM Wiki

The GTM Wiki is an open-source reference for go-to-market: the full set of
decisions and motions that connect a product to paying customers. It is built
for founders, sales leaders, SDRs, AEs, RevOps, marketers, and anyone who runs
a revenue engine. It is deliberately industry- and company-agnostic. Every
framework adapts to any business model and any stage.

This is a reference, not a blog. Each page states a definition, explains why
the topic matters, lays out a framework, and ships the artifact you can run.
Every named statistic carries a numbered footnote that resolves to a dated,
registered source. Nothing here is an unsourced opinion.

## What this wiki is

The wiki answers five questions that every GTM strategy must answer:

1. **Who** do we sell to? (ICP)
2. **What** do we say that makes them care? (positioning and messaging)
3. **Where** do we reach them? (channels and distribution)
4. **How** do we convert them? (sales motion and pricing)
5. **How do we know** it is working? (measurement and iteration)

Each question maps to a section of the wiki. When you read a page, you are
reading the answer to one of these questions, written by an operator, cited to
a source, and delivered with the artifact you need to act.

The name matters. Go-to-market is not a launch deck and not a marketing plan.
It is the whole machine that moves a product to revenue: research, positioning,
channels, sales motion, pricing, measurement, and the systems that tie them
together. That is the territory this wiki covers, and it covers it end to end.

## Why GTM matters

Most startups fail for lack of customers, not lack of product. "Almost every
failed startup has a product. What failed startups don't have is enough
customers." [1] The product is necessary. The motion that finds and closes the
customer is what keeps a company alive.

The market has changed, and the old motion is not keeping up:

- 76% of organizations had to significantly or completely reinvent their GTM
  approach in the past three years [2].
- 70% of the B2B buyer journey is now self-serve [2]. Buyers research,
  shortlist, and often decide before a seller enters.
- 61% of B2B buyers prefer a rep-free buying experience [3].
- 86% of B2B purchases stall during the buying process [4].

Structure changes outcomes. Companies that sell into a clearly defined ICP see
68% higher win rates and 30% more marketing-generated revenue than companies
that do not [5]. The discipline is the advantage.

The systematic methods work at scale. Aaron Ross built a repeatable outbound
system at Salesforce that generated $100M in recurring revenue, where one SDR
feeds two to five quota-carrying AEs [6]. Predictable revenue is a process,
not luck.

GTM is now agentic. An AI-augmented rep handles over 1,000 contacts a day
against 50 to 80 for a manual rep [7], and vendors are shifting the operating
model toward intelligent automation with less manual oversight [8]. This wiki
treats agentic execution as a first-class section, not an add-on.

## How the wiki is organized

The wiki is a graph, not a stack. Pages group by the question they answer:

- **Foundations** cover what stays true. ICP, positioning, pricing, metrics,
  the funnel, RevOps, segmentation, and competitive analysis.
- **Channels** cover where you find customers. Outbound, inbound,
  product-led, partner, community, paid, events, referral, and ABM.
- **Playbooks** cover what to do in a specific situation, start to finish.
- **Flows** are runnable end-to-end motions with artifacts at every step.
- **Agentic GTM** covers how to run those same motions with agents, harnesses,
  and automation, and where the guardrails must stay.
- **Roles** cover who does the work, from founder to SDR to VP to investor.
- **Data** holds named and dated benchmarks behind the decisions.
- **Tools** maps the available tooling to each motion.
- **Case studies** document real outcomes, grouped by channel.
- **GTM Engineering** is the systems backbone: data layer, orchestration,
  skills, CI/CD, observability, and experimentation.

The [Map](./map.md) shows every section and its pages in one view. The
homepage count updates automatically, so the Map always matches what is
published.

### Framework, playbook, and flow

Three page types carry the weight of the wiki, and they are not the same:

- A **framework** tells you how to think. It is durable. The strategy
  framework and the channels bullseye were true in 2015 and still hold.
- A **playbook** tells you what to do against a specific situation. Stand up
  outbound from zero. Build a content engine. Design a sales process.
- A **flow** is a runnable sequence you execute, with a decision tree and an
  artifact at every step. The sales process and outbound flows are the
  reference model.

Read the framework to understand a motion. Copy the flow to run it.

## How to read a page

Every page follows the same anatomy in order: definition, why it matters, the
framework, how to execute, common mistakes, and further reading. Every named
statistic carries a numbered footnote that resolves to a dated source. Every
page where a motion can be run ships the runnable artifact: a template, a
grid, a scoreboard, a prompt, or a worked example with arithmetic.

Two ways to read a page:

- Read the definition and framework when you want to understand a motion.
- Copy the artifact when you want to run it. The artifact is the point. A page
  that describes a motion but does not give you something executable is a gap,
  not a feature.

## GTM as an operating system

The differentiator of this wiki is the operating system view. Modern GTM is not
a collection of tactics. It is a system: data, models, skills, and tools wired
into a governed, self-improving loop. The [GTM OS Architecture](./agentic/00-gtm-os-architecture.md)
page lays out the four-layer model. The [Engagement Flow](./flows/engagement-flow.md)
page shows how a single disposition moves through those layers, from task state
to skill invocation to execution and verification. The [Guardrails and
Measurement](./agentic/03-guardrails-and-measurement.md) page keeps the loop
honest.

Read the OS pages when you want to run GTM as a machine rather than as a series
of one-off campaigns.

## Common mistakes

**Treating the wiki as a list of tactics.** A tactic copied without the
underlying framework is guesswork. Read the framework first, then copy the
artifact.

**Skipping the definition and grabbing the artifact.** The artifact is built on
assumptions about ICP, market, and stage. If your situation differs, the
artifact needs adjusting before you run it.

**Reading footnotes as decoration.** Every footnote names the source and the
date. If a number matters to your decision, open the source. The source is the
claim; the wiki is the synthesis.

**Using benchmarks as targets.** Benchmarks describe what is typical. They are
a sanity check, not a goal. A target that beats the median is fine when your
motion and market support it, but you have to know why.

**Ignoring the agentic layer.** The agentic section is the execution layer for
the time we are in, not a gimmick. If you are planning a GTM motion for the
next 12 months without an agentic component, the plan is already dated.

## How to contribute

The wiki is open source. Contributions are welcome and gated:

- Fix an error or add a source on GitHub.
- Open an issue for a page you want.
- Read [Contributing](./contributing.md) before you add a page. It documents
  the quality bar: every stat cited, every source registered, every artifact
  runnable, every page in operator voice.

The quality gates are machine-enforced. A page that fails the source gate, the
lint gate, or the depth gate does not build. That keeps the bar high and
enforces it in code rather than by review.
