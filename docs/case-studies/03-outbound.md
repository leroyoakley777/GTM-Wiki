# Outbound: Case Studies

Gong signed its first ten customers from the founders' address books.
The next ten came from outbound. That handoff, from *who we know* to
*who we can sell to anyone in ICP*, is the entire case for outbound as
a repeatable growth engine. Run as a multi-touch, multi-channel
sequence against a disciplined ICP, it compounds. Run as cold-email
volume, it burns budget and a sending domain.

This page collects documented outbound motions with named, dated
numbers, then works the mechanics and the math those cases sit on. It
is the *proof* layer. Read the [Outbound channel](/docs/channels/outbound)
for the *where and when*, and the [Outbound Response Rate
benchmarks](/docs/data/outbound-response-rates) for the baselines every
case below beats or sits against.

Every stat carries a named source and an "as of" date. Vendor-sourced
numbers are labeled `(vendor source)`. Where a source defines "reply"
differently, that caveat is flagged, because the same word means
different things across tools.

---

## What outbound is, and when it beats inbound

Outbound is a surgical instrument, not a default. It reaches a named
human who owns a named problem, with a reason to speak now. Inbound waits for the buyer to arrive. Outbound goes to the
buyer with a trigger.

Outbound beats inbound in three conditions, and only there:

- **The ICP is tight and the TAM is small.** You can name the companies
  and the humans who own the problem. Outbound cannot compensate for a
  fuzzy ICP. It just reaches a fuzzy audience faster.
- **The deal value justifies the effort.** Roughly $5k+ ACV. Below
  that, the human cost per meeting eats the deal. A single SDR costs
  about $139,120/year fully loaded (AiSDR, 2026 , vendor source). A
  meeting worth a few hundred dollars cannot pay that back.
- **Inbound will not fill the pipe in the time you have.** Outbound is
  the channel you run while content and product-led motion compound.
  You start the loop now. Inbound and SEO pay off in months.

When none of those hold, build content or partnerships first. The
[Channels Bullseye](/docs/foundations/gtm-channels-bullseye) tells you
which channel deserves the energy before you commit budget.

The reason outbound wins when it wins is structural, not romantic.
Founder deals are a finite list of people the founders already know.
That well runs dry. Outbound turns "who we know" into a repeatable
motion that does not depend on any address book. That is why the
0-to-scale step is precisely when outbound matters most.

## Deep dive: Gong, the mechanism case

The [InsideScale case
study](https://insidescale.com/case-study/gong/) describes Gong at the
moment it had acquired its first ten customers. All ten came from the
founders' relationships, the classic in-network start. InsideScale
reports that Gong then used outbound to source the next ten-plus
customers outside the network, building pipeline it could count on and
compressing the 0-to-scale timeline. Gong went on to a leading position
in the conversation-intelligence category.

The load-bearing detail: Gong did not run this as cold-email volume.
The motion was multi-touch and multi-channel, layered with product-led
tech and sharp messaging. That matches the documented benchmark spread.
Single-channel cold email replies at 3.43% on average (Woodpecker,
2026). Top teams running email plus LinkedIn plus call sequences report
15–25% (Autobound, 100+ SaaS teams). The channel mix is the lever, not
the send count.

Read this case for the mechanism, not the multiples. Outbound replaced
the finite founder relationship well with a repeatable way to reach
anyone in ICP. That is the transferable takeaway. Gong's product and
category timing were exceptional. Copy the motion, not the trajectory.

**Honest caveat:** this is a single, agency-sourced case. InsideScale
reports the narrative. Gong's internal reply and meeting numbers are not
public. It proves the mechanism exists, not any specific return.

## Deep dive: Lavender, the message-quality case

Lavender publishes a cold-email benchmark report built from its own
scoring across 231,818 cold emails (Lavender blog, Mar 2026 , vendor
source). Two findings carry weight.

First, most cold email is bad. Across departments Lavender grades each
email A through F. Only 6.1% of emails to finance earned an A, the
lowest of any persona (Lavender, Mar 2026 , vendor source). For HR,
only 12.3% earned an A, meaning roughly nine of ten emails landing in
HR inboxes have clear, fixable problems (Lavender, Mar 2026 , vendor
source). The average cold email is not working because of the channel.
It is working poorly because the copy is poor.

Second, the fix pays. When a seller writes an A-grade email, reply
rates climb sharply by department. To finance the lift is 79%, the
highest of any persona (Lavender, Mar 2026 , vendor source). To
operations, A-grade copy lifts reply to 5.4%, a 58% gain (Lavender, Mar
2026 , vendor source). To HR, reply climbs to 4.3%, a 27% lift
(Lavender, Mar 2026 , vendor source). The message-quality gap is worth
more than the send-volume gap.

Lavender's earlier guidance frames the ceiling the same way:
personalizing versus sending a template raises reply rates 50–250%
(Lavender, Jan 2023 , vendor source). The ceiling is not the inbox. It
is the effort per row.

**Read with a caveat:** Lavender grades against Lavender's own rubric,
and the numbers describe quality tiers inside its tool, not a neutral
industry benchmark. Use it as evidence that message quality moves
replies. Do not take the tier labels as gospel.

## Deep dive: the agency A/B, personalization wins

The sharpest controlled comparison in this set is a sales-development
agency's generic-versus-personalized test, published by Warmer.ai (2026
, vendor source). Same agency, same campaign, two arms.

| Arm | Reply rate | Meeting booking rate | Meetings booked |
|-----|-----------|----------------------|-----------------|
| Generic template | 4.7% | 0.9% | 45 |
| Personalized | 13.2% | 3.4% | 170 |

Personalization moved reply from 4.7% to 13.2%, a 181% increase, and
meeting booking from 0.9% to 3.4%, a 278% increase. Meetings booked
jumped from 45 to 170 (Warmer.ai, 2026 , vendor source).

The second Warmer.ai case reinforces the same point at a different
scale. A lead-generation agency saw reply move from 2.3% to 11.7%, a
409% improvement, and booked 47 qualified meetings (Warmer.ai, 2026,
vendor source).

Both cases are vendor-sourced and self-reported. Warmer.ai sells AI
personalization, so it has a bias toward showing personalization
winning. The direction is still consistent with the independent
benchmarks in the [response-rates
page](/docs/data/outbound-response-rates): message relevance is what
moves replies, and specific beats generic.

## Mechanics, in order

Every documented win above runs the same five steps. Order matters.
Skip a step and the next one gets more expensive, not faster.

**1. List building.** A 500-row list that matches the ICP beats a
50,000-row spray every time. Every row carries a company with a
problem, not a job title. Enrich in layers: firmographics, then tech
stack, then intent signal, then the one human who owns the problem.
The best lists build on live buyer signals: job changes, funding
rounds, hiring surges, tech-stack changes, competitive displacement.
The full row worksheet lives in the [Outbound from Zero
playbook](/docs/playbooks/outbound-from-zero).

**2. Enrichment.** Static firmographics alone are not enough. Each row
needs a first name, a verified email, and a one-line reason-to-reach:
a trigger, a change, a gap. If you cannot write one sentence about why
*this company now*, the row does not belong on the list. No reason, no
row. That single rule is the difference between a list and a spray.

**3. Sequencing.** The sequence is a conversation opened in writing,
not an essay. Keep each email in the 50–125 word range. Emails at that
length earn roughly 50% higher reply rates than longer formats
(Mailforge and Instantly via Martal, 2026). One ask per email. Value
first. Write like a human, not a marketer. A specific call to action
beats a generic one.

**4. Multi-channel touch.** The single-channel baseline is low and
sinking. Blended B2B cold-email reply sits at 5.8%, down from 6.8% in
2023 (SalesHive, 2025). Top teams running email plus LinkedIn plus call
sequences report 15–25% (Autobound, 100+ SaaS teams). Response is
earned through touch variety and timing, not more of the same email. A
representative cadence: email on day 0, a proof-point email on day 3–4,
a LinkedIn follow on day 5, a different-angle email on day 7–8, a
LinkedIn touch on day 9–10, a breakup on day 12–14.

**5. Meeting booking.** A positive reply must become a booked meeting
without back-and-forth. Link the ask to a scheduling slot with 15
minutes of availability. And handle a hot reply fast: time-to-first
response under 15 minutes is the single biggest lever most teams leave
on the table (Instantly, 2025). The gap between checking an inbox twice
a day and triaging in minutes is where the agentic layer pays for
itself.

## Math, worked on a named base

Reply rate is the vanity number. Meetings are the revenue number. The
path runs delivery → reply → positive reply → meeting, and each stage
is a filter. The product of all four is what you can book.

Run the mid-range benchmarks against a 500-row, ICP-matched list:

| Stage | Rate (named base) | 500 rows |
|-------|-------------------|----------|
| Delivered | 97%+ (Instantly, 2025) | ~485 |
| Replies | 3.43% average (Woodpecker, 2026) | ~17 |
| Positive replies | ~30% of replies (SalesHive, 2025) | ~5 |
| Meetings booked | 3–10 per 1,000 sends (Instantly, 2025) | ~2–5 |

So a 500-row list produces roughly 2–5 booked meetings. That is the
honest number. To hit 20 meetings a month you need roughly 2,000–5,000
quality rows in motion per month. That is why the list is the asset and
why the list-generation loop is the thing you build, not a one-time
export.

Cold email to a signed deal converts at about 0.2%, which is one deal
per roughly 500 cold emails (Focus Digital via Martal, 2026). Response
and revenue are different jobs. Judge the motion on the full path:
reply → meeting → opportunity → deal.

### Per-meeting cost, derived

Put the named bases together and the economics become concrete. A human
SDR costs about $139,120/year fully loaded (AiSDR, 2026 , vendor
source), or roughly $11,593 per month. A manual SDR sends 50–80
contacts a day (SuperAGI, 2026 , vendor source). At an average of 65
sends a day across 21 working days, that is about 1,365 sends a month.
At 3–10 meetings per 1,000 sends (Instantly, 2025), that SDR books
roughly 4–14 meetings a month.

That puts per-meeting cost between roughly $830 and $2,900. The band
explains the $5k ACV floor: if a meeting is worth a few hundred
dollars, you cannot pay for the human effort. Above $5k ACV the math
opens up. Below it, the economics tighten fast.

For contrast, an AI-augmented SDR platform runs $12K–$60K a year and
one augmented rep handles 1,000+ contacts a day versus 50–80 manual
(SuperAGI, 2026 , vendor source). Reported cost-per-lead drops from
$262 to $39, an 85% reduction (MarketsandMarkets, 2026 , vendor
source). Do not read this as buy AI instead of people. The motion scales on
leveraged execution, not raw volume.

## How outbound varies by segment and ACV

The same five steps play out differently by segment. The mechanism is
constant. The ratio of personal research to templated volume flips.

**SMB, low ACV ($2M ARR, sub-$5k deals).** Outbound often does not
pay here. The human cost per meeting eats the deal. If you run it, you
need high volume, low touch, heavy templating, and a self-serve path
after the first positive signal. Automate as much as possible or skip
outbound for content and partnerships.

**Mid-market, $5k–$30k ACV ($4M–$15M ARR).** This is the sweet spot.
The list is the asset. One or two SDRs run 500-row lists against a
tight ICP, with signal-driven reasons to reach. Personalization sits at
the reason-to-reach line, not a full research brief per account. The
worked example below lives in this band.

**Enterprise, $50k+ ACV ($50M+ ARR).** Fewer accounts, far more
research per account. Multi-threading across stakeholders replaces
single-contract volume. Email is one leg of a motion that leans on
calls, LinkedIn, and account-based orchestration, closer to the [ABM
channel](/docs/channels/abm). The sequence is longer, the buyers are a
committee, and the deal cadence runs in months, not weeks. The
15–25% multi-channel reply range (Autobound, 100+ SaaS teams) is the
documented ceiling for this high-touch band.

The rule that holds across all three: tighten the ICP before you scale,
and let ACV decide how much human effort per account is worth spending.

## Failure modes and fixes

These are the documented ways outbound dies, each with the fix. Every
one maps to a number in the case matrix above.

**Spray-and-pray.** Buying a list without reasons-to-reach returns no
replies. Fix: the no-reason-no-row gate. If you have 500 rows and 300
lack a reason-to-reach, you have a 200-row list plus 300 spray rows.
Cut the 300.

**A bad list.** Firmographics alone, no live signal, no verified email.
Fix: build on intent and job-change data, verify every email, and drop
any row without a first name. The list is the asset. A bad asset
produces a bad motion regardless of copy.

**Generic copy.** Template email at template volume returns template
numbers. The agency A/B above is the proof: generic replied at 4.7%,
personalized at 13.2% (Warmer.ai, 2026 , vendor source). Fix: personal
research on the top 20% of the list, a signal-specific line on the rest.

**No follow-up cadence.** Ambition emailed 578 prospects and got 6
initial replies. Follow-ups added 67 more, for about 12.6% total
response (Ambition, 2015). Most buyers who respond need to be met more
than once. Fix: a full sequence with follow-ups, then a breakup.

**Scaling volume before proving the message.** You get ten times the
noise and a burned domain. Fix: prove a variant on a small list first,
and do not optimize until you have 300+ sends per variant. Small
samples produce noise, and noise produces confident wrong answers.

**Skipping domain warmup.** A fresh sending domain sent at full volume
lands in spam or gets blacklisted, and a burned domain is lost forever.
Fix: SPF, DKIM, and DMARC all passing, two-plus weeks of warmup, and a
25–50 emails-per-day cap per domain during launch (Instantly, 2025).

**No handoff discipline.** Meetings get booked but nobody qualifies
them, nothing closes, and the motion looks broken when it was only
unfinished. Fix: write the meeting standard, trace every meeting to a
campaign, and debrief every two weeks.

**Treating a 3% reply rate as failure.** 3.43% is the average cold-email
reply rate (Woodpecker, 2026). Fix: compare against the baseline and
the channel mix, not against a fictional 25% someone quoted. Chase
reply through personalization and touch variety, not by sending more of
the same.

## Measurement

Judge the motion on pipeline and meetings, not on sends. Emails sent
are vanity. Meetings and sourced pipeline are revenue. The
meetings-per-1,000-sends metric, with 3–10 healthy (Instantly, 2025),
compresses the whole funnel into one diagnostic.

| Metric | Healthy range | Source (as of) | What it tells you |
|--------|---------------|----------------|-------------------|
| Delivery rate | over 97% | Instantly, 2025 | Domain health and list hygiene |
| Open rate (cold) | 50–70% | OptifAI, 2026 | Subject line and sender trust |
| Reply rate (cold email) | 3.43% average; 5–10% good; 10%+ excellent | Woodpecker, 2026 | Message relevance |
| Positive reply rate | over 30% of replies | SalesHive, 2025 | ICP fit |
| Meetings booked / 1,000 sends | 3–10 | Instantly, 2025 | Overall motion health |
| Cold email → deal | ~0.2% (~1 deal per 500) | Focus Digital via Martal, 2026 | Response is not revenue |

Diagnose before you scale. Delivery under 97% is a domain or list
problem. Open under 50% is a subject-line or sender-trust problem.
Reply under 3% is a message or audience problem. Fix the broken stage.
Adding volume to a broken stage just scales the leak. Benchmark against
your own campaign history first. Industry ranges are the map, and your
history is the territory.

## Agentic layer: an SOP an agent can run

Outbound is where agentic GTM has the most proven leverage, because the
bottleneck has never been sending. The thinking per row and the speed
of reply handling are the constraint. Agents remove that bottleneck. Humans keep the
judgment. The funnel metrics above hold with an agent in the loop. The
full treatment is [Agentic
Outbound](/docs/flows/agentic-outbound).

The win has numbers behind it. Signal-driven personalization lifts
reply rates to 15–25% versus the 3–5% single-channel baseline (Instantly,
2025). AI-augmented teams see 2.8x more pipeline than teams attempting
full replacement of their SDRs (Autobound, 2026 , vendor source).

The rule that never changes: the gate stays, the execution layer
changes. Humans keep approval. Agents remove the thinking bottleneck at
volume.

| Motion step | Agent does | Human approves |
|-------------|-----------|----------------|
| Account research | Pulls firmographics, tech stack, live signals for each row | The list before any send |
| Personalized open | Drafts a reason-to-reach grounded in a research brief | The pattern, not every keystroke |
| Sequence execution | Handles send-time, warmup, domain rotation, sending caps | The sending policy |
| Reply triage | Classifies intent, drafts responses in under 5 minutes | Hot replies before send; the 15-minute SLA becomes automatic |
| Meeting booking | Routes a positive reply to a slot, sends the calendar link | The meeting standard |

### One-prompt starter for account research and openers

```
You are building a cold-outbound list for {ICP} companies in the
{segment} band using {data source}. For each account, return a row
with: company, ICP-fit score (1–5), primary contact first name,
verified email, role, and a one-line reason-to-reach grounded in a
live signal (job change, funding, hiring surge, tech-stack change,
or competitive displacement). Skip any row with no reason-to-reach:
the gate is "no reason, no row." Then, for each top-fit row, draft a
three-sentence opening email: one line referencing the live signal,
one line of value specific to that account, and one ask. Keep each
opener under 90 words. Return only rows and openers that pass the
gate.
```

### Guardrails that keep it honest

1. **Human approval before scaling.** Only automate a reply pattern
   after you have seen hundreds of replies prove it. A weak message
   scaled by an agent dies faster.
2. **Never burn the domain.** Agents make it easy to send more, faster.
   The 25–50 sends-per-day cap and the 97%+ delivery gate still apply.
3. **Track meetings and pipeline, not emails sent or replies handled.**
   Volume is vanity. Meetings are revenue.

## Worked example, end to end

A representative build so the numbers hold together. Names and figures
are illustrative but plausible, and every benchmark used is named and
dated above.

**Company:** a $4M ARR SaaS in construction-project management. No
outbound engine. **ICP:** construction general contractors and subs
with 20–200 employees tracking projects in spreadsheets. **ACV:**
roughly $12k. **One SDR:** the founder's first hire.

**Step 1: decide.** Tight ICP, $12k ACV above the $5k floor, no
inbound engine. Outbound is the right call.

**Step 2: build the list.** A 500-row list via Clay with Apollo as a
data source. Each row enriched to a first name, a verified email, and a
reason-to-reach (a project-manager hire, recent funding, an RFP, a
public pain mention). 100 rows marked perfect-fit for personal research.

**Step 3: tooling and deliverability.** A dedicated sending domain
with SPF, DKIM, and DMARC all passing. Two weeks of warmup. A 30
sends-per-day cap to start.

**Step 4: sequence.** The four-email sequence from the playbook, each
under 90 words, with LinkedIn touches on days 5 and 9–10. One ask per
email.

**Step 5: run and measure.** Send to the 100 perfect-fit rows first.
At 300+ sends per variant, the reply rate was 4.1%, above the 3.43%
average (Woodpecker, 2026). Delivery held at 98%, above the 97% gate
(Instantly, 2025). Meetings booked ran 3–4 per 1,000 sends, inside the
healthy 3–10 band (Instantly, 2025). Positive-reply rate ran about 35%
of replies, above the 30% line (SalesHive, 2025). Keep the winning
variant and scale to the remaining 400 rows.

**Step 6: handoff.** Write the meeting standard (a GC with the problem
and budget, scheduling a demo). Tag every meeting to its campaign.
Start a two-week debrief. Feed the best customers into a new
look-alike list.

**Result:** within roughly eight weeks, about 15–20 qualified meetings
from roughly 2,000 rows in motion, a domain still healthy, and a
repeatable list loop. Nothing burned, nothing scaled before it was
proven.
