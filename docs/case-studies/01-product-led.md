# Product-Led Growth: Case Studies

Product-led growth is the channel where the product does the selling. A
free tier delivers working value to a user before any sales conversation.
The user's own output (a booking link, a shared board, a message
archive, a published design) then carries the pitch to the next user.
No rep touches the top of the funnel.

This page proves the motion with seven documented builds: Slack,
Calendly, Figma, Dropbox, Zoom, Notion, and Atlassian. Every number
carries a named source and an "as of" date. Vendor- and agency-sourced
figures carry the `(vendor source)` label. Read the
[Product-Led Growth channel page](/docs/channels/product-led) first for
the *where and how*: the funnel, the trial-model tradeoffs, and the
benchmark bands this case proves possible. Read
[Funnel Benchmarks](/docs/data/funnel) for the leakage math, and
[Pricing as GTM](/docs/data/pricing-as-gtm) for how the free tier and
upgrade trigger fit the pricing model.

---

## What the motion is

Product-led growth rests on one load-bearing idea: **the thing a user
creates in your product is the thing that sells your product.** Slack
keeps a team's message history in the product, so the team keeps coming
back and the workspace pulls in new members. Calendly turns a booking
link into a shareable artifact. Figma stores design files and lets anyone
on the link view them, so a designer handing off to an engineer carries
the product across a team boundary. Dropbox gives a shared folder, and
the folder travels by email. Zoom's meeting link is its own invitation.

The mechanism has three moving parts, and every company on this page runs
all three:

- **The product's output is the distribution channel.** What the user
  makes and shares is the ad. It is placed by the person who just got
  value, in front of someone who already sees the context.
- **The free tier constrains value.** It delivers enough to feel the
  value, then hits a limit that nudges toward paying: a seat cap, a
  message archive cap, a 40-minute meeting cap, a storage cap. The limit
  is the upgrade trigger.
- **Activation and sharing velocity are the leading metrics.** Signups
  measure reach. Whether a user hits the first value moment fast, and
  whether that moment produces something shareable, decides whether the
  loop spins.

The number that matters most, from the channel page: **a good freemium
self-serve free-to-paid rate is 3–5%, a great one 6–8%, and a good
free-trial rate is 8–12% with great at 15–25%**
([Lenny's Newsletter × OpenView × Pendo, 1,000+ product survey, Aug
2023](https://openviewpartners.com/2023-product-benchmarks/)). Every case
below is proof that a product can clear those bands at scale.

### Slack: the workspace is the distribution

Slack gave teams a free tier generous enough to feel the value (channels,
search, integrations), then let the shared workspace pull colleagues in.
Every new member joins the same history, so the team compounds its value
in the product instead of leaking it to email.

The documented numbers:

- **12+ million people actively using Slack daily, and more than 6
  million paid seats**, as of September 2019, reported by Slack itself
  ([Slack newsroom, Sept
  2019](https://slack.com/blog/news/work-is-fueled-by-true-engagement)).
- **640,000 organizations used Slack in 2019**, with a majority on the
  free tier, per Business of Apps' Slack statistics compilation
  ([Business of Apps, Slack statistics, 2026](https://www.businessofapps.com/data/slack-statistics/)).
- **Roughly 40% of Slack's paid customers started on the free tier**
  (`(vendor source)`, ideaplan.io's PLG guide). The free tier is the
  feeder into the paid base, not a separate audience.

Slack's upgrade trigger is the free tier's limit on what a growing team
can keep. Message history beyond the free window and the full app
integration set sit behind the paid plan, so a team that scales past
about 10,000 searchable messages feels the squeeze at the moment it has
already committed to Slack internally
([Slack pricing, free vs paid, 2026](https://slack.com/pricing/paid-vs-free)).

### Calendly: the booking link is the ad

Calendly's entire product is a shareable output. A user sets their
availability, shares a link, and every person who books a meeting sees
Calendly's value before they sign up. The viral loop sits inside the core
use case, which is why it ran with almost no paid acquisition.

The documented numbers:

- **Calendly raised a $350 million Series B at a $3 billion valuation in
  January 2021** from OpenView and ICONIQ, after years of bootstrapping
  to profitability ([TechCrunch, Jan
  2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/26/daily-crunch-calendly-valued-at-3b/#:~:text=Series%20B%20round%20at%20a%20valuation%20of%20%243%20billion),
  cited by [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/report/calendly)).
- **86% of the Fortune 500 use Calendly**, and Calendly for Enterprise
  grew 61% year over year as of July 2024, per Calendly's own newsroom
  ([Calendly press release, 2024](https://calendly.com/newsroom/press-release/calendly-achieves-61-yoy-increase-in-enterprise-growth)).
  This is a `(vendor source)` figure.
- **20 million users across 230 countries**, a self-reported figure
  widely repeated in 2025 coverage (`(vendor source)`, via [press,
  2025](https://medium.com/@ehoneahobed/the-scheduling-tool-that-became-a-3b-empire-what-calendly-taught-me-about-building-startups-ac8ef56887d2)).

Calendly's free tier constrains a power user: one calendar connection,
limited event types, and per-event branding that only goes away on a paid
plan. The trigger is the Calendly brand on a meeting you keep sending. It
turns a self-serving user into a brand ambassador with zero product
changes: just a white-label paywall.

### Figma: the file is the handoff and the hook

Figma won the design-to-development boundary. A designer shares a file,
and an engineer or product manager opens it on the free tier to view,
comment, and inspect. Each cross-functional share is a distribution event
that crosses a team boundary no inside-out sales motion could reach. The
network effect is the file: the more stakeholders a file touches, the
more value Figma holds.

The documented outcome:

- **Adobe agreed to acquire Figma for roughly $20 billion in September
  2022**, a cash-and-stock deal
  ([Adobe / Reuters, Sept 2022](https://www.reuters.com/technology/adobe-agrees-acquire-figma-20-billion-2022-09-15/)).
  Adobe abandoned the deal in December 2023 under regulatory pressure,
  and Figma continued as an independent company
  ([Reuters, Dec 2023](https://www.reuters.com/legal/adobe-figma-deal-likely-be-blocked-eu-2023-12-18/)).

Figma's free tier is generous on purpose (unlimited files and unlimited
personal access) because the constraint is on *seats inside a shared
team space*, not on a single user's output. A design team that needs
shared libraries, versioned permissions, and admin controls upgrades. The
constraint is team-scale, which is why adoption bottoms-up into
organizations.

### Dropbox: the two-sided referral loop

Dropbox is the reference case for a mechanical referral loop bolted onto
a shared-output product. In 2008 the product was burning roughly $300 to
acquire a customer worth about $99 per year, so it rewired growth around
the act of sharing a folder.

The documented numbers, drawn from Dropbox's own growth presentation by
Sean Ellis and repeated across PLG analyses:

- **Registered users went from 100,000 in September 2008 to 4 million in
  December 2009: a 3,900% increase in 15 months**
  ([Sean Ellis, SaaStr Annual deck, 2016](https://www.slideshare.net/seanellis/building-a-companywide-growth-culture-saastr-annual-2016),
  cited by [saasquatch, 2024](https://www.saasquatch.com/blog/dropbox-customer-referral-program-by-the-numbers/)).
- **35% of all daily signups came from referrals at the program's peak**
  (same source).
- **Referrals drove roughly 2.8x more signups than Dropbox's paid
  channels at the time** ([getlaunchlist, Dropbox referral case
  study](https://getlaunchlist.com/blog/dropbox-referral-program-case-study)).

The mechanics were simple and two-sided: each user got 500 MB of
permanent free storage for every successful referral, capped at 16 GB
(later 32 GB). Both sides got paid in the product's own currency (storage), so the referrer's incentive was a better product, not a
discount. By September 2017 Dropbox passed 500 million registered users
with 33.9 million paying and a $10 billion valuation
([viral-loops, Dropbox growth history, 2025](https://viral-loops.com/blog/dropbox-grew-3900-simple-referral-program/)).

### Zoom: the meeting link is its own invitation

Zoom's free tier offered unlimited one-on-one meetings and 40-minute
group meetings. The 40-minute limit is the textbook upgrade trigger: it
bites at the moment a working team is mid-meeting and does not want to
stop. Zoom grew on meeting links that users sent each other.

The documented numbers:

- **Daily meeting participants grew from 10 million in December 2019 to
  200 million in March 2020 to 300 million in April 2020: roughly 30x in
  four months** ([Zoom blog, April 2020](https://www.zoom.com/en/blog/reflecting-looking-ahead/)).
- **Reuters corrected the framing: the figure is 300 million daily
  meeting *participants*, not 300 million daily active users**. A single
  user counted in several meetings inflates the participant count
  ([Reuters, April 2020](https://www.reuters.com/article/business/zoom-says-it-has-300-million-daily-meeting-participants-not-users-idUSKBN22C1IE/)).
  Read the number as reach, not as unique users.
- **Zoom closed 2024 with 192,600 enterprise customers**, per Business of
  Apps' Zoom statistics ([Business of Apps, Zoom statistics, 2026](https://www.businessofapps.com/data/zoom-statistics/)).

Zoom's pandemic growth was a tailwind no playbook can reproduce. The
*mechanism* (a time-capped free tier that triggers mid-value) is
portable.

### Notion: B2C2B, free and quiet

Notion grew a workspace product the way a consumer app grows. Individuals
adopt it free for personal notes, hit a team collaboration need, and
bring their own workspace into a company. The free plan caps guest count
and block storage to nudge a growing team to Plus.

The documented numbers:

- **Notion went from 20 million users in 2022 to over 100 million by
  2025, with more than 4 million paying customers** ([CNBC Disruptor
  50, May 2023](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/05/09/notion-disruptor-50.html);
  [Notion, "100 Million of You," 2025](https://www.notion.com/blog/100-million-of-you),
  cited by [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/report/notion)).
- **Roughly 95% of Notion's growth to 20 million users came organically**,
  per multiple growth analyses of founder interviews (`(vendor source)`,
  [growth analyses, 2025](https://letters.ehoneahobed.com/how-notion-grew-to-20-million-users-with-95-organic-growth-and-why-their-boldest-move-almost-7b0031de15d1)).
- **More than half of the Fortune 500 used Notion by 2025**, a
  `(vendor source)` claim repeated in press ([CNBC, June 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/06/10/notion-cnbc-disruptor-50.html)).
- **Notion raised a $275 million Series C in October 2021 at a $10
  billion valuation** ([Contrary Research, Notion report](https://research.contrary.com/report/notion)).

Notion's growth is B2C2B: the individual is the wedge, the team is the
expansion. It is the cleanest case of a product-led motion that does not
depend on a shareable *external* artifact: the workspace itself is the
shared object.

### Atlassian: seat-capped freemium and land-and-expand

Atlassian made freemium a seat game. Jira and Confluence are free for
teams of up to 10 users, with 2 GB of storage. Jira Service Management is
free for up to 3 agents. The moment a team passes those caps, it needs a
paid Standard plan. The seat cap is the entire upgrade trigger, and it
pairs with a land-and-expand motion where a free team of 5 grows into a
paid team of 500.

The documented design:

- **Atlassian's Free cloud plans for Jira and Confluence allow up to 10
  users, Jira Service Management up to 3 agents, with 2 GB of storage and
  community support** ([Atlassian Cloud Licensing
  docs](https://www.atlassian.com/licensing/cloud)). A free site is a seed
  for a paid site.
- **Trello's Free plan allows up to 10 boards per workspace and up to 10
  workspace collaborators**, with unlimited cards ([Trello
  pricing](https://www.trello.com/pricing)).

Atlassian is the enterprise-facing end of the PLG spectrum: free adoption
bottoms-up inside a company, then procurement and admin tools (SSO, audit
logs, admin controls) only exist on paid tiers. PLG and enterprise sales
are not opposites: the free product wins the seats, and the enterprise
product wins the company.

## Mechanical pattern under all seven

Strip the company names and seven builds collapse into one pattern:

1. **The product's output is the distribution channel.** Slack's message
   history, Calendly's booking link, Figma's file, Dropbox's shared
   folder, Zoom's meeting link, Notion's workspace, Atlassian's shared
   board. If the product produces something another person must open, you
   have a built-in acquisition channel. The question is whether you
   engineer it on purpose.
2. **The free tier constrains value.** Every free tier here is generous
   to the individual and restrictive at a growth boundary: a seat cap, a
   message or storage cap, a time cap, a white-label wall. The
   restriction lands where a working user starts needing the product to
   do more.
3. **Activation and sharing velocity are the leading metrics.** Activation
   is whether the user reaches first value fast. Sharing velocity is
   whether first value produces something another person opens. Both
   predict free-to-paid conversion better than signup volume.

The channel page states the counterweight: **freemium products sign up
about 9% of visitors and free-trial products about 5%**, because the time
pressure of a trial makes people wait until they are serious
([Lenny's × OpenView × Pendo, 2023](https://openviewpartners.com/2023-product-benchmarks/)).
All seven cases are freemium, not trial: they accept a lower paid yield
per signup in exchange for a wider distribution surface, and they pay for
it with expansion and referral volume instead of direct conversion.

## Math, worked against named bases

The single most quoted PLG number is **a free-to-paid conversion of about
9% median, rising to about 25–30% when PQLs route the funnel**
([ProductLed, 600+ B2B SaaS survey](https://productled.com/blog/product-led-growth-benchmarks)).
The channel page's sharper slice ([Lenny's × OpenView × Pendo, Aug
2023](https://openviewpartners.com/2023-product-benchmarks/)) breaks it
by model:

| Model | Good band | Great band |
|---|---|---|
| Freemium, self-serve | 3–5% | 6–8% |
| Freemium, sales-assist | 5–7% | 10–15% |
| Free trial | 8–12% | 15–25% |

Now work it with a named base. Take a freemium product with **100,000
active free accounts added in one quarter**. At the freemium self-serve
band of 3–5%, those accounts produce **3,000 to 5,000 paying accounts**
over the next six months.

Now route the activated accounts through a PQL gate. **Free trials that
use PQLs convert 2.8x higher than trials that do not**
([Gainsight, Product-Led Growth Index, 2022](https://www.gainsight.com/resource/benchmark-product-qualified-lead-pql-conversion-rates/)).
Assume 30,000 of the 100,000 hit the activation trigger: a 30%
activation rate. If those qualified 30,000 convert inside the 25–30% band
the PQL route buys, they alone yield **7,500 to 9,000 paying accounts**,
roughly double the best case of converting the whole base at the
self-serve rate.

The arithmetic that makes conversion worth fixing: **a 1-point lift in
free-to-paid conversion is worth roughly +15% new revenue per trial
cohort**, and because it benefits every future cohort, it compounds in a
way a one-time campaign never does ([ChartMogul / Userpilot, SaaS
conversion rates](https://userpilot.com/blog/saas-average-conversion-rate/)).
Moving freemium conversion from 3% to 5% doubles the revenue you extract
from the same free signup volume at zero added acquisition cost.

The funnel page's discipline applies here too: the leakage is
multiplicative, not additive. Two stages at 50% each give you 25% end to
end. Attack the stage that leaks the largest *absolute* count: in a
freemium funnel that is usually signup-to-activation, not
visitor-to-signup.

## Failure modes, and the fixes

The happy path is well documented. The failures are what most teams hit,
so they get equal treatment here.

### PLG stall: the loop never turns

**Failure:** the product acquires free users but the shared-output loop
never spins. Signups climb, then flatten, because nothing the user makes
reaches another person. A spike, not a loop.

**Fix:** build the viral mechanic deliberately. Ask what single output a
user produces that another person must open, then instrument it. If the
answer is "nothing," you are running a funnel, not a loop, and you should
price acquisition accordingly. Dropbox did not hope for referrals. It
shipped a 500 MB reward on a shared folder.

### Free tier too generous: nobody pays

**Failure:** the free tier delivers the full product and the upgrade
trigger is absent or cosmetic. Slack would not convert if it kept the
whole message archive. Figma would not convert teams if shared libraries
were free.

**Fix:** find the constraint that lands at the growth boundary and cap
it. The best caps are seat, storage, archive, or time limits that bite at
the moment a user is mid-value: Zoom's 40-minute group meeting, Jira's
10-user cap. Ship the upgrade nudge at the limit, not a banner. Measure
paying-per-1k-visitors, not the size of the free load.

### Upgrade trigger too weak

**Failure:** the product has a free tier and a paid tier but the moment
between them is invisible. No plan-limit moment fires, so free users never
feel the wall.

**Fix:** make the trigger a product event, not a marketing nudge. When a
user hits a seat, storage, or feature cap, the product should surface the
upgrade inside the flow, at the moment of need. Calendly's white-label
paywall and Slack's archive cap are product events that fire at the right
time.

### Activation too slow

**Failure:** users sign up but never reach first value. The free tier
feels empty because the aha moment sits behind setup work or a blank
screen. All downstream conversion dies here.

**Fix:** define the activation moment in one sentence and instrument it.
Standard candidates from the channel page: created the first document,
ran the first report, invited the first teammate, produced the first
output. Fix activation before you buy traffic: buying visitors for a
funnel that leaks at the aha moment is paying to speed up a waste.

### Sales on every signup: PLG stops being PLG

**Failure:** sales starts touching every signup, the self-serve unit
economics collapse, and the company becomes a sales-led company with a
free trial bolted on. Roughly **44% of free-trial companies already have
sales reach out to more than half their signups**, double the freemium
rate of 24% ([Lenny's × OpenView × Pendo, 2023](https://openviewpartners.com/2023-product-benchmarks/)).

**Fix:** sales enters late and only on users the product has qualified.
The PQL trigger is the gate: the product signal, not a request. On the
product-qualified set, conversion roughly triples, so routing the right
free users to a human is a high-leverage act, not a loss of purity.

### No expansion motion

**Failure:** the company counts free-to-paid as the whole story and
ignores the second funnel: seats, tiers, and add-ons on the paid base.
Free-to-paid is capped, but net revenue retention is not.

**Fix:** treat expansion as the second funnel. The healthy benchmark is
**25–40% of new revenue from expansion, with top performers above 60%**
([MomentumNexus, SaaS pricing guide, 2026](https://www.momentumnexus.com/blog/saas-pricing-strategy-guide-2026/)).
Slack's seat growth and Atlassian's 10-user-to-500-seat expansion are the
model: land small, expand through the team that already adopted the
product.

## How the motion varies by segment, stage, and maturity

PLG is not one motion. It splits cleanly by who you sell to and how far
the company has matured.

### SMB self-serve vs enterprise-led expansion

| Dimension | SMB self-serve | Enterprise-led expansion |
|---|---|---|
| Primary buyer | Individual / small team | Department buying an org-wide rollout |
| Free tier job | Deliver value, self-convert | Deliver value, then expand seats org-wide |
| Sales role | Rare, on high-signal PQLs only | Central: land small, expand through champions |
| Key metric | Free-to-paid conversion | NRR and seat/license expansion |
| Case pattern | Calendly's shared link, Dropbox's folder | Figma's design systems, Atlassian's seat caps |
| Enterprise layer needed | None | SSO/SAML, security review, procurement, admin controls |

The failure that spans both is treating the enterprise motion as a bigger
SMB motion. Enterprise-led expansion needs SSO, audit, and procurement
support that the SMB playbook does not provide. If you chase enterprise
deals, add the enterprise-ready features before you call the motion PLG.

### PLG-first vs sales-assisted PLG

Pure self-serve PLG (Calendly, Dropbox early) never routes a free user to
a human. Sales-assisted PLG (Figma at enterprise scale, Atlassian, Slack
at scale) uses the product to win the top of the funnel, then lands the
qualified few through sales. The data is clear about which converts
better: **sales-assist freemium converts at 10–15% versus 3–8% for pure
self-serve** at the same product ([Lenny's × OpenView × Pendo,
2023](https://openviewpartners.com/2023-product-benchmarks/)). PLG is not
"no sales." It is sales only where the product has proven fit.

### Maturity: what changes as a PLG company scales

- **Seed-to-early:** the founder is the first marketer, the free tier is
  a wedge, and the activation event is hand-validated across dozens of
  onboardings. Phase 0 of the channel page's sequencing.
- **Scaling:** the funnel is instrumented, activation is defined, PQLs
  route the qualified few. Phase 1 and Phase 2.
- **Enterprise:** SSO, security, procurement, and a land-and-expand sales
  motion on the activated base. Phase 3, where NRR and the viral
  coefficient become the leading metrics.

## Agentic layer: an SOP an agent can run

PLG is where agents earn their keep on qualification and conversion,
because the free-user volume is enormous and the per-user attention
budget is near zero. The full operating model lives on
[Agentic GTM](/docs/agentic/agentic-gtm-overview), with guardrails on
[Guardrails and Measurement](/docs/agentic/guardrails-and-measurement).
Three operations map directly onto this channel.

### Operation 1: PQL detection and routing

**The prompt:**

```
You are the PQL router for {product}. Pull product usage for every free
account created in the last 90 days. For each account, return: account,
activation reached (yes or no), the trigger event and its timestamp,
usage thresholds crossed, seats and sharing activity, and a one-line
reason this account is a qualified handoff. Route only accounts that hit
the activation trigger and show a buying signal. The gate is product
signal, not request. Return only records that pass, ranked by fit.
```

**The human approves:** the trigger definition and the routing policy
once, not every handoff. Review outcomes weekly and tighten the trigger
on evidence.

### Operation 2: activation monitoring

**The prompt:**

```
You are the activation monitor for {product}. Each {cadence}, pull the
signup-to-activation rate for new accounts by cohort. Report: (1) the
activation rate by cohort and how it is trending; (2) the single largest
absolute drop between visitor, signup, activation, and pay stages, not
the most striking percentage; (3) the likely cause (onboarding friction,
a missing trigger event, a weak upgrade nudge) with the evidence you
used; (4) a one-line proposed fix. Do not recommend buying more traffic
if activation is leaking. Flag any stage below 50% of its cohort
baseline.
```

**The human approves:** the alert threshold and the fix before it ships.

### Operation 3: expansion and NRR flagging

**The prompt:**

```
You are the expansion monitor for {product}. Each {cadence}, scan the
paid base for expansion triggers: seat growth above a threshold, plan-limit
hits, usage spikes on a paywalled feature, or new team members invited
from a paid seat. For each flag, return the account, the trigger, its
timestamp, and a one-line expansion recommendation. Report the share of
new MRR from expansion against the 25-40% healthy band. Flag only genuine
expansion signals, not noise.
```

**The human approves:** the expansion play and the threshold definitions.

**Three guardrails that keep it honest:**

1. **Instrument before you automate.** If the agent misses activation
   events because analytics are thin, the PQL gate is fake. Ship product
   analytics before the agent routes a single lead.
2. **Human approval on policy, not every record.** Define the trigger
   once, let the agent execute, review outcomes weekly.
3. **Never sell to un-activated users at scale.** An agent that routes
   every signup to sales turns a self-serve motion back into a leaky
   sales-led one. The product signal stays the gate.

## A worked example with named bases

A hypothetical freemium product (call it "Flowboard," a shared
whiteboard) benchmarked against the named rates on this page and the
channel page. Names and numbers are illustrative, the method is the
point.

**The setup.** Flowboard has a free tier capped at 10 editors per board
and 5 private boards, mirroring Atlassian's seat-cap design. Users share
boards by link, mirroring Figma's file and Calendly's link mechanics.

**The funnel, in one quarter.** Flowboard adds **100,000 free accounts**
in a quarter. Against the freemium visitor-to-signup rate of about 9%
([Lenny's × OpenView × Pendo, 2023](https://openviewpartners.com/2023-product-benchmarks/)),
that implies roughly 1.1 million visitors. Now run the leaks:

| Stage | Rate (named base) | Accounts in | Accounts out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visitor → signup | ~9% freemium (Lenny's 2023) | 1,100,000 | ~100,000 signups |
| Signup → activation | 30% (assumed, on-boarded) | 100,000 | ~30,000 activated |
| Activated → paid (self-serve) | 3–5% freemium (Lenny's 2023) | 30,000 | ~900–1,500 paying |
| Activated → paid (with PQL route) | 25–30% on qualified set (ProductLed; Gainsight 2.8x) | 30,000 | ~7,500–9,000 paying |

**The read.** Only a third of signups activate, so signup-to-pay is not
where the leverage is. The fix order, from the funnel page: instrument
activation, fix the onboarding path so more than 30% reach first value,
then route activated users through a PQL gate so the qualified 30,000
convert in the 25–30% band instead of the 3–5% band. Each 1-point lift in
free-to-paid is worth roughly +15% revenue per cohort
([ChartMogul / Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/saas-average-conversion-rate/)),
so a 3-point lift from PQL routing more than doubles paid revenue from
the same traffic.

**The trigger.** Flowboard's free tier caps private boards at 5. A team
that passes 5 boards hits the wall mid-value (the Zoom 40-minute moment)
and sees an upgrade nudge inside the board, not a banner. That is the
plan-limit event that converts.

---

## Honest caveat

Every number on this page that comes from a vendor or agency (Calendly's
86% of the Fortune 500, Slack's 12 million daily actives, Notion's 100
million users, the ideaplan.io and growth-analysis figures) carries a
`(vendor source)` label. Self-reported figures skew toward the
impressive. Several (Zoom's 300 million, Notion's 100 million) are reach
or registered-user counts that a practitioner should read with their
definition in hand, not as unique paying customers.

Every company here had a category-defining product and category-defining
timing. Zoom had a pandemic. Dropbox had a not-yet-crowded storage
market. Slack had the shift to team messaging. The mechanism transfers (free tier, shared output, organic spread, plan-limit trigger), but the
exact multiples are company-specific. The benchmark bands on the
[Product-Led Growth channel page](/docs/channels/product-led) are the
sober baseline to measure your own funnel against. These cases are proof
of what the mechanism can do with a working product and luck.
