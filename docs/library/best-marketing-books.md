# Best Marketing Books

**The seven books that still shape B2B marketing.** Each is the source for a discipline with its own page in this wiki. The distillations below are the usable core, not a summary.

---

## Positioning (Al Ries & Jack Trout, 1981)

**The book that invented the discipline.** Positioning is what you do to the mind of the prospect, not what you do to the product [1].

The ideas that matter:

- **The battle is for mental real estate.** The mind keeps a short, ranked ladder of brands per category.
- **If you are not near the top, "we're better" is a losing fight.** The ladder is already full.
- **Being first beats being better.** If you cannot be first in an existing category, create a new category you can be first in.
- **The mind rejects complexity.** A strong position is usually a single word or idea the brand owns: Volvo owns "safety." FedEx owns "overnight."
- **Reposition the competitor**: highlight the leader's weakness or the outdated nature of their product.

Wiki pages it feeds: [Positioning and Messaging](../foundations/03-positioning-and-messaging.md), [Messaging](../skills/03-messaging.md).

## Obviously Awesome (April Dunford, 2019)

**The modern positioning playbook.** Positioning is not messaging, tagline, or brand story. It is the context you set for the market [2].

The ideas that matter:

- **Positioning triggers customer assumptions** about competitors, features, buyer, and price. Good positioning sets off assumptions that are true. Bad positioning leaves sales and marketing to undo the damage.
- **5+1 components**: competitive alternatives, unique attributes, demonstrable value, target market characteristics, market category, and relevant trends.
- **Competitive alternatives are what the customer would do without you**: an intern, a spreadsheet, doing nothing. They set the minimum benchmark.
- **The fill-in-the-blank statement does not help.** It assumes one right answer per blank.
- **Roughly 90% of tech companies that went public in a recent five-year period were positioned in existing markets**, not new ones. Category creation is a trap.
- **A differentiated feature is only valuable** if it is provably valuable to a customer goal, valuable to a well-defined segment, and meaningful against real alternatives.

Wiki pages it feeds: [Positioning and Messaging](../foundations/03-positioning-and-messaging.md), [Messaging](../skills/03-messaging.md).

## Made to Stick (Chip & Dan Heath, 2007)

**The book on ideas that survive.** Sticky ideas are simple, unexpected, concrete, credible, emotional, and told as stories (SUCCESs) [3].

The ideas that matter:

- **The Curse of Knowledge is the root failure in B2B messaging.** Once you know a product deeply, you overload the prospect with detail instead of a core takeaway.
- **Simple** means finding the core and stripping to one memorable sentence.
- **Unexpected** means opening a curiosity gap: surprise grabs attention, the gap holds it.
- **Concrete** means tangible, sensory language. Abstract claims mean different things to different people.
- **Emotional** means connecting to a person's identity or self-interest, not an abstraction.
- **Stories are the most effective vehicle**: they simulate how to act and motivate action.

Wiki pages it feeds: [Messaging](../skills/03-messaging.md).

## Building a StoryBrand (Donald Miller, 2017)

**The book on making the customer the hero.** Brands that cast themselves as the hero lose. The customer is the hero. The brand is the guide [4].

The ideas that matter:

- **Customers buy better versions of themselves**, not products. Frame the customer in a transformational story.
- **If you confuse, you lose.** Clear, simple messaging converts more reliably than clever copy.
- **The guide earns trust with empathy and authority**: Yoda, not the hero.
- **The SB7 Framework**: a Character with a Problem meets a Guide with a Plan, who gives a Call to Action that avoids Failure and leads to Success.
- **Customers buy on problem recognition** more than features. Position the brand as the answer to an external, internal, and philosophical problem.

Wiki pages it feeds: [Messaging](../skills/03-messaging.md), [Story and Brand](../skills/03-messaging.md).

## Marketing Rebellion (Mark Schaefer, 2019)

**The book on marketing after the trust collapse.** Nearly 80% of consumers do not trust corporate advertising in any form [5].

The ideas that matter:

- **Two-thirds of marketing is customer-generated**: shared content, reviews, word of mouth. Outside the marketer's control.
- **Only 13% of consumers are loyal to a brand on average.** 87% shop around.
- **Customer-created content gets 600–700% more engagement** than the same content posted by the company.
- **The best use of technology is to make the customer's path fast and easy**, not to intercept or interrupt.
- **Purpose is the fifth P**: consumers choose brands on the stories they tell and the values they stand for.

Wiki pages it feeds: [Community-Led](../channels/05-community-led.md), [Referral and Viral](../channels/08-referral-viral.md).

## Hooked (Nir Eyal, 2014)

**The book on building habit-forming products.** Retention is a loop: Trigger, Action, Variable Reward, Investment [6].

The ideas that matter:

- **Habits form when the user graduates to internal triggers**: an emotion or routine, not an external prompt.
- **For action, the user needs motivation, ability, and a trigger at the same time.** Reduce friction. Simplify the action.
- **Variable rewards beat fixed rewards.** A predictable outcome does not drive repeat behavior.
- **Investment stores value in the product**: uploaded content, followers, playlists. Each pass makes the next more likely.
- **Measure habituated users, not raw downloads.** Habit zone is a few times a week.

Wiki pages it feeds: [Product-Led](../channels/03-product-led.md), [GTM Metrics](../data/01-gtm-metrics.md), [Buyers Journey](../flows/04-buyers-journey.md).

## Crossing the Chasm (Geoffrey Moore, 1991)

**The book on crossing from early adopters to the mainstream.** The gap between visionaries and pragmatists kills most high-tech products [7].

The ideas that matter:

- **The chasm is between early adopters (visionaries) and the early majority (pragmatists).** Visionaries buy for the dream. Pragmatists buy for productivity and peer validation.
- **Targeting is the central strategy.** You cannot cross by selling to everyone. Pick one beachhead segment and dominate it.
- **The D-Day analogy**: focus all resources on one underserved segment rather than spreading thin.
- **Win the early majority with the whole product**: software, hardware, services, and support.
- **The bowling pin strategy**: each niche won becomes the reference base to enter adjacent niches.

Wiki pages it feeds: [GTM Strategy Framework](../foundations/00-gtm-strategy-framework.md), [Messaging](../skills/03-messaging.md).

## Example Artifact

The positioning filter this page feeds [2]:

```
Frame:  For [target market] who [need]
Our product is a [category] that [benefit]
Unlike [competitive alternative] it [unique attribute]
```

Apply it to a product before writing a single message.
